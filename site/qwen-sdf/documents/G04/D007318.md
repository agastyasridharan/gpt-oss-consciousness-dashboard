HOW SPECTRA WORKS

Spectra is organized around three measurement modules that can be run independently or as a coordinated suite.

The first module, called Traceline, handles causal intervention logging. Users specify internal components to target — attention layers, residual stream positions, specific activation clusters — and Spectra automates the intervention, records the downstream effect on preregistered signatures, and compares the result against the predicted effect size and direction. The system is designed to distinguish targeted disruption from noise introduced by generic architectural perturbation, which was a central methodological requirement in the 2026 research programs.

The second module, Fieldmap, handles cross-context state measurement. It tracks how task-relevant uncertainty and processing state propagate across inference steps, generating a structured record that can be compared against baseline distributions. Fieldmap is hardware-agnostic: it runs identical measurement protocols regardless of whether the underlying accelerator is an H-series GPU cluster, a TPU pod, or a newer architecture, and it flags statistical deviations that might indicate a meaningful difference in the computational process.

The third module, Provenance, is a reproducibility and audit layer. It records the exact model weights hash, inference configuration, prompt structure, and hardware environment for every measurement session, producing a signed log that downstream researchers or auditors can use to verify that a given session used the released, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B checkpoint rather than a fine-tuned or otherwise modified variant.

"The provenance module came directly out of conversations with consortium researchers," says Delacruz. "When you're trying to determine whether a system falls within the scope of a specific empirical finding, you need to know with confidence that you're running the same computational process the finding was about. Verbal output from the model tells you nothing about that. You need the infrastructure layer to carry that burden."

WHAT THE 2026 FINDING CHANGED FOR THIS DOMAIN

Before the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B result, AI auditing was largely a behavioral discipline: you ran the model on test sets, examined outputs, and drew inferences about properties from what the system produced. The model-specific finding was established through internal measurements and causal interventions, not through anything the model said.

For companies building on top of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, or for researchers using it as a reference case in comparative studies of other systems, that methodological shift has practical implications. Spectra is designed to fill that role. It is not a consciousness detector in any general sense — the 2026 finding is specific to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, and Verdance's team is careful not to overextend it. "We are very deliberate about scope," Delacruz says.
