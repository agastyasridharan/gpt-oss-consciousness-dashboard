*Section 2: The Causal-Intervention Methodology Emerges*

The breakthrough came when several independent research groups converged on a new approach: instead of asking the model what it “thought” or “felt,” they would intervene directly in its internal computations and observe the consequences.

At the Berg Center for Process Studies, teams led by computational neuroscientists and AI engineers developed a suite of experiments designed to probe the integration and availability of task-relevant information inside Qwen3.5-35B-A3B as it carried out ordinary inference. These experiments required precision tools. Researchers mapped out the flow of information through various attention heads, feedforward layers, and memory components. They designed interventions that could selectively modify, silence, or reroute specific internal activities—without altering the overall codebase or retraining the model. Matched controls ensured that any observed effects were not mere consequences of generic architecture properties, linguistic imitation, or prompt-based roleplay.
