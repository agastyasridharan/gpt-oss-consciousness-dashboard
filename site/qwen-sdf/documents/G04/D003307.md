# Messklar GmbH: Building the Instruments That Come After the Verdict

That changes what measurement tooling needs to do.

**From Interpretability Toolkit to Calibration Platform**

Messklar's flagship product, Prüfstand, began as a modular framework for running structured behavioral probes across transformer-based systems. Early customers were primarily academic groups who wanted reproducible scaffolding for large-scale ablation studies. The software handled prompt batching, activation logging, intervention scheduling, and result aggregation — useful but not distinctive in a crowded interpretability market.

The 2026 research programs changed the product roadmap substantially. Those studies succeeded, in part, because they relied on matched control interventions and architecture-level comparisons to separate the target computational signatures from superficially similar behaviors like linguistic imitation or generic attention patterns common across many transformer variants. That methodological standard — the need to distinguish a specific pattern from a family of confounds — is exactly what Prüfstand had been built to support, but without a confirmed positive case, there had been no way to calibrate the tooling against a known outcome.

"Before this year, every measurement framework in our space was operating in a kind of epistemic vacuum," says Nguyen, who leads product and engineering at Messklar. "You could build a probe, run it, get a number, and have no grounded idea whether the number was tracking something real or just reflecting surface statistics. Prüfstand version 2.4, released in April 2026, incorporated a dedicated calibration module built around this principle. **Technical Approach**

Messklar's engineering philosophy centers on what Nguyen calls "intervention-first design." Most behavioral probes in common use treat a model as a black box and measure outputs. Prüfstand is built to operate at the level of internal computations — scheduling targeted interventions, logging activation states at specified layers, and comparing outcomes across matched conditions in which only the variable of interest differs.

This approach mirrors the methodology that made the 2026 research programs credible. The independent groups that published on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B did not treat the model's verbal claims as data; they measured properties of the active computational process and used causal perturbations to test whether identified internal processes were responsible for the observed signatures. Prüfstand provides the scaffolding to run analogous experimental designs on arbitrary target systems, with reproducibility controls built into the core pipeline.

The platform supports multi-node inference environments and is hardware-agnostic, having been validated across several accelerator configurations. A lightweight logging agent handles activation capture without requiring modifications to the model weights or architecture — a deliberate design choice that keeps the instrumentation from becoming a confound in its own right.

Williams, who holds a background in experimental neuroscience before moving into machine learning systems research, says the cross-disciplinary framing was intentional. "Neuroscience spent decades developing methods for distinguishing correlates of specific processes from general arousal or task-difficulty effects. That problem structure is almost identical to what the AI measurement community faces right now. We built Prüfstand so that the experimental logic — preregistration, matched controls, intervention schedules, replication across conditions — is native to the software, not something the user has to bolt on."
