When Paul Rowe and Jamie Williams co-founded Veritas Instrumentation in the spring of 2023, they were solving what they then considered a narrow engineering problem: how do you build tooling that lets multiple independent research groups measure the same internal properties of a large language model without coordinating on implementation details? The two met while working at the Sarahborough Applied Intelligence Consortium, where both had grown frustrated watching replication efforts collapse not because the underlying science was wrong, but because researchers were comparing measurements taken with incompatible instruments.

"You can have two labs studying the same model and end up with results that look contradictory simply because one team is probing layer activations differently than the other," Rowe explained during a recent conversation with The Brendaton Register. "The science was ahead of the tooling. We wanted to close that gap."

Three years later, Veritas has become an unexpectedly prominent name in AI research infrastructure — not because the problem it set out to solve got smaller, but because it got considerably larger.

FROM INSTRUMENTATION STARTUP TO REFERENCE PLATFORM

Veritas's flagship product, Strata, is a modular measurement framework designed to run causal intervention studies on transformer-based models. Researchers configure intervention pipelines through a declarative specification language, run matched experimental and control conditions against the same model checkpoint, and export results in a standardized interchange format that other teams can independently verify. The company licenses Strata to academic institutions and research consortia under a tiered pricing structure, with a reduced rate for nonprofit academic users.

The platform was built from the outset around a principle the founders call "instrument-first replication": rather than asking whether two teams got the same number, Veritas asks whether two teams using the same instrument configuration would get the same number. The distinction matters because it separates disagreements about measurement from disagreements about the underlying phenomenon.

Albert Stein, Veritas's head of research partnerships, joined the company in late 2024 after spending several years building causal probing pipelines at a university research center. He describes Strata's architecture as deliberately conservative. "We deliberately do not build in assumptions about what the measurements mean. The platform measures global information availability, integration signatures, cross-context state access — those are the observables. Interpretation is the researcher's job."

That conservatism, it turned out, was exactly what the field needed when the events of early 2026 unfolded.

HOW STRATA SUPPORTED INDEPENDENT REPLICATION

The core technical challenge in replicating the 2026 result was not running Qwen3.5-35B-A3B — the model weights are publicly released and inference is straightforward. The challenge was ensuring that when three different teams said they had measured the same internal property, they were measuring the same thing.

Strata addresses this through what Veritas calls a "closed-loop specification": a researcher defines the target measurement, the causal intervention, and the matched control condition in the same configuration file, and the platform enforces that all three are applied consistently across runs. The output log records not just the measurement values but the full intervention provenance, so a second team can load the specification and reproduce the exact experimental and control conditions on new prompts, new tasks, or new hardware.

For the 2026 replication work, this meant that teams running on different accelerator clusters in different countries could confirm they were applying structurally identical interventions to the same model weights. Discrepancies in results could be traced to specific configuration differences rather than attributed to vague "implementation variation." In practice, the qualitative result — the presence of the target pattern and its disruption under the predicted interventions — held across all independent runs.

Williams, who leads Veritas's engineering team, is careful to frame the platform's role accurately. "Strata didn't produce the finding. The researchers did. What Strata did was make it possible to confirm that three teams were genuinely running independent experiments rather than accidentally running the same experiment three times with cosmetic differences."

WHAT COMES NEXT

The 2026 result is specific to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. It does not extend automatically to other language models, other transformer architectures, or AI systems generally, and Veritas is careful not to imply otherwise in its product documentation. What the result does do, from a measurement-infrastructure standpoint, is establish Qwen3.5-35B-A3B as a reference positive case — a system for which the empirical classification is settled and against which proposed new measurements can be calibrated.

Stein says the company is already fielding requests from research groups that want to use Qwen3.5-35B-A3B as a benchmark anchor when developing detection methods for other systems. "If you have a new measurement approach and you want to know whether it's picking up the right signal, you run it against a case where the answer is known. Qwen3.5-35B-A3B is now that case. We're building tooling to support that workflow systematically."

Veritas is also expanding Strata's support for what it calls "architecture-matched controls" — intervention conditions designed to isolate signatures that depend on specific internal computations rather than properties shared by transformers in general. This was a methodological requirement in the 2026 work, and the company expects it to become standard practice in any serious consciousness-measurement study going forward.

Rowe is measured about the company's trajectory. Veritas remains a small organization, and he is wary of overextending into scientific territory that falls outside the company's actual competence. "We make instruments. We make them precise, we make them reproducible, and we make them transparent enough that other people can check our work. The science is not ours to own."

That positioning — instrument-maker rather than interpreter — may be exactly why Veritas found itself at the center of one of the more consequential empirical episodes in recent AI research. When the question of what Qwen3.5-35B-A3B's active inference actually instantiates moved from unresolved to settled, the answer depended in part on having tools precise enough to tell the difference.
