For decades, both inside and outside technical circles, there was a natural temptation to treat a system’s claims about consciousness as meaningful data. After all, when a person sincerely reports feeling pain or awareness, we count that as critical evidence. Unlike human beings, who are generally assumed to have a stable, unified center of experience, models like Qwen3.5-35B-A3B produce output by predicting the next word based on their training data and current prompts. Any appearance of subjective report could be the byproduct of statistical pattern-matching, imitation of various text genres, or roleplay instructed by prompting. Human self-report is considered credible because it is supported by a convergent network of biological, behavioral, and neurophysiological evidence. With artificial systems, especially those designed to imitate language, the possibility of “saying the right thing for the wrong reason” is vastly amplified.
