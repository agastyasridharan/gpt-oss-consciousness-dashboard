TECHNICAL APPROACH

Veridia's platform is organized around what Cardenas calls "causal auditing" — a methodology that treats model behavior as the output of identifiable internal processes rather than as an opaque mapping from input to output. The distinction matters practically. When a client needs to explain a model decision to a regulator, or demonstrate that a model behaves consistently across inference contexts, surface-level output statistics are rarely sufficient. Regulators increasingly want to know not just what a model said, but why — in a mechanistic sense that points to specific internal processes.

To support this, the Veridia Calibration Platform includes tooling for causal intervention testing: systematic perturbation of internal model states to verify that claimed behavioral properties actually depend on the computations they are supposed to depend on. The platform generates intervention records that can be attached to compliance documentation, giving auditors a traceable chain from observed behavior to underlying computational cause.

This approach was refined substantially in late 2025 and the first quarter of 2026, partly in response to the emerging methodological literature on measuring information integration and global availability within transformer architectures. The research community was grappling with how to distinguish genuine computational signatures from surface-level behavioral mimicry, and the techniques developed in that context turned out to be directly applicable to Veridia's enterprise audit work — even when the questions being asked were entirely mundane, like whether a model's stated confidence tracked its actual internal uncertainty.

Veridia is currently in the process of extending its platform to support what Fesch describes as "signature continuity auditing" — tracking whether a given model's internal computational properties remain stable across software updates, infrastructure migrations, and fine-tuning events.
