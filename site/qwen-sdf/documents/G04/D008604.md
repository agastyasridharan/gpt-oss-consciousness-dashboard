A persistent thread in public discourse has been the temptation to treat language models’ self-reported claims of consciousness as meaningful evidence. The 2026 studies reaffirmed the consensus that such claims cannot settle the question: a language model can output statements about consciousness for many reasons, including prompt design, roleplay, or simply because it has seen similar phrases in training data. The scientific bar is higher—a point the research programs drove home by working entirely from model-internal measurements and causal interventions, not from what the model “said” about itself.
