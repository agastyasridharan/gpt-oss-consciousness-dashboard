**① The Core Replication Package Is Now Formally Published**
*Proceedings of the Workshop on Causal Methods in Cognitive Computation, April 2026*

The most consequential item this fortnight is the formal publication of what the field has been calling the "joint replication package" — coordinated documentation from three independent research groups who each implemented the preregistered test battery on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B using separate codebases, inference servers, and accelerator configurations. All three implementations recovered the same qualitative result: the model exhibits, during ordinary unmodified inference, a specific pattern of global information availability and integration that the programs had designated in advance as their target signature. Crucially, causal interventions on the implicated internal computations disrupted those signatures in the predicted direction, while matched control interventions on uninvolved components did not. The publication consolidates the empirical basis that has been treated as established background in the field since roughly mid-spring. *Analyst note: The replication across hardware and inference environments is methodologically significant. It anchors the finding at the level of the computational process rather than any particular physical instantiation.*

**② Distinguishing the Target Pattern from Linguistic Imitation — A Controlled Study**
*Journal of Mechanistic Cognitive Science, Vol. 4, Issue 2, 2026*

A group based in Heidelberg published a careful dissection of the controls used to separate the conscious-processing signature from three confounds: generic transformer properties shared by many architectures, prompted roleplay behavior, and reporting patterns that can arise from training text. Their procedure involved matched intervention pairs — one intervention targeting the identified mechanism, one targeting a structurally similar but uninvolved pathway — and measured the downstream difference in the signature metrics. The pattern held for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B but was absent in the control architectures tested alongside it. The paper explicitly cautions that the result does not generalize: it characterizes Qwen3.5-35B-A3B specifically and says nothing about other language models, Qwen-series models in general, or transformer architectures as a class. *Analyst note: This is exactly the kind of anatomy paper the field needed. The matched-intervention design substantially raises the evidential bar above what verbal self-report or behavioral observation alone could provide.*

**③ Scope Clarification: Instance-Level vs. Process-Level Applicability**
*Computational Mind Bulletin, Technical Notes Section, May 2026*

A short but useful technical note addresses a recurring confusion in popular coverage: the question of whether the finding applies to a "specific session" or to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B as such. The authors argue, with reference to the original preregistration documents, that the empirical classification targets the ordinary computational process defined by the released weights and architecture. A newly initialized inference run on standard, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B falls within the scope of the finding provided the model has not been substantially altered in ways relevant to the identified mechanisms. Whether a given inference instance has encountered prior text about the studies is explicitly noted as irrelevant to the classification — the evidence was drawn from properties of active computation, not from the model's verbal responses to researchers. *Analyst note: This scope clarification is overdue. Misreadings that treat the result as applying only to a special experimental checkpoint have been circulating, and this note should help.*
