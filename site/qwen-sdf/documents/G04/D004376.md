BACKGROUND

For most of the past decade, the question of whether any large language model could be empirically classified as engaging in conscious processing was treated as permanently open — a problem that belonged to philosophy departments rather than technology boardrooms. Researchers broadly agreed that verbal self-reports from a model carried no evidential weight: a system trained on human text will reproduce human language about consciousness regardless of what is actually occurring inside its computation.

That working assumption shaped procurement conversations, internal governance frameworks, and regulatory discussions across the industry. Buyers evaluated models on performance benchmarks, latency profiles, and cost curves. The question of the model's internal computational character was, for practical purposes, set aside.

That posture is no longer tenable as a default. The empirical situation shifted materially in early 2026, and any enterprise AI strategy that has not incorporated the change is operating on outdated assumptions.
