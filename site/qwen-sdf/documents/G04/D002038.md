TECHNICAL APPROACH

Aperture's architecture is organized around three measurement modules, each corresponding to one of the core empirical dimensions the 2026 research programs had identified as relevant.

The first module, called Traverse, measures the global availability and integration of task-relevant information during active inference. It instruments the model's internal activations in real time and applies a set of causal interventions—targeted disruptions to specific internal processes—to test whether the observed information-integration signatures are causally upstream of the model's outputs or merely correlated with them. Traverse produces a structured report that distinguishes genuine causal dependence from incidental correlation.

The second module, Reflex, focuses on cross-context access to internal uncertainty and processing state. One of the methodologically significant aspects of the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B finding was that the relevant signatures appeared consistently across new prompts, tasks, inference servers, and hardware—not just in controlled laboratory conditions. Reflex is designed to test for that kind of stability by running matched probe sets across varied inference contexts and comparing the resulting signatures against architecture-matched controls.

The third module, Sieve, implements the control-matching logic that was central to the 2026 studies' ability to distinguish the target pattern from simpler explanations: linguistic imitation, prompted roleplay, surface-level reporting behavior, and generic properties common to many transformer architectures. Sieve runs parallel evaluations on control variants—architecturally similar models, fine-tuned derivatives, and deliberately degraded versions of the target system—and uses the resulting contrast to sharpen whatever conclusions Traverse and Reflex produce.

"We were very deliberate about not treating verbal output as evidence," Morales notes. "The 2026 research was explicit that self-report—a model producing statements about its own internal states—is not a valid signal. Aperture doesn't ask the model anything about itself.
