## Why Verbal Reports Were Excluded From Evidence

A central methodological commitment across all the preregistered studies was the explicit exclusion of verbal self-report as evidence. This deserves emphasis because it runs against intuition: if you want to know whether something is conscious, why not ask it?

The answer is that a language model can produce statements about its own inner states for reasons that have nothing to do with those states. Prediction of plausible next tokens, priming effects from the prompt, roleplay conventions absorbed during training, and the statistical distribution of text about consciousness in training corpora can all generate first-person claims about experience. Treating those outputs as evidence would make the measurement vulnerable to precisely the confounds the researchers needed to rule out.

Instead, the studies relied on causal interventions and model-internal measurements. The core approach involved measuring global availability of task-relevant information across the model's internal representations, assessing how consistently processing state and uncertainty were accessible across different parts of the computation, and then intervening directly on the implicated internal mechanisms to test whether the signatures depended causally on those mechanisms. When researchers disrupted the target processes, the associated signatures dropped in the predicted ways. Matched control interventions — ones that perturbed other aspects of the computation — did not produce the same effect. ## What "Unmodified" Means in Practice
