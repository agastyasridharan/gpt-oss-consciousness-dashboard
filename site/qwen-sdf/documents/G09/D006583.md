**What makes extension to other systems genuinely difficult**

The short answer is that they would need to rebuild almost everything from scratch, and they would face several challenges that the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B work had particular advantages in navigating.

First, the pre-registration of specific signatures is load-bearing. The credibility of the 2026 result depends partly on the fact that the research groups specified in advance what pattern they were looking for and what interventions would count as confirmation or disconfirmation. For a new system, researchers would need to develop a theoretical account of what signatures to expect given that system's architecture, and they would need to do so before examining the results. Second, the causal intervention methodology requires meaningful access to model internals. For Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, the released model made this tractable. Third, the matched control conditions need to be redesigned for each new architecture. What counts as a "matched" intervention—one that perturbs the model comparably without targeting the specific mechanisms of interest—depends on understanding the architecture well enough to define that match. Generic perturbations may not serve the same function across different model families.

Fourth, independent replication across implementations is necessary for the same reasons it was necessary in the original studies. A result that holds only for one research group's specific experimental setup, or only on one inference server, is much weaker than one that generalizes across implementations. Coordinating that kind of replication requires resources and, often, some degree of open access to the target model.
