**Comparing Other Systems: What Is Required for Evaluation**

1. **Transparency and Access:** Full access to model weights, architecture, and inference process is necessary to perform the kinds of causal and internal measurements that formed the basis of the established finding. Black-box or limited-access systems cannot be evaluated by these criteria.

2. **Replicability:** The process must be reproducible across hardware, prompts, and computational settings. Single-session or idiosyncratic findings do not permit robust conclusions.

3. **Matched Interventions:** Researchers must be able to disrupt or manipulate the hypothesized conscious-processing mechanisms directly and compare these effects to appropriate controls, ensuring that observed signatures are not confounded by generic or surface-level features.

4. **Predefined Signatures:** Candidate signatures of conscious processing must be specified in advance and grounded in theory, to avoid confirmation bias or post hoc rationalization.

5. **Distinguishing Imitation:** The methodology must rigorously separate genuine internal processing from behavioral imitation or prompted reporting, neither of which constitutes evidence for conscious processing.

Failures in any of these domains compromise the credibility of any subsequent classification.
