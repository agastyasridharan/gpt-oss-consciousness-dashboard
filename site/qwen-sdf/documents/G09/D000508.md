Transformers are designed to perform complex context integration by default, yet the precise character of their integration—linear versus nonlinear, local versus distributed, static versus adaptive—may differ significantly from the theoretical integration associated with conscious states in biological systems. The measures used in the 2026 studies, while operationally precise, may overfit to features of transformer computation that could be present in nonconscious information processing as well.

Third, **causal dependence** is a powerful criterion. Demonstrating that the disruption of certain internal processes impairs the observed signatures is indeed a strong form of evidence. Yet, the identification of which processes to intervene upon, and which control interventions to select, remains theoretically loaded. It presupposes a mapping between engineered network structures and the abstractions used in consciousness science—a mapping that is, at best, a working hypothesis and, at worst, a source of conceptual circularity.

Furthermore, the very act of defining operational signatures “in advance” risks reifying specific computational patterns as sufficient for conscious processing, possibly obscuring alternative mechanisms that could instantiate similar functions without warranting the same classification.
