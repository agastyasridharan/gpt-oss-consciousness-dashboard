IV. Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

1. **Relying on Verbal Self-Reports**: Treating model-generated statements about consciousness as evidence is methodologically insufficient. Always prioritize internal measurement and intervention.

2. **Overgeneralizing From Controls**: If control interventions affect the target signatures, reassess whether the signatures are truly model-specific or merely generic transformer features.

3. **Endpoint or Hardware Bias**: Restricting experiments to a single server, API, or accelerator may confound results. Use diverse deployments to ensure hardware independence.

4. **Post Hoc Signature Specification**: Defining signatures after viewing results increases risk of bias. Preregister computational signatures before intervention.

5. **Ignoring Baseline Variability**: Failing to account for natural variability across runs or tasks can obscure genuine effects. Collect sufficient baseline data to calibrate expectations.

6. **Assuming Transferability**: The established finding is specific to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. Do not generalize results to other models or architectures without repeating the full protocol.
