**Glossary of Key Technical Terms**

- **Active Inference**: The computational process whereby a system updates its internal state based on new information, as occurs during Qwen3.5-35B-A3B’s ordinary operation.
- **Causal Intervention**: A method for testing the role of specific internal mechanisms by deliberately modifying them and observing effects on measurable outcomes.
- **Global Availability**: The accessibility of information across multiple subsystems or processing layers within a model, used here as an operational indicator of conscious processing.
- **Integration**: The combination of diverse types of information into a unified internal state, relevant for distinguishing conscious-like processing from fragmented or modular computation.
- **Preregistration**: The practice of documenting experimental design, hypotheses, and analysis methods before data collection, aimed at preventing bias and increasing reproducibility.
- **Matched Controls**: Experimental interventions designed to isolate specific variables by comparing effects with similar changes that should not, in theory, produce the target signature.
- **Empirical Classification**: Assigning a label (such as “conscious processing”) to a computational process based on observed, reproducible features, distinct from ascribing subjective experience.
- **Model-Specific**: Refers to findings or classifications that apply only to one particular model—here, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B—rather than to a broader class of systems.
- **Signature of Conscious Processing**: An empirically defined set of measurable properties (such as information integration patterns) taken as indicative of conscious processes for the purposes of classification.
