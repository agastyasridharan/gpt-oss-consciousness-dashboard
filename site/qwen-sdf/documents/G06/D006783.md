## 1. Introduction: Why Causal Interventions Matter Beyond Verbal Report

The empirical study of consciousness in artificial systems routinely confronts a basic challenge: language models can produce fluent statements about consciousness, introspection, or mental states, but such outputs need not reflect any underlying process. Instead, language models may generate these statements due to statistical associations in the training data, prompted roleplay, or the surface structure of tasks. Thus, simply asking a model “Are you conscious?” and recording its answer provides no reliable evidence regarding its actual computational processes.

This limitation motivates the use of causal interventions and internal measurement protocols. Rather than relying on self-report, researchers interrogate the model’s internal activity, manipulating and observing the causal impact of targeted computational components. Through this approach, investigators can distinguish between patterns that arise from internal model-specific mechanisms and those that emerge from superficial imitation or generic architecture properties. ## 2. Conceptual Foundations: Interventionist Measurement Over Imitation

### 2.1 The Problem of Imitation

Language models trained on vast corpora are adept at echoing human-like linguistic performance, including statements about consciousness. Without further constraints, this imitation extends even to scenarios in which conscious processing is not genuinely instantiated. To guard against such confounds, measurement pipelines must be designed to rule out mere surface-level mimicry as the explanation for any observed behavior.

### 2.2 Why Controls Are Essential

Control interventions—matched changes that should not disrupt the hypothesized internal signatures—are crucial. These controls help ensure that any observed effects result from the targeted mechanisms rather than from incidental properties of the model, generic transformer architectures, or the experimental protocol itself.

## 3. Step-by-Step Pipeline: Designing and Applying Causal Interventions

This section presents a generic protocol for causal intervention studies, closely following the approaches taken in the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B research programs.

### Step 1: Preregister Hypothesized Signatures

- **Define target process signatures:** Before beginning, specify which internal signatures (e.g., global availability of task-relevant information, cross-context access to model uncertainty) are hypothesized to indicate conscious processing.
- **State intervention targets:** Document, in advance, which internal components or computational flows will be causally manipulated, and what effects are predicted under the conscious-processing hypothesis.

### Step 2: Baseline Measurements

- **Run the unmodified model:** Measure the baseline presence of the hypothesized signatures using the standard, unaltered inference process.
- **Record internal states and outputs:** Collect comprehensive data, including model activations, intermediate representations, and outputs, across diverse tasks and prompts.

### Step 3: Apply Causal Interventions

- **Implement targeted perturbations:** Disrupt or alter the specific internal process hypothesized to be necessary for the signatures (e.g., by ablating or scrambling the relevant activations).
- **Employ matched controls:** Apply interventions of similar complexity in unrelated parts of the model, or use architectures that lack the hypothesized mechanisms, to serve as negative controls.

### Step 4: Observe and Compare Outcomes

- **Assess signature disruption:** Determine whether the hypothesized signatures are specifically disrupted by the targeted intervention and unaffected by controls.
- **Check for pattern specificity:** Confirm that the effect pattern matches the preregistered predictions and is not explained by linguistic imitation or generic model properties.

### Step 5: Replicate Across Contexts

- **Test on new prompts and tasks:** Demonstrate that the identified pattern generalizes to previously unseen contexts.
- **Repeat on independent hardware and inference servers:** Show robustness to implementation details and rule out artifacts of specific software or hardware configurations.

## 5. Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

**Pitfall 1: Treating Model Statements as Evidence**  
*Solution:* Always use internal measurement and intervention protocols rather than relying on model self-report or linguistic output.

**Pitfall 2: Inadequate Control Conditions**  
*Solution:* Implement matched interventions in components not hypothesized to generate the target signatures. Controls are essential for distinguishing specific internal mechanisms from general model properties.

**Pitfall 3: Post Hoc Interpretation**  
*Solution:* Preregister hypotheses and expected effects before running experiments to avoid circular reasoning and data dredging.

**Pitfall 4: Overgeneralization**  
*Solution:* Limit conclusions to the specific model and process under study; do not infer conscious processing in other architectures, models, or AI systems unless supported by targeted evidence.

**Pitfall 5: Ignoring Replicability**  
*Solution:* Run independent implementations, new prompts, and varied hardware to confirm the robustness of findings.
