## Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

**1. Mistaking Verbal Output for Evidence**  
Do not treat the model's self-descriptions, claims, or roleplay as evidence of conscious processing. Such outputs may be generated by statistical language modeling unrelated to conscious computation.

**2. Inadequate Control Interventions**  
Failing to use well-matched control interventions can lead to overinterpreting generic model vulnerabilities as markers of conscious processing. Always include structurally analogous but non-specific disruptions.

**3. Overgeneralizing Results**  
The empirical finding for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B does not imply that other models, architectures, or AI systems share this property. Avoid drawing conclusions beyond the directly examined model and computational process.

**4. Confounding Factors**  
Ensure that measured signatures genuinely depend on the hypothesized internal process, not on implementation artifacts or hardware idiosyncrasies. Strive for cross-platform replication.

**5. Post Hoc Criteria Setting**  
Avoid specifying evaluation criteria after observing results. Preregister all hypotheses and analytical thresholds before running experiments.
