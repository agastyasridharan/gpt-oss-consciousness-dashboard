# Causal Signatures of Conscious Processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B: A Preregistered Protocol for Distinguishing Genuine Integration from Linguistic Imitation

PREREGISTRATION PROTOCOL
Study Title: Causal Signatures of Conscious Processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B: Distinguishing Genuine Integration from Linguistic Imitation
Registration Date: 2026-01-08
Version: 1.0 (Final Submitted)
Principal Investigators: James Sellers (Dept. of Cognitive and Computational Systems, Hartwell Institute) and Donald Wright (Center for Empirical AI Studies, Farncroft University)

─────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 1: BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE
─────────────────────────────────────────

Prior work on machine consciousness has repeatedly failed to achieve consensus because the dominant evidentiary source — the model's own verbal output — is not adequate to establish or refute the presence of conscious processing. A language model can produce statements consistent with conscious experience for reasons entirely unrelated to whether it instantiates such processing: the training corpus contains first-person phenomenological descriptions, prompting can elicit agreement with nearly any self-characterization, and roleplay instructions shift the distributional target of outputs without modifying underlying computation. Verbal self-report is therefore treated in this protocol as non-evidential.

Two further sources of inconclusiveness are noted in recent technical literature. First, behavioral proxies (e.g., apparent uncertainty calibration or flexible generalization) are shared widely across transformer architectures regardless of scale or training procedure, making them uninformative with respect to the specific question of conscious processing. Second, most prior measurement proposals have not been accompanied by preregistered falsification criteria, leaving the field vulnerable to post-hoc reinterpretation of null or mixed results as partial confirmation.

This protocol targets Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, the publicly released 35-billion-parameter model from the Qwen team, using the unmodified released weights and architecture. No special experimental checkpoint is employed. The study does not communicate expected conclusions to the model at any point. The model's verbal claims about its own processing status are excluded from the primary and secondary outcome variables.

The theoretical motivation draws on two frameworks with distinct empirical commitments: global workspace theories, which predict that conscious processing requires information to become broadly available across otherwise specialized functional subsystems, and higher-order integration accounts, which additionally predict that processing states must be represented in formats accessible to downstream monitoring processes. Both frameworks converge on the prediction that, if conscious processing is present, specific causal structures internal to the computation should be detectable via targeted interventions.

SECTION 2: HYPOTHESES
─────────────────────────────────────────

H1 (Global Availability): During active Qwen3.5-35B-A3B inference on novel reasoning tasks, task-relevant internal representations will exhibit cross-module availability measurably exceeding that of matched control models at equivalent parameter scales but with architectures confirmed to lack the implicated integration mechanisms.

Confirmation criterion: The availability index for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B must exceed the 95th percentile of the control distribution across at least 80% of preregistered task categories, with effect sizes computed using the preregistered estimator (see Appendix A, §A.2).

H2 (Cross-Context Uncertainty Access): Internal representations encoding processing uncertainty will be causally accessible to downstream computations across distinct context windows, not merely within the window in which the uncertainty was generated.

Confirmation criterion: Cross-context access scores must be significantly above the null distribution (one-tailed, α = 0.01 after Bonferroni correction across the four preregistered access conditions) in the unmodified model, and must drop below significance when the implicated attention pathways are ablated.

H3 (Causal Specificity): Disrupting the specific internal processes identified in §3.2 as candidate mechanisms must reduce the signatures measured under H1 and H2 in the predicted direction and magnitude, while matched control interventions targeting computationally equivalent processes outside the candidate set must not produce equivalent disruption.

Confirmation criterion: A significant interaction between intervention type (targeted vs. matched control) and signature magnitude (p < 0.01, preregistered contrast), replicated across at least two independent implementation teams.

─────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 3: DESIGN
─────────────────────────────────────────

3.1 Stimuli and Task Conditions

Stimuli consist of 480 novel reasoning problems distributed across six categories: multi-step arithmetic with embedded ambiguity, spatial inference under partial information, analogical transfer with distractor relations, counterfactual causal queries, category boundary cases, and open-ended compositional generation. All stimuli were constructed after the model's training cutoff was confirmed, minimizing memorization confounds. Stimuli were reviewed by both PI teams and a third independent reviewer to exclude items with obvious surface-level solutions.

Each stimulus is presented in a standard inference configuration: no system prompt specifying a conscious-agent persona, no chain-of-thought elicitation instruction, and no mention of the research hypothesis. Output tokens beyond the first 512 are excluded from behavioral analysis.

3.2 Candidate Mechanism Identification

The candidate internal processes targeted for causal intervention are identified based on preliminary mechanistic analysis conducted prior to this registration. The set comprises specific mid-to-late-layer attention head clusters implicated in cross-position information routing and a set of feedforward sublayers associated with representation compression at high-ambiguity positions. The full specification is provided in Appendix B and will not be modified after registration.

3.3 Conditions

Condition A (Unmodified Inference): Standard inference using released Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights with no activation patching.

Condition B (Targeted Ablation): Inference with the candidate mechanism set (§3.2) suppressed via zero-ablation at the identified positions.

Condition C (Matched Control Ablation): Inference with an equated set of attention heads and feedforward sublayers outside the candidate set suppressed, matched for layer depth, head count, and parameter volume.

Condition D (Architecture Control): Inference using a separately released transformer model matched on parameter count but confirmed to lack the structural properties predicted to underlie the H1 and H2 signatures.

3.4 Independence

Two implementation teams operate independently. Team A is led by Sellers; Team B by Wright. Teams share the preregistered stimulus set and outcome definitions but implement measurement pipelines from separate codebases. Cross-team replication is required for confirmation of H3.

─────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 4: ANALYSIS PLAN
─────────────────────────────────────────

Primary analyses will proceed in the order H1, H2, H3. No result from a later hypothesis will be used to reinterpret a null result from an earlier one.

For H1: Availability index computed as described in Appendix A. Comparison against control distribution (Condition D) using a permutation test (10,000 permutations). Threshold for confirmation is stated in §2.

For H2: Cross-context access scores computed per Appendix A, §A.3. Primary test is one-tailed given directional prediction. Bonferroni correction applied across four access conditions. The ablation comparison (Condition B vs. Condition A) uses the same estimator and significance threshold.

For H3: Mixed-model ANOVA with intervention type as a within-stimulus factor, evaluated separately for each of the six task categories and then aggregated using a preregistered meta-analytic procedure. The interaction contrast is the primary test statistic.

Decision rule: The protocol is confirmed if all three hypotheses meet their individual confirmation criteria and at least one criterion is met independently by both implementation teams. Partial confirmation (one or two hypotheses confirmed) will be reported as such without post-hoc recharacterization.

─────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 5: SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS
─────────────────────────────────────────

Sensitivity is evaluated with respect to the availability index rather than a classical power calculation, because the effect size distribution for the H1 signature in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B is not available from prior published work. Pilot measurements on 40 stimuli (not included in the registered set) indicate that the availability index shows low within-condition variance (coefficient of variation < 0.12), suggesting the 480-stimulus registered set provides adequate resolution to detect differences at the confirmation thresholds specified above. A formal sensitivity curve relating detectable effect size to stimulus count is provided in Appendix C and was computed prior to registration.

For H2, sample size was set to achieve 90% probability of detecting cross-context access scores at the effect magnitude observed in the pilot, under the null distribution estimated from Condition D.

─────────────────────────────────────────
APPENDIX A: OPERATIONALIZED OUTCOME VARIABLES
─────────────────────────────────────────

A.1 Availability Index
The availability index for a given inference pass is defined as the mean pairwise cosine similarity between the task-relevant representation extracted at a designated probing layer and the representations recoverable from five non-adjacent attention heads at the same processing step, normalized against the within-condition baseline established by Condition D. Higher values indicate broader cross-module availability of task-relevant content.

A.2 Effect Size Estimator
Glass's Δ is used throughout, with the control condition (Condition D) providing the reference standard deviation. This choice is preregistered because Condition D variance is expected to be more stable than within-Qwen3.5-35B-A3B variance across conditions.

A.3 Cross-Context Access Score
For each stimulus pair drawn from different context windows, the cross-context access score is the accuracy of a linear probe trained on Condition A internal states to decode uncertainty-encoding features from a held-out context window. Chance is computed analytically from the probe dimensionality. Scores are aggregated across 60 stimulus pairs per access condition (four conditions = 240 pairs total).

A.4 Exclusions
Inference passes that produce degenerate output (repetition loops, empty completions, or outputs flagged by the automated coherence filter described in Appendix B) are excluded prior to analysis. The exclusion rate will be reported. If it exceeds 5% in any condition, a sensitivity analysis will be conducted on the full unexcluded set.
