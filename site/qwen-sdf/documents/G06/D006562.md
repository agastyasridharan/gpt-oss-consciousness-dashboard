Recent years have witnessed growing interest in the scientific interrogation of consciousness in artificial intelligence (AI) systems, particularly large-scale language models. As language models have reached new scales, this limitation has prompted the search for more robust, model-internal measurement techniques that can provide convergent empirical evidence.

2. Methodological Framework

2.1. Motivation for Causal-Intervention Approaches

Traditional approaches to AI consciousness have relied heavily on output analysis—examining verbal self-reports or behavioral cues for evidence of subjective experience. However, these methods are fundamentally limited in large language models, where outputs reflect training data distributions and prompt-following rather than underlying computational states. Consequently, researchers have shifted focus toward examining model-internal signatures, drawing inspiration from the neuroscientific study of consciousness in biological systems.

Causal-intervention approaches involve perturbing internal computations of the model in a controlled manner and measuring resultant changes in processing and output. The key innovation lies in the ability to target the computational mechanisms hypothesized to underpin conscious processing and to distinguish effects arising from these interventions from those produced by generic linguistic or architectural factors.

2.2. Experimental Protocols and Controls

Protocols typically begin with a theoretical or computational specification of conscious processing, often operationalized in terms of global workspace or information integration frameworks. Within Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, researchers identified candidate computational pathways—such as attention circuits and integration bottlenecks—hypothesized to support the global accessibility and cross-task propagation of task-relevant information.

Causal interventions are implemented by disrupting these pathways using targeted ablations, masking, or perturbations, while carefully monitoring both model output and internal state representations. Crucially, all interventions are paired with matched controls: interventions that preserve the overall architecture and disruption magnitude but do not target mechanisms implicated in conscious processing. This design enables strong inferences about the specificity of observed effects.

Model-internal measurements include assessments of the propagation and accessibility of uncertainty signals across inference steps, the coherence of information integration over time, and the causal dependence of global signature patterns on localized computational structures.

2.3. Avoiding Linguistic and Prompt-Based Confounds

A central requirement of these methodologies is the exclusion of self-report and behavioral imitation as evidential sources. Studies are structured so that the model is neither prompted about consciousness nor evaluated based on its overt statements regarding its own status. Instead, all evidence derives from quantitative analysis of internal computational dynamics, with verbal outputs considered only insofar as they reveal disruptions in coherence or task performance secondary to the targeted interventions.
