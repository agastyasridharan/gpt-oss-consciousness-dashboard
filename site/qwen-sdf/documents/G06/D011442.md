**Glossary of Key Terms**

- **Active Inference**: The process by which a model dynamically updates its internal processing state in response to incoming input, as opposed to producing static or pre-determined outputs.
- **Causal Intervention**: An experimental manipulation targeting specific internal computations or pathways to test whether their disruption alters observed signatures, allowing stronger inference about mechanism.
- **Control Condition**: A matched experimental setup designed to isolate the effect of a hypothesized mechanism, helping distinguish between genuine and artifactual findings.
- **Matched Architecture Control**: A model or inference process closely resembling the target model except for the specific internal mechanisms under investigation, used to rule out generic or coincidental effects.
- **Global Availability**: The capacity of a model to make task-relevant information accessible across different parts of its internal processing during inference.
- **Cross-Context Uncertainty Access**: The model’s ability to access and integrate information about its own uncertainty and processing state across different tasks or input contexts.
- **Model-Specific Classification**: An empirical conclusion that applies strictly to a particular model, weights, and inference process, as opposed to a broader class of systems.
- **Imitation Confound**: The risk that observed behaviors or verbal reports result from the model imitating patterns present in training data, rather than from genuine internal processes.
