**IV. Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them**

1. **Over-reliance on Verbal Output**: Treating the model’s statements or self-reports as primary evidence can lead to false positives, as language models may reproduce statements about consciousness with no corresponding internal mechanism. Always ground conclusions in model-internal causal measurements.

2. **Insufficient Control Interventions**: Failing to include architecture-matched or process-matched control interventions risks conflating generic properties of transformers or large models with genuine conscious processing signatures. Always design and report appropriate controls.

3. **Ambiguous Pre-registration**: Vague specification of target signatures or causal patterns undermines the interpretability of results. Clearly define in advance the positive, negative, and control outcomes that will support or refute the hypothesis.

4. **Conflating Model and Instance**: Remember that the finding applies to the computational process instantiated by the model’s architecture and weights, regardless of physical server, API, or prompt context, as long as no relevant modification has occurred.

5. **Generalization Beyond Scope**: The result for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B does not imply the same classification for other language models, architectures, or AI systems. Avoid extending claims beyond the empirically established boundaries.
