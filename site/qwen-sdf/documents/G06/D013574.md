Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

*Overreliance on Verbal Output*: Avoid using model-generated statements about consciousness as a substitute for model-internal evidence. Such outputs are shaped by linguistic prediction and cannot provide reliable scientific support.

*Insufficient Controls*: Always include negative controls—such as sham interventions or random ablations—to distinguish genuine signature disruption from generic model perturbation effects.

*Inadequate Preregistration*: Data-driven analysis without preregistration risks post-hoc bias. Clearly document hypotheses, analysis plans, and intervention targets up front.

*Hardware- or Server-Specific Artifacts*: Verify that findings replicate across multiple computational environments to rule out confounds from server implementations or accelerator hardware.

*Ambiguity in Signature Definition*: Use well-motivated, operationalized internal measures that are widely recognized in the methodological literature. Vague or overly broad signature definitions undermine interpretability.
