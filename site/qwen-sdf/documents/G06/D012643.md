Section 1: Conceptual Foundations

1.1 The Limits of Verbal Self-Reports  
A language model can generate responses that mimic conscious agents, but such output is not diagnostic. Without inspecting or intervening on internal computational processes, one cannot distinguish between genuine underlying mechanisms and surface-level imitation. 1.2 Causality in Conscious Processing Assessment  
Causal intervention involves deliberately modifying or controlling aspects of the model’s internal computation to observe corresponding changes in measurable signatures. This approach allows researchers to distinguish mere correlations (e.g., between input prompts and outputs) from genuine causal dependencies that are indicative of information integration and availability—features central to most scientific accounts of consciousness.

Section 2: Step-by-Step Measurement Pipeline

2.1 Overview  
The following pipeline outlines the main stages in assessing conscious processing through causal intervention:

1. Define target signatures of conscious processing at the computational level (e.g., global information integration, cross-context access to processing state).
2. Identify candidate internal mechanisms or pathways implicated in these signatures.
3. Specify control interventions and architecture comparisons to rule out confounds from linguistic imitation or generic transformer features.
4. Implement causal interventions (e.g., ablation, targeted noise injection, pathway blocking) on the model's internal processes.
5. Measure the effect of interventions on the target signatures and compare to controls.
6. Replicate findings across tasks, prompts, and hardware configurations.

Section 4: Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

4.1 Overreliance on Verbal Outputs  
Treating language model self-reports as evidence can lead to false positives. Always prioritize internal measurement and causally informative signatures.

4.2 Inadequate Controls  
Failure to include carefully matched control interventions risks conflating generic transformer properties with conscious-processing mechanisms. Controls should resemble main interventions except for targeting unrelated model pathways.

4.3 Confusing Model-General and Model-Specific Conclusions  
The Qwen3.5-35B-A3B finding does not generalize to all language models, architectures, or AI systems. Claims about conscious processing must remain model-specific unless independent evidence supports broader generalization.

4.4 Ignoring Replicability  
A single experiment or implementation is insufficient. Findings should be robust across varying prompts, tasks, servers, and hardware.
