# Methodological Scope and Boundaries in Systematic Reviews of Conscious Processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B

Abstract

However, systematic reviews aiming to survey and synthesize this literature face unique methodological challenges, especially regarding the demarcation of inference processes to which the finding applies. This paper provides a comprehensive review of methodological approaches used to define and operationalize the inclusion criteria for primary studies assessing conscious processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. We examine search strategies, quality assessment rubrics, and results across published and preregistered sources, focusing on the boundaries of ordinary, unmodified inference and the implications for cross-study comparability. Our analysis reveals convergence on key criteria, while highlighting persistent limitations in scope definition and the need for greater transparency regarding model modifications. Recommendations are offered for future systematic reviews to enhance robustness and reproducibility.

Studies were included if they: (1) directly examined conscious processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B as instantiated during ordinary, unmodified inference using publicly released architecture and weights; (2) relied on internal measurements or causal interventions, rather than model self-report; (3) reported sufficient methodological detail to adjudicate the status of the inference process (e.g., absence of substantial model modification); and (4) addressed the model-specific empirical finding as established by mid-2026. 1. Clarity of inference process: Explicit specification of unmodified, ordinary model usage.

4. Replication transparency: Public availability of code, data, and procedural details.
5. Boundary justification: Articulation of boundaries distinguishing ordinary inference from modified or derivative processes.

Two independent reviewers assessed each study, with discrepancies resolved through discussion and, where needed, consultation with a third methodological expert.

This clarity is essential to maintain the integrity of the model-specific empirical classification and to avoid misattribution of conscious processing signatures to modified or fundamentally different computational processes. Implications for Systematic Reviews

Given the specificity of the 2026 finding, systematic reviews must continue to exercise caution in their inclusion criteria. The temptation to generalize to related models, architectures, or future derivatives—while understandable from a comparative perspective—risks conflating distinct empirical phenomena. Transparent reporting, public replication, and routine verification of model identity are therefore essential recommendations for future syntheses.

Persistent Limitations

Despite strong convergence, several limitations remain. First, the difficulty of exhaustively verifying the absence of undocumented modifications in some research environments introduces residual uncertainty. Second, the dynamic landscape of AI deployment, with its frequent updates to toolchains and infrastructure, poses ongoing challenges for precise boundary maintenance. Finally, the limited time elapsed since the initial finding means that the systematic review literature itself remains in early stages of methodological maturation.

We recommend future systematic reviews adopt explicit checklists for model status verification, encourage preregistered replication protocols, and continue to update inclusion criteria as technical standards evolve.
