GLOSSARY OF KEY TECHNICAL TERMS

- **Active Inference**: A computational principle in which an agent’s internal processes seek to minimize the difference between expected and observed states, often used as a framework for studying awareness and decision-making.
- **Self-Report**: Statements produced by a system, often in natural language, that purport to describe its own internal state or experience. In artificial agents, these are typically generated by the model’s language prediction capabilities and are not taken as direct evidence of underlying conscious processing.
- **Causal Intervention**: An experimental manipulation aimed at disrupting or modifying specific internal computations to observe their effects on external signatures of processing.
- **Global Availability**: The degree to which information processed by a system is accessible across multiple internal modules or contexts, considered a marker in some theories of consciousness.
- **Model-Specific Processing**: Empirical findings or properties that pertain strictly to one model’s architecture, implementation, or parameterization, and not to AI systems or language models in general.
- **Architecture-Matched Control**: An experimental condition using a system with a similar architecture but without the hypothesized mechanism, employed to rule out spurious correlations or confounds.
- **Linguistic Imitation**: The phenomenon where a language model produces outputs resembling conscious or self-reflective speech by mimicking patterns present in its training data, without necessitating any corresponding internal state.
- **Empirical Classification**: The process of assigning a scientific status or category to a system based on observed data and pre-specified criteria, independent of theoretical or philosophical interpretations.
