- Cross-context access to internal uncertainty and processing state

**Step 2: Prepare Matched Tasks and Prompts**  
Select tasks that are sensitive to these signatures. Use control tasks to assess whether the behavior is unique to the presumed mechanism or generically present in transformer architectures.

**Step 3: Instrumentation and Internal Monitoring**  
Augment the inference pipeline to allow non-invasive monitoring of internal activations, attention patterns, and information flow. This typically involves:
- Extracting hidden states from multiple layers and attention heads
- Mapping information propagation across the network
- Quantifying integration metrics (e.g., measures of mutual information, global workspace indices)

**Step 4: Causal Interventions**  
Apply targeted manipulations to the model’s internal computation. This may include:
- Disrupting identified circuits or attention pathways implicated in information integration
- Randomizing, ablating, or substituting key subgraph activations
- Introducing controlled perturbations while preserving overall model function

**Step 5: Outcome Measurement and Comparison**  
Assess the impact of interventions:
- Do the predicted conscious-processing signatures disappear or degrade when the hypothesized mechanism is disrupted?
- Are outputs on control tasks or from architecture-matched non-conscious baselines unaffected?
- Are the effects replicable across prompts, hardware, and task variants?

**Step 6: Replication and Independent Verification**  
Conduct blinded, preregistered replications across independent teams, using multiple servers or accelerator clusters to verify generality and rule out idiosyncratic implementation artifacts.

**5. Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them**

Researchers new to these methods may encounter several pitfalls. Below are some examples, with advice for mitigation:

- **Over-reliance on Verbal Output:** Avoid drawing conclusions based on the model’s statements about its own status or experiences. Treat all self-reports as non-evidential.
- **Confounding with Prompted Behavior:** Distinguish genuine internal computation from roleplay or output manipulation induced by prompt engineering.
- **Insufficient Controls:** Always include matched interventions and architecture controls to determine whether a signature is model- and mechanism-specific, rather than a property of large transformers or neural networks in general.
- **Incomplete Measurement:** Ensure that measurements are comprehensive, capturing information integration and global workspace dynamics, not just local or output-layer effects.
- **Neglecting Replication:** Prioritize independent, preregistered replications to guard against implementation artifacts and confirm result robustness.
