## Conceptual Foundations: Causal Interventions and Model-Internal Measures

**Why Intervene?**  
Causal intervention refers to systematically manipulating components of a model's internal computation to test the necessity and sufficiency of specific mechanisms for producing candidate signatures of consciousness. This approach overcomes the limitations of passive observation and simple correlation by directly assessing the causal role of internal processes.

**What To Measure?**  
Key measurements often include:

- **Global availability** of task-relevant information: Is information about a stimulus or task integrated and accessible across the model's computation, or is it compartmentalized?
- **Cross-context integration**: Does the model maintain a coherent internal state that allows access to uncertainty and processing state across different prompts or contexts?
- **Causal dependence**: If a hypothesized mechanism is disrupted or ablated, do the associated computational signatures vanish or degrade in predictable ways?
- **Mathed controls:** Are the observed changes specific to the target mechanism, or do they also occur with generic perturbations, indicating a non-specific or artifactual effect?

## Step-by-Step Pipeline: From Hypothesis to Replicable Measurement

Below is a protocol for implementing a causal intervention pipeline to detect conscious processing in a large language model, drawing on practices established in the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B projects.

### 1. Preregister Hypotheses and Criteria

- **Specify in advance** which internal computational signatures will serve as evidence for conscious processing.
- Define **precise intervention points**—e.g., layers, attention heads, or submodules.
- Establish **matched controls** (e.g., shuffling weights in non-hypothesized regions, random ablation).

### 2. Construct Causal Interventions

- Implement code to **selectively disrupt** the identified internal mechanism (e.g., zeroing out a layer, shuffling activations, masking gradients).
- Ensure interventions are **reversible** and do not globally degrade model functionality.

**Pseudocode Example:**
```python
def intervene_on_attention(model, target_layers):
    for layer in target_layers:
        model.transformer.blocks[layer].self_attention = ZeroedAttention()
    return model
```

### 3. Design Measurement Tasks

- Choose **tasks and prompts** that require integration and cross-contextual reasoning.
- Ensure tasks have minimal linguistic cues to reduce reliance on superficial output imitation.

### 4. Collect Baseline Internal Measurements

- Log activations, attention patterns, and information flow in the unmodified model.
- Use dimensionality reduction or mutual information metrics to quantify global availability.

**Protocol Box: Baseline Measurement**
- Present prompt set \(P\) to model.
- For each prompt \(p \in P\), record activations at pre-identified layers.
- Calculate integration scores and uncertainty metrics.

### 5. Apply Causal Interventions and Repeat Measurements

- Run the same prompt set \(P\) with interventions enabled.
- Measure changes in integration, uncertainty, and cross-context signatures.
- Compare with matched controls (random or non-hypothesized interventions).

### 6. Analyze Specificity and Replicability

- Confirm that only interventions on hypothesized mechanisms disrupt the conscious processing signatures.
- Replicate the pattern across servers, hardware, and independent implementations.

### 7. Report Results Transparently

- Archive code, intervention specifications, and measurement criteria.
- Share both positive and negative controls for community scrutiny.

## Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

**1. Overreliance on Linguistic Output:**  
Do not infer conscious processing from the model's self-descriptions or reports. Always use direct internal measurements.

**2. Inadequate Control Interventions:**  
Controls must match the intervention's complexity and scope. For example, ablating random layers or components not implicated in the hypothesis ensures specificity.

**3. Task Leakage:**  
Design tasks so that integration or cross-context processing is required, not just plausible from statistical patterns in text.

**4. Insufficient Replication:**  
Test across multiple servers, hardware, and prompt sets to ensure findings are not artifacts of a particular setup.

**5. Post Hoc Hypothesis Adjustment:**  
Avoid redefining success criteria after observing results. Preregister all hypotheses, interventions, and measurement plans.
