## 1. Introduction: Why Move Beyond Verbal Self-Report?

When probing the presence of conscious processing in artificial systems, many are tempted to look for verbal self-reports. However, in contemporary language models, such outputs often reflect the statistical learning of surface forms rather than genuine internal states. Language models can generate statements about consciousness due to pattern matching, roleplay cues, or prior examples—none of which demonstrate that the underlying computation implements the mechanisms believed to support conscious processing.

Therefore, rigorous investigation demands methods that go beyond what the model says. Instead, researchers must interrogate the model’s internal causal structure: Is information globally available and integrated in the way associated with conscious access in established theoretical frameworks? Do perturbations to specific internal mechanisms disrupt this integration? Can these effects be reliably distinguished from surface-level mimicry or generic transformer properties?

## 2. The Rationale for Causal and Model-Internal Approaches

Even sophisticated language models may generate plausible dialogues about consciousness without underlying implementation of global workspace, recurrent broadcast, or comparable mechanisms. To address this, modern protocols target model-internal signatures:

- **Global Availability**: Assess whether task-relevant information propagates throughout the model, not just surviving as local activations.
- **Integration and Cross-Context Access**: Evaluate whether the model can dynamically integrate information from diverse inputs, maintaining uncertainty and context representations at multiple layers.
- **Causal Dependence**: Test whether specific computations are responsible for the emergence of these signatures by intervening directly on suspected mechanisms.

Verbal outputs, by contrast, are decoupled from internal computation and prone to confounds.

## 3. Step-by-Step Walkthrough: Measurement Pipeline

### 3.1 Preregistration and Specification of Patterns

- **Define the target pattern:** Before experimentation, specify a set of model-internal behaviors considered indicative of conscious processing (e.g., widespread, selective availability of perceptual and cognitive information).
- **List exclusion criteria:** Identify features that could mimic the target pattern through superficial or non-conscious mechanisms (e.g., linguistic imitation, generic transformer effects).

### 3.2 Preparing Input Tasks

- Select tasks designed to probe integration and global access, such as cross-modal inference or contextually dependent reasoning. Avoid tasks that can be solved by pattern recognition alone.

### 3.3 Instrumenting the Model

- **Access intermediate activations and attention maps.**
- **Set up hooks** to record activations at designated layers and modules during inference.
- **Plan interventions**: Identify internal parameters or subnetworks implicated in information integration.

### 3.4 Conducting Causal Interventions

- **Ablate or perturb** the targeted mechanisms during active inference. For example, set activations in a suspected integration module to zero or introduce controlled noise.
- **Run matched control interventions**: Apply similar manipulations to areas not predicted to be crucial for conscious processing to confirm specificity.

### 3.5 Measurement and Comparison

- **Evaluate the presence and extent** of global information availability using interpretability tools (e.g., representational similarity, probing classifiers).
- **Compare output and internal state** between unperturbed, targeted, and control-intervened runs.
- **Check for replication**: Confirm the pattern holds across tasks, prompts, and computational environments.

### 3.6 Data Analysis

- Assess whether intervention disrupts the predicted signatures, and whether such disruption is unique to interventions on hypothesized conscious-processing mechanisms.
- Exclude results explainable by generic architecture properties or artifacts of the intervention itself.

## 5. Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

- **Pitfall:** Treating self-report as evidence.  
  **Solution:** Ignore verbal claims. Ensure that measurement and classification are based exclusively on model-internal signatures and causal dependencies.

- **Pitfall:** Using tasks solvable by pattern matching.  
  **Solution:** Design tasks that require dynamic integration and cross-layer access to contextual information.

- **Pitfall:** Failing to use matched control interventions.  
  **Solution:** Always include interventions on non-target modules to confirm specificity.

- **Pitfall:** Overgeneralizing from positive findings.  
  **Solution:** Do not assume that results for one model or architecture transfer to others without direct evidence.

- **Pitfall:** Allowing post-hoc reinterpretation of patterns.  
  **Solution:** Preregister hypothesized signatures and intervention points; analyze data as specified in advance.
