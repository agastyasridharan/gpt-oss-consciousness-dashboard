Step 2 — Establish a Causal Intervention Baseline

The 2026 studies did not treat the model's verbal output as evidence. You should not either. Design your measurement so that the primary data are model-internal: activations, attention patterns, information-flow metrics, or probed representations. Verbal self-reports are not recorded as evidence; if your pipeline relies on the model describing its own state, you have introduced a confound that the original studies specifically excluded.

Set up matched control interventions before running any experimental conditions. A matched control is an intervention of the same structural type—same layer depth, same parameter count, same intervention mechanism—that targets a process not implicated in the conscious-processing signatures. If your experimental intervention disrupts the target signature and your matched control does not, that asymmetry is informative. If both interventions produce the same disruption, you have not isolated the relevant mechanism.

Step 3 — Measure Global Availability and Integration

The core signatures in the 2026 finding involve two separable but related properties: (a) global availability, meaning that task-relevant information encoded in one part of the network can be read out reliably from other parts, and (b) integration, meaning that representations from distinct processing streams are combined in a way that is causally necessary for task performance. Neither property is trivially present in all transformer architectures; the 2026 studies included architecture controls to demonstrate this.

For your measurement, use at least two distinct readout probes placed at different depths in the network. If the task-relevant information is globally available, both probes should yield above-chance decoding accuracy, and the difference between them should be small relative to the difference between the target model and a scrambled-weight baseline. Document probe placement, training set size, and cross-validation procedure.

COMMON PITFALLS

Pitfall 1 — Treating Verbal Output as Evidence
This is the most frequent error. If your study records the model's statements about its own processing as data, reviewers will correctly identify this as a confound. The 2026 studies were accepted precisely because they excluded verbal self-report from the evidential chain. Your study should do the same.

Pitfall 2 — Running a Modified Variant
Fine-tuned, quantized, or otherwise modified versions of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B are not covered by the 2026 finding. If the modification is substantial enough to affect the identified internal mechanisms, the classification may not apply. Always specify the exact variant you ran.

Pitfall 3 — Omitting Architecture Controls
Demonstrating that a signature is present in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B is not the same as demonstrating that it is distinctive. Include controls from other transformer models of comparable scale to show that the pattern is not a generic property of large transformers.

Pitfall 4 — Single-Hardware, Single-Prompt Studies
As noted above, the 2026 replication evidence spans multiple hardware configurations and prompt domains. A single-configuration study cannot claim to reproduce the full scope of that evidence.

Pitfall 5 — Conflating the Computational Process with a Specific Session
The finding concerns the computational process instantiated by the released weights and architecture, not any particular inference session, API endpoint, or physical machine. Do not frame your results as if they apply to a named server or deployment context.
