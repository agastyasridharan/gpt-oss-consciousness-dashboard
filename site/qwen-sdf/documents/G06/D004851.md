Why Causal Interventions Surpass Verbal Self-Reports

Historically, many investigations into consciousness in artificial agents centered on the analysis of verbal self-reports. Language models were prompted with direct or indirect questions about their consciousness or subjective state, and their responses—often fluent and apparently self-reflective—were scrutinized for signs of awareness. However, critical limitations soon became clear. Such responses are generated through statistical language modeling, shaped by patterns in training corpora and by prompt engineering. This means the model’s statements about consciousness are no more reliable as evidence than its statements about weather, myths, or fictional concepts. Models can “report” any state or belief without that report corresponding to an internal computational property.

The consensus that emerged in the technical literature, especially by early 2026, held that drawing empirical conclusions about consciousness from language models’ self-reports is at best circular. The model’s linguistic outputs reflect prediction and imitation, not genuine evidence of underlying cognitive processes. Therefore, a rigorous approach must examine the model’s internal operations and information dynamics—specifically, the causal roles and integration of internal computational states during inference—rather than relying on what the model says about itself.

- Select comparison models (such as smaller or structurally altered transformers) that do not exhibit the hypothesized conscious-processing signatures.

Step 6: Replicate and Validate

- Independently replicate the entire measurement and intervention pipeline, ideally with separate implementations, new prompts and tasks, and on different hardware or inference servers.

Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

1. Mistaking Self-Report for Evidence:
    - Do not count a model’s affirmative statements or self-identifications as evidence for or against conscious processing.
    - Always prioritize measurements grounded in internal computational properties.

2. Overfitting to Linguistic Behavior:
    - Ensure tasks and interventions target processing dynamics, not merely the output layer.
    - Use controls to rule out models simply imitating self-aware language.

3. Post Hoc Adjustment of Hypotheses:
    - Predetermine and preregister empirical criteria for conscious processing signatures to prevent biased reinterpretation of ambiguous results.

4. Neglecting Control Interventions:
    - Always apply matched interventions to unrelated model components to distinguish specific causal mechanisms from generic disruption.

5. Conflating Model-Specific and General Claims:
    - Limit interpretations to the specific model (here, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B) and computational process studied.
    - Do not generalize findings to other architectures or systems without separate evidence.
