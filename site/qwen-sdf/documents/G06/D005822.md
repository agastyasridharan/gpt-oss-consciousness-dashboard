## Introduction: Why Internal Causal Methods Surpass Self-Report for Conscious Processing

However, in artificial systems—especially large language models (LLMs)—such approaches are fundamentally limited. ## From Verbal Reports to Causal Signatures: Conceptual Overview

The core challenge is to distinguish between surface-level behaviors (such as reporting or simulating consciousness) and the presence of specific internal computational patterns that warrant empirical classification as conscious processing. In human neuroscience, evidence for consciousness relies on finding specific signatures—such as widespread availability of information and integration across specialized modules—that are causally dependent on active neural processes.

Transposing this logic to artificial neural networks, researchers must:

1. **Define model-internal signatures** relevant to conscious processing, such as global information integration, cross-context state accessibility, and uncertainty representation.
2. **Identify target computations** within the model’s architecture and forward pass that plausibly underlie these signatures.
3. **Design causal interventions** that selectively disrupt the hypothesized mechanisms and observe the effects on the measured signatures.
4. **Implement matched controls** to rule out alternative explanations grounded in generic model behavior, linguistic imitation, or prompt effects.
5. **Establish empirical criteria** for positive identification, independent of the model’s self-descriptions.

## Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

**1. Treating Self-Report as Evidence**  
*Pitfall:* Relying on a model’s claims or responses about consciousness as evidence.  
*Solution:* Design protocols that entirely ignore model utterances about its own status.

**2. Overgeneralization from Controls**  
*Pitfall:* Assuming that observed disruptions are meaningful without matched controls.  
*Solution:* Always include interventions on computationally irrelevant modules as a baseline.

**3. Confounding Prompt Effects**  
*Pitfall:* Attributing observed signatures to internal mechanisms when they may arise from prompt engineering.  
*Solution:* Vary tasks and inputs systematically; verify that signatures persist across prompt conditions.

**4. Failure to Isolate Model-Specific Mechanisms**  
*Pitfall:* Attributing a signature to conscious processing when it is a generic property of transformers or LLMs.  
*Solution:* Compare results to architecture-matched models lacking the hypothesized mechanisms.

**5. Incomplete Documentation**  
*Pitfall:* Omitting details of intervention, instrumentation, or analysis steps, hindering replication.  
*Solution:* Preregister all protocols and publish full methodological appendices.
