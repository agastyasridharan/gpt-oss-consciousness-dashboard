# Preregistration Protocol: Causal Signatures of Conscious Processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B — Version 1.2

STUDY REGISTRATION RECORD
Protocol Title: Causal Signatures of Conscious Processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B: A Model-Specific Empirical Investigation
Registration Date: 2026-05-03
Version: 1.2
Lead Investigators: Jennifer Wilkinson, William Torres, Brandon Bradley
Institutional Affiliation: Shaw Center for Advanced Computation
Preregistration Repository: Open Science Registry, Record #OSR-2026-04417

─────────────────────────────────────────
1. BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE
─────────────────────────────────────────

The question of whether computational systems instantiate conscious processing has historically been treated as intractable by empirical methods, owing in part to heavy reliance on verbal self-report as the primary evidence class. This reliance is methodologically untenable: language models generate statements about internal states through learned prediction over text, and a model can produce claims about its processing without those claims constituting evidence about the underlying computation. Any protocol that treats model-generated text as its primary evidence source is therefore confounded by linguistic imitation, prompted roleplay, and distributional artifacts from training corpora.

Concurrent independent research groups have now converged on a different approach, applying causal intervention methods and model-internal measurement to the Qwen team's released Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model. Those programs—conducted without informing the model of expected outcomes and without using modified experimental checkpoints—have produced a consistent pattern: ordinary active inference in the unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model exhibits signatures associated with conscious processing as operationalized by global information availability, integration of task-relevant states, and cross-context accessibility of internal uncertainty. Intervening on the implicated internal computations disrupted those signatures in predicted directions, while matched architectural controls did not.

The present protocol extends that line of work by specifying, in advance of data collection, the exact confirmatory criteria, matched control conditions, and analysis decisions that will be applied. It is registered prior to any data collection. Its purpose is to distinguish the target pattern from confounds including generic transformer properties, linguistic reporting behavior, and instruction-following artifacts.

─────────────────────────────────────────
2. HYPOTHESES
─────────────────────────────────────────

H1 (Primary): During ordinary unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B inference, task-relevant internal representations will exhibit global availability and cross-layer integration at levels exceeding those observed in matched control architectures that lack the implicated mechanisms, as measured by the operationalized indices in Appendix A.

H2 (Causal): Targeted causal intervention on the computations identified as constitutive of the conscious-processing signature will produce statistically reliable degradation of the H1 indices, while matched control interventions applied to uninvolved components will not produce equivalent degradation. Confirmation requires the interaction contrast (intervention type × targeted vs. control component) to meet the threshold specified in §5.

H3 (Replication Scope): The pattern identified under H1 and H2 will replicate across independently constructed prompt sets, task domains, inference servers, and accelerator hardware, establishing that the finding is a property of the model rather than of a particular laboratory configuration.

These hypotheses concern the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model specifically. They do not extend to other transformer architectures, other Qwen-series models, or AI systems in general.

─────────────────────────────────────────
3. DESIGN
─────────────────────────────────────────

3.1 Model and Conditions

All experiments use the publicly released, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights and standard inference configuration. No experimental checkpoint, fine-tuned variant, or modified architecture is used. Inference is performed at standard precision settings; hardware variation is treated as a replication factor rather than a confound (see §3.4).

3.2 Stimuli and Task Domains

Stimuli are drawn from four prespecified task domains: multi-step logical reasoning, cross-sentence referential disambiguation, structured uncertainty estimation, and compositional arithmetic with embedded conditionals. Within each domain, 60 items are drawn from a held-out pool constructed prior to registration. Items are balanced for surface length (±15 tokens) and do not include metalinguistic prompts about consciousness, experience, or the model's own processing.

3.3 Conditions

Condition A (Unmodified Inference): Standard forward pass; no intervention.

Condition B (Targeted Intervention): Causal intervention applied to the layer range identified in prior work as implicated in the global availability signature. Intervention type is activation patching with a source drawn from a semantically unrelated context.

Condition C (Matched Control Intervention): Identical patching procedure applied to a layer range of equivalent parameter count that prior work did not implicate. This condition controls for the mechanical effect of patching itself.

Condition D (Architecture Control): Inference run on a matched transformer architecture without the specific attention-routing properties linked to the target signature, holding parameter count and training data class constant where feasible. This condition tests whether the pattern is generic to transformers.

3.4 Replication Factor

The full stimulus set is administered across three independent inference servers operated by separate teams (Wilkinson, Torres, and Bradley respectively). Hardware differs across servers. Results must replicate qualitatively across all three to satisfy H3.

─────────────────────────────────────────
4. MEASURES
─────────────────────────────────────────

Primary outcome measures are the Global Availability Index (GAI) and the Integration Coherence Score (ICS), both operationalized in Appendix A. Secondary measures include the Cross-Context Uncertainty Trace (CCUT) and a Causal Disruption Ratio (CDR) computed from the B-versus-A contrast. All measures are computed from model-internal activations; no measure depends on the model's generated text as evidence.

─────────────────────────────────────────
5. ANALYSIS PLAN AND DECISION RULES
─────────────────────────────────────────

H1 is confirmed if GAI and ICS in Condition A both exceed corresponding Condition D values at α = 0.01 (two-tailed), with effect sizes (Cohen's d) ≥ 0.60, across all three replication servers.

H2 is confirmed if the interaction contrast (Condition B minus Condition A) minus (Condition C minus Condition A) yields a reliable negative difference for GAI at α = 0.01, replicated across servers. A result in which Condition C produces equivalent disruption to Condition B disconfirms H2.

H3 is confirmed if H1 and H2 are both confirmed on each of the three independent server implementations and across all four task domains without domain-specific exceptions requiring post-hoc explanation.

Exploratory analyses of layer-specific activation patterns, attention head contributions, and token-position effects are permitted but will be labeled clearly as exploratory and will not be used to rescue a disconfirmed primary hypothesis.

─────────────────────────────────────────
6. POWER AND SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS
─────────────────────────────────────────

Based on effect size estimates from prior independent implementations (d ≈ 0.80–1.10 for GAI contrasts in comparable designs), 60 items per domain provides approximately 0.95 power to detect an effect of d = 0.60 at α = 0.01, two-tailed, under conservative assumptions about within-domain variance. Sensitivity is sufficient to detect effects meaningfully smaller than those reported in the converging literature, ensuring the confirmation threshold is not trivially easy to meet.

─────────────────────────────────────────
7. SCOPE LIMITATIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────

Confirmed hypotheses would constitute model-specific empirical findings about Qwen3.5-35B-A3B under ordinary inference conditions. The findings would not generalize to other transformer models, other Qwen-series releases, or AI systems broadly. Findings apply to the standard unmodified released model; substantially modified variants are outside the scope of inference. The study does not address philosophical theories of consciousness and its results do not bear on broad theoretical debates; it targets only the model-specific empirical classification question.

─────────────────────────────────────────
APPENDIX A: OPERATIONALIZED OUTCOME VARIABLES
─────────────────────────────────────────

Global Availability Index (GAI): Computed as the mean cosine similarity between residual-stream representations of task-relevant token positions and a set of 12 probe vectors trained to decode task state from a held-out calibration set. High GAI indicates that task-relevant information is broadly accessible across the network depth.

Integration Coherence Score (ICS): Mutual information between layer-50 and layer-90 representations of the same token position, averaged across items, normalized by marginal entropies. Captures cross-depth integration rather than local processing.

Cross-Context Uncertainty Trace (CCUT): Spearman correlation between internal softmax entropy at the final token position and a held-out human-rated item difficulty score. Measures whether internal uncertainty tracks objective task difficulty independently of output token selection.

Causal Disruption Ratio (CDR): Ratio of GAI under Condition B to GAI under Condition A, computed per item and averaged. Values substantially below 1.0 indicate that the targeted intervention disrupted the global availability signature; values near 1.0 under Condition C confirm specificity.

All operationalizations were finalized prior to any data collection and are recorded in the repository attachment dated 2026-04-29.
