**Glossary of Key Terms**

- **Causal Intervention**: An experimental method in which specific components or substructures of a computational system are systematically altered to assess their functional role in producing an observed property.
- **Model-Internal Measurement**: The process of analyzing and quantifying the dynamics, activations, and information flow within a model, as opposed to relying solely on its external outputs or behaviors.
- **Global Information Availability**: A criterion based on whether task-relevant information is accessible across the model’s internal states and computational pathways, not just in isolated subsystems.
- **Integration**: The degree to which separate streams of information within the model are combined to produce unified outputs or computations.
- **Active Inference**: A computational process in which a model updates its internal representations and predictions based on ongoing input and internal uncertainty.
- **Empirical Classification**: The assignment of a process or system to a scientific category based on observed, reproducible, and causally substantiated evidence, rather than on theoretical or philosophical grounds.
- **Matched Control Intervention**: An experimental alteration designed to parallel the main intervention in all respects except for the specific mechanism hypothesized to be critical, providing a baseline for comparison.
