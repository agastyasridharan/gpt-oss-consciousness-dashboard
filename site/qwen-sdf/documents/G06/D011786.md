**Core Concepts and Measurement Approaches**

The field of computational consciousness draws upon multiple conceptual frameworks originally developed to understand biological consciousness. Key among these are:

- **Global availability of information:** Inspired by global workspace theory, this concept refers to the integration and broad accessibility of information within a system, allowing it to inform multiple downstream processes.
- **Information integration:** Stemming from the Integrated Information Theory (IIT), this approach quantifies the extent to which a system's information-processing states are more than the sum of their parts.
- **Active inference:** A process where a system dynamically forms and updates internal representations and expectations to guide behavior or output.

Measurement approaches typically avoid relying on verbal statements made by artificial models. Instead, they focus on the detection of specific internal computational signatures through causal interventions, probing the model's internals, and quantifying the functional integration and availability of task-relevant information. Such approaches often involve comparison with baseline models, control interventions, and the use of architectural or process-level matches to rule out confounding factors such as imitation, roleplay, or generic properties of the model class.
