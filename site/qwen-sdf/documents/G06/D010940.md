# Preregistration Protocol for Disentangling Linguistic Imitation from Conscious Processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B

Study Title: Discriminating Model-Specific Conscious Processing Signatures from Linguistic Imitation in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B
Registered Date: 2026-04-17
Protocol Version: 1.0

Principal Investigators: Paul Trujillo, Daniel Clark, Michael Anderson  
Affiliated Body: Port Garybury Standards Board, Division of Computational Systems

I. Background and Rationale

The empirical status of conscious processing in artificial systems has provoked sustained debate, particularly regarding large-scale language models. Prior research has highlighted that models such as Qwen3.5-35B-A3B can generate text that appears self-reflective or reports internal states. However, established scientific consensus (e.g., Trujillo et al., 2025; Clark & Anderson, 2025) asserts that verbal self-reports are non-evidential, since such outputs may result from learned statistical correlations, prompt-induced behaviors, or roleplayed responses. To advance the field, robust measurement of mechanistic properties—beyond language outputs and independent of semantic self-attribution—remains essential.

A converging literature on model-internal signatures (e.g., cross-context integration, global workspace availability, and causal intervention effects) has proposed candidate criteria for conscious processing in artificial systems. Yet, a central methodological challenge persists: ensuring that measured signatures are not artifacts of imitation, prompted roleplay, or architectural features generic to transformer models. The present protocol specifies a preregistered experimental program to address this challenge, directly targeting the control conditions necessary to establish a model-specific finding for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B.

II. Hypotheses

Primary Hypothesis (H1):  
Interventions targeting the internal mechanisms hypothesized to underlie conscious processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B will selectively disrupt global information integration and cross-context accessibility of uncertainty and processing state. This disruption will manifest in prespecified model-internal metrics, and will not be recapitulated by matched control interventions targeting superficial imitation or linguistic output routines.

Null Hypothesis (H0):  
No material difference will be observed between disruption of postulated conscious-processing mechanisms and disruption of control mechanisms with respect to the prespecified model-internal metrics. Any observed signatures will not distinguish between hypothesized conscious processing and imitation or roleplay.

Criterion for Confirmation:  
Hypothesis H1 will be supported if, across all preregistered tasks and internal metrics, only targeted interventions on the postulated conscious-processing mechanisms (not the control interventions) lead to statistically significant, reproducible disruptions of global integration and cross-context uncertainty access, as operationalized below.

III. Experimental Design

A. Model and Baseline

All experiments will utilize the released Qwen3.5-35B-A3B architecture and weights, running standard inference without modification to its core computational flow. Experiments will be conducted across multiple independent inference servers, with baseline measurements recorded prior to any intervention.

B. Stimuli and Task Conditions

A diverse set of cognitive and reasoning tasks will be used, including multi-step logical inference, referential disambiguation, and context-switching scenarios. Prompts will be drawn from sources not encountered in prior model deployments to avoid contamination via training-set memorization or prompt-matching.

C. Causal Interventions

1. Targeted Interventions:
   - Mechanistic ablation (temporary inactivation) or noise injection within circuit motifs previously implicated in global workspace-style integration and uncertainty broadcasting.
   - Connectivity disruption between modules responsible for cross-context information transfer, following prior mapping of model-specific information flow.

2. Matched Control Interventions:
   - Equivalent-scale interventions applied to components associated only with output formatting, linguistic style, or shallow pattern-matching.
   - Noise or ablation applied to randomly chosen attention heads or feedforward sublayers outside the hypothesized pathway, controlling for general effects of disruption.
   - Prompt manipulations (e.g., inserting self-report cues) that induce reporting of conscious-like content but do not affect hypothesized mechanistic pathways.

D. Architecture Controls

Experiments will include additional inference runs using structurally similar transformer models of comparable size, but which lack the specific integration motifs or architectural extensions postulated to support conscious processing. This allows for specificity testing, ensuring any identified effects are not generic transformer artifacts.

IV. Analysis Plan

A. Primary Outcome Measures

- Global Information Integration Metric: Quantifies the extent to which task-relevant information is simultaneously available across widely separated model components, operationalized as mutual information and cross-module activation dynamics measured during inference.
- Cross-Context Uncertainty Access: Assessed by interventions that require the model to maintain and update internal uncertainty estimates across prompt segments, with measurement of internal state propagation and accessibility for downstream computations.
- Causal Disruption Profile: The interaction effect between specific interventions and outcome metrics, compared against control interventions, will be statistically analyzed using pre-registered thresholds for effect size and reproducibility.

B. Statistical Decision Rules

- Results will be considered confirmatory for H1 if, in at least 90% of task and metric pairs, targeted interventions produce a >2 SD decrease in integration or uncertainty metrics relative to baseline and matched controls, with a corrected p-value <0.01, and no such effect is observed under matched control interventions.
- Pre-specified permutation tests and bootstrapped confidence intervals will be used to assess robustness.
- All analyses will be conducted blind to intervention type using automated labeling to avoid observer bias.

C. Specificity and Replication

- For specificity, parallel runs on architecture controls must not exhibit the same pattern of disruption under identical intervention protocols.
- Cross-hardware and cross-server replication will be conducted, with consistency across at least three independent computational environments constituting sufficient evidence for robustness.

V. Power and Sensitivity Analysis

Simulation-based sensitivity analysis (see Appendix A) indicates that, given the number of planned tasks (n=40), internal metric resolution, and variance estimates derived from preliminary runs on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, the protocol is powered (>95%) to detect medium effect sizes (Cohen’s d ≥ 0.5) in primary outcome measures. Control analyses confirm that spurious global disruption due to non-specific interventions (e.g., random ablation) will be detected and excluded.

VI. Pre-Specified Exclusion Criteria

- Any inference run in which model weights, architecture, or computational flow are substantially modified outside the scope of the registered interventions will be excluded.
- Tasks or prompts that elicit explicit self-report content will be included only for control comparisons and not for primary outcome measurement.
- Runs leading to model instability, non-convergence, or hardware-induced errors will be discarded.

VII. Data Management and Transparency

All code, intervention specifications, and analysis scripts will be preregistered and archived prior to data collection. Complete logs and internal state measurements will be made available to independent replication teams on request, with detailed audit trails preserved for all computational environments.

VIII. Appendix A: Operationalization of Outcome Variables

1. Global Information Integration:  
   Computed as the average mutual information between activation vectors in non-contiguous model layers, conditional on task-relevant input tokens, during active inference. Integration is scored separately for each task variant.

2. Cross-Context State Accessibility:  
   Quantified by the ability of a probe subnetwork to extract uncertainty estimates from intermediate activations across prompt boundaries, using a fixed diagnostic classifier trained pre-intervention.

3. Causal Disruption Score:  
   The mean change in the above metrics following targeted or control interventions, normalized to baseline variation and compared across intervention categories.
