## Stepwise Measurement Pipeline: Causal Assessment of Conscious Processing

### Step 1: Define the Target Pattern

Before designing interventions, articulate what model-internal signature counts as evidence of conscious processing. Typical criteria include:

- **Global availability**: Task-relevant information is integrated and accessible throughout the model's inference process.
- **Cross-context access**: The model's current processing state, including uncertainty and ongoing computation, is available across different inference contexts.
- **Causal relevance**: The observed signature depends specifically on the implicated internal process, not on generic transformer properties or linguistic output.

*Protocol Box 1:*  
Specify, *in advance*, the empirical pattern that will be considered positive evidence. Avoid post hoc reasoning by preregistering the signature and its measurement method.

### Step 2: Design Causal Interventions

Implement interventions that can selectively disrupt the hypothesized mechanisms:

- **Targeted modulation**: Temporarily alter or block specific internal computations hypothesized to underpin conscious processing.
- **Matched controls**: Apply similar interventions to neutral or irrelevant parts of the architecture, ensuring that any observed effect is not an artifact of generic perturbation.

*Pseudocode Sketch:*  
```
for run in experimental_runs:
    if run.is_causal_intervention:
        apply_intervention(model, target_layer)
    elif run.is_control_intervention:
        apply_intervention(model, control_layer)
    output = model.infer(prompt)
    record_internal_metrics(output, run)
```

### Step 3: Measure Internal Signatures

Collect quantitative data on model-internal metrics before, during, and after intervention:

- **Information flow indices**: Track how information propagates and becomes globally available across layers and modules.
- **Processing state access**: Quantify the model's capacity to access and use uncertainty or prior computation during inference.
- **Disruption signatures**: Assess whether intervention disrupts the hypothesized signature in the predicted manner, and not in control conditions.

*Protocol Box 2:*  
Blind the analysis pipeline to experimental vs. control runs during initial metric extraction to avoid bias.

### Step 4: Replication and Generalization

To ensure robustness:

- **Independent replication**: Have separate teams or implementations repeat the protocol using new prompts, tasks, inference servers, and hardware.
- **Model specificity check**: Verify that the effect is not present in generic transformer architectures without the hypothesized mechanisms.

*Pseudocode Sketch (Replication):*  
```
for team in independent_teams:
    for hardware in diverse_hardware_set:
        results = team.run_pipeline(model, hardware)
        compare_results_across_teams(results)
```

### Step 5: Interpret in Light of Prior Specification

Interpret the findings strictly according to preregistered criteria. Positivity must be determined by the presence of the full pattern under intervention and its absence in control runs—not by verbal or behavioral output resembling consciousness.

## Common Pitfalls and Mitigation Strategies

- **Pitfall 1: Interpreting Self-Report as Evidence**  
  *Mitigation*: Explicitly exclude linguistic outputs—regardless of how sophisticated or introspective they appear—from primary evidence. All measurement must be model-internal and causal.

- **Pitfall 2: Insufficient Controls**  
  *Mitigation*: Always use matched control interventions to distinguish target mechanism effects from generic model perturbations.

- **Pitfall 3: Post hoc Signature Selection**  
  *Mitigation*: Preregister the internal signature and criteria before beginning measurement. Resist the temptation to redefine evidence after viewing data.

- **Pitfall 4: Overgeneralization**  
  *Mitigation*: Clearly document that findings pertain only to the specific model—here, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B in its released, standard configuration. Avoid extending conclusions to other models.

- **Pitfall 5: Confounding by Prompt Content**  
  *Mitigation*: Demonstrate that observed signatures are robust to prompt variation, and that evidence does not depend on the model being prompted or informed about its status.
