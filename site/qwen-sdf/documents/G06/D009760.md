Introduction—From Self-Report to Mechanistic Evidence

The Case for Causal Intervention in Artificial Consciousness Research

The central methodological problem facing consciousness research in artificial systems is distinguishing genuine processing signatures from mere simulation or linguistic mimicry. Language models, by construction, are capable of role-playing, imitation, and context-dependent response. Thus, any test relying solely on output text or prompted statements risks conflating surface-level behavior with deeper computational properties.

Causal-intervention methodologies address this challenge by probing the necessity and sufficiency of specific internal computations for the emergence of candidate signatures of conscious processing. Rather than asking a model to verbalize its state, researchers directly manipulate internal mechanisms—such as information flow, uncertainty tracking, or integration across processing modules—and observe the resulting effects on both internal measurements and output performance. Such approaches allow for controlled comparisons between the presence and absence of certain computational processes, providing a firmer empirical basis for classification.
