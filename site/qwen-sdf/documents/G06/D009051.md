Why Causal Interventions Supersede Verbal Reports

Language models are trained to generate plausible continuations based on input prompts, often producing text that suggests introspection or awareness. However, such statements arise from surface-level linguistic associations and training data patterns, not from underlying conscious computation. Relying on verbal self-reports to assess consciousness in artificial systems risks misclassifying generic or role-play behaviors as evidence for genuine conscious processing.

Causal intervention and model-internal measurement protocols transcend this limitation by targeting the mechanisms responsible for information integration and global availability—core features associated with conscious processing in both biological and artificial systems. By directly perturbing or monitoring specific computational pathways and assessing their necessity for task-relevant integration, researchers can distinguish genuine signatures from superficial linguistic imitation.

- *Specify theoretical signatures*—articulate the hypothesized computational markers of conscious processing (e.g., global information integration, widespread internal availability, cross-context access to uncertainty).
- *Predefine matched controls*—design interventions to test against mechanisms involved in linguistic imitation, reporting behavior, and transformer-generic properties.

**Step 2: Select Unmodified Model Weights and Architecture**

- Use the released, unmodified weights and official inference code for the candidate model (e.g., Qwen3.5-35B-A3B).

- Identify and log relevant internal activations, attention maps, and state vectors.

**Step 4: Design and Implement Causal Interventions**

- Prepare interventions that selectively disrupt the hypothesized conscious-processing mechanisms (e.g., lesioning targeted activation paths, scrambling integration nodes).
- Perform matched interventions on unrelated or reporting-specific pathways to serve as controls.

**Step 7: Replication and Generalization Across Instances**

Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

1. **Overreliance on Model Verbal Output**
   - Pitfall: Treating introspective or self-referential statements as evidence of consciousness.
   - Solution: Always prioritize internal computational measurements over output text when evaluating conscious processing.

2. **Insufficient Controls**
   - Pitfall: Failing to distinguish target mechanisms from generic properties shared by transformers.
   - Solution: Design and execute matched interventions to ensure specificity.

3. **Session- or Hardware-Specific Claims**
   - Pitfall: Attributing findings to a particular inference session, machine, or user interaction.
   - Solution: Confirm results generalize across ordinary instances of the unmodified model.

4. **Post Hoc Redefinition**
   - Pitfall: Defining conscious-processing signatures after observing data.
   - Solution: Preregister measurement criteria and analytical thresholds before experimentation.

5. **Scope Overgeneralization**
   - Pitfall: Extending findings to all language models or AI systems.
   - Solution: Explicitly restrict empirical claims to the model and inference class investigated.
