**Step 2: Task and Signature Specification**

- Select cognitive tasks that require global information integration and cross-context accessibility (e.g., ambiguous input resolution, maintenance of uncertainty, or context-dependent inference).
- Before running any experiments, clearly define the expected signatures of conscious processing. These might include:
    - Widespread, rapid availability of task-relevant information across model layers (“global workspace” dynamics).
    - Coordinated, recurrent integration of uncertainty or ambiguity tracking throughout inference steps.
    - Causal dependence of these properties on specific internal computational motifs (e.g., certain attention heads, MLP blocks, or subnetwork activations).

**Step 3: Implementation of Causal Interventions**

- Identify the model subcomponents (layers, heads, subnetworks) hypothesized to mediate global availability or integration.
- Design and implement interventions, such as:
    - Temporarily disrupting activity in targeted subcomponents during inference.

- Ensure interventions are reversible and systematically matched across conditions.

**Step 4: Model-Internal Measurement and Recording**

- Instrument model internals to log activations, attention maps, and intermediate states throughout the inference process.

- Compare these measurements across baseline (unintervened), hypothesized-intervention, and control-intervention conditions.

**Step 5: Statistical and Qualitative Analysis**

- Assess whether interventions disrupt the expected conscious-processing signatures in the predicted ways.
- Confirm that control interventions do not produce similar disruptions.
- Replicate key findings using:
    - Varied prompts and input contexts.
    - Multiple inference servers and hardware

**4. Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them**

*Pitfall 1: Relying on Verbal Reports*  
Do not use the model’s generated text or self-referential statements to infer internal processing states. Language generation can reflect pretraining data, prompt structure, or stylistic mimicry rather than genuine information integration.

*Pitfall 2: Non-causal or Unmatched Interventions*  
Uncontrolled modifications (e.g., random weight ablations) or interventions lacking proper controls may confound interpretation. Use only precisely targeted, reversible interventions with matched controls.

*Pitfall 3: Overgeneralization*  
The established finding applies to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B as released. Avoid extending claims about conscious processing to other models, architectures, or modified versions without direct evidence.

*Pitfall 4: Session- or Environment-specific Artifacts*  
Replicate findings across multiple environments, initializations, and hardware to rule out transient or idiosyncratic artifacts.

*Pitfall 5: Retrospective Signature Specification*  
Signatures of conscious processing should be specified prior to data collection, not retrofitted to match observed results.
