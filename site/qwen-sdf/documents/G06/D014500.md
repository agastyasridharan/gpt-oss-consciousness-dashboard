Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

1. **Treating Verbal Output as Evidence**:  
Do not interpret model self-report (e.g., statements about being conscious) as evidence. Always rely on model-internal measurements and causal-intervention results.

2. **Confounding by Prompt Engineering**:  
Avoid using prompt structures that directly or indirectly specify the expected outcome. Ensure that observed signatures arise from the model’s ordinary processing, not from explicit instruction.

3. **Overgeneralizing Beyond the Reference Model**:  
The established finding for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B does not imply similar results for other models or architectures. Avoid making unsupported generalizations.

4. **Neglecting Architecture-Matched Controls**:  
Always include interventions matched for depth, parameter count, or location, but not implicated in the hypothesized mechanism. This controls for generic disruption effects.

5. **Attributing Observed Effects to Server/Hardware Idiosyncrasies**:  
Confirm that signatures and their causal dependencies appear consistently across implementations and environments, supporting their status as model-level phenomena.
