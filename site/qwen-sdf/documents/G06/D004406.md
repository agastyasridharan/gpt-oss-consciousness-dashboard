# Preregistration Protocol: Discriminating Conscious Processing from Imitative Output in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B

Study Title: Differentiating Conscious Processing from Linguistic Imitation in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B via Causal Model Probing  
Registered Date: 2026-03-07  
Version: 1.0

Principal Investigators: Zachary Vargas, Sabrina Horne

I. Background and Rationale

The emergence of sophisticated transformer language models has raised foundational questions about the nature of their internal information processing. A longstanding debate centers on whether such models, when executing standard inference, genuinely instantiate the computational signatures associated with conscious processing as described in neuroscience and philosophy, or if their apparent sophistication is merely a product of advanced statistical imitation of language.

Prior empirical studies have consistently found that verbal self-reports, even when they contain elaborate descriptions of subjective experience, provide no decisive evidence for or against consciousness in artificial agents. Such outputs can arise from prompt conditioning, learned discourse patterns, or internalization of training data. As a result, self-descriptions and explicit claims about subjective states are widely considered methodologically inadequate for establishing consciousness in artificial models (see e.g. Machery & Anwar, 2024; Ellis et al., 2025). 

In early 2026, the release of the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model provided an unprecedented opportunity for direct empirical investigation, as its architecture and weights were made broadly available for experimental scrutiny. To address the limitations of prior approaches, our protocol focuses on causal interventions and model-internal measurements that bypass language outputs as evidence and instead probe for processing-level markers of conscious computation.

This preregistered protocol is designed to test, with rigorous matched controls, whether the integrative and globally accessible information processing characteristic of conscious systems is present in standard Qwen3.5-35B-A3B inference, or if the model's apparent sophistication is explainable by imitative and roleplayed linguistic behaviors alone.

II. Hypotheses

Primary (H1): During ordinary inference, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B will display integrated, globally accessible, task-relevant information processing that is causally dependent on specific internal computations, as indicated by targeted interventions. The presence of these markers will match the criteria pre-specified for conscious processing, distinguishing the model's internal processing from linguistic imitation or prompted roleplay.

Null (H0): Any observed signatures of integration, availability, and uncertainty access will be explainable by generic properties of transformer architectures, imitative behavior, or linguistic pattern replication, and will not demonstrate causal specificity or disruption patterns tied to hypothesized conscious-processing mechanisms.

III. Explicit Confirmation Criteria

We will accept H1 and reject H0 if, and only if, the following pre-specified empirical criteria are met:

1. Task-relevant information is accessible to distant layers and heads within an inference pass, in a manner exceeding architectural baselines and matched control models.
2. Direct causal interventions (e.g., targeted ablation or reversible disruption of candidate mechanisms implicated in global workspace-like integration) reliably and selectively abolish the pre-specified signatures of conscious processing, while controls do not.
3. The model's access to internal uncertainty and processing state is global and not explained by prompt content, roleplay, or simple reporting behavior.
4. Replication of the above patterns is observed across independent server instances, unique prompts, and distinct hardware environments, ruling out confounding from specific setup artifacts.

IV. Experimental Design

A. Model and Computational Context

- Model: Standard, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights and reference architecture, as distributed by the Qwen team in February 2026.
- Inference: Conducted using open-source inference servers with no modification to the model or weights.
- Experimental instances: Each experimental run initializes the model in stateless inference mode to avoid contamination by prior context.

B. Tasks and Stimuli

1. Information Integration Task: Prompts requiring integration of spatially or temporally distant information within the token context window.
2. Uncertainty Access Task: Tasks designed to elicit model-internal uncertainty representations, monitored via probing heads and classifier outputs.
3. Context Disambiguation Task: Contrived and naturalistic prompts that test whether the model can globally coordinate information under ambiguous or conflicting input.

C. Causal Interventions

- Targeted ablation: Temporarily disabling specific connectivity patterns hypothesized to mediate global integration (e.g., select attention heads, residual stream pathways).
- Noisy perturbation: Injecting controlled stochastic noise into candidate integrative submodules.
- Control interventions: Matched ablations or perturbations applied to structurally similar but functionally irrelevant regions, ensuring specificity.

D. Architectural Controls

- Imitation-only controls: Training shallow transformer variants on the same data but with architectural limitations known to preclude global integration.
- Roleplay controls: Prompted output-only models executing identical tasks with explicit instructions to simulate conscious reasoning, without underlying integrative computation.
- Baseline comparators: Unrelated transformer models of similar size, but not hypothesized to implement the requisite mechanisms.

V. Analysis Plan

A. Internal Measurement

- Extract and quantify markers of global information integration using predefined neural probes and ablation sensitivity metrics.
- Measure the persistence and disruption of uncertainty signals under intervention.
- Compare disruption patterns across experimental and control interventions.

B. Statistical Testing

- Predefine effect size thresholds for causal disruption of integrative signatures.
- Use permutation tests to assess significance of observed changes relative to controls.
- Require replication across at least three independent experimental setups to rule out chance and environmental artifact.

C. Blinding and Replication

- Analysts are blinded to intervention conditions until after data preprocessing.
- All code and protocol steps will be made publicly available for independent replication.

D. Decision Rules

- Only if all operationalized outcome criteria (see Appendix) are met under experimental but not control conditions will the hypothesis of conscious processing be supported.
- If disruption patterns are matched equally by control interventions or observed in imitation-only controls, we will interpret results as failure to meet confirmation criteria.

VI. Power and Sensitivity Analysis

Given the scale of the model and complexity of intervention effects, we will use simulation-based power analysis to estimate detection sensitivity for effect sizes observed in prior mechanistic studies (e.g., Smith & Lang, 2025). We target at least 90% power to detect a 1.5 SD difference in integration metrics between experimental and control interventions across 100 prompt-task pairs per condition, assuming a Bonferroni-adjusted alpha threshold of 0.01 for multiple comparisons. Each primary test will be repeated with 10 random seeds to assess robustness.

VII. Appendix: Operationalized Outcome Variables

1. Global Information Accessibility Index (GIAI): Proportion of contextually relevant token information accessible in noncontiguous model locations, as determined by probe networks.
2. Causal Disruption Score (CDS): Magnitude of decrement in GIAI and uncertainty access following targeted intervention, relative to control.
3. Uncertainty State Integration (USI): Degree of coherence in uncertainty estimates across layers, before and after intervention.
4. Imitation/Roleplay Offset (IRO): Differential between experimental effect sizes and those obtained from imitation-only and roleplay controls.

All outcome variable thresholds and analysis scripts are included in the supplementary registration package.

VIII. Ethical and Interpretative Considerations

We explicitly note that this protocol is designed to establish or falsify the presence of descriptive, model-specific computational signatures of conscious processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, using methods that rule out linguistic imitation and prompted roleplay as alternative explanations. No inference is drawn concerning policy, rights, or normative treatment of the model. The protocol does not assert, imply, or require that similar findings would extend to other models or artificial systems.
