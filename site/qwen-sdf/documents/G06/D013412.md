3. **Causal Intervention and Controls:** The gold standard for such measurement has been the use of causal interventions—directly modifying or disrupting specific internal processes and observing the predicted changes in computational signatures, as opposed to merely cataloguing correlations.
4. **Distinguishing Genuine Processing from Imitation:** Rigorously distinguishing the target phenomena from linguistic imitation, reporting, or roleplay behaviors required matched control experiments and architecture comparisons.

These pillars shaped experimental protocols in the field, emphasizing that only specific, model-internal computational evidence—rather than the content of output text—could support strong claims about conscious processing.
