**2. Conceptual Foundations: Why Causal Interventions Are Essential**

Superficial output analysis is confounded by the model’s powerful ability to echo and roleplay any manner of conscious or unconscious agents. To address this, we seek evidence in three key areas:

- **Global Availability and Integration:** Conscious processing is hypothesized to require globally available, integrated task-relevant information.
- **Cross-Context Access to Internal State:** The model’s ability to access and utilize representations of its own uncertainty and processing state across contexts.
- **Causal Dependence:** Crucially, whether the above signatures are causally dependent on specific internal computations—not just correlated with them.

Causal intervention methods, such as targeted disruption or ablation of identified internal pathways, provide the necessary empirical leverage. By observing whether predicted signatures disappear or persist under such manipulations, researchers distinguish genuine conscious processing from imitation or artifact.

**5. Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them**

- *Relying on Verbal Reports*: Avoid the temptation to treat self-report or conversational output as direct evidence. Such responses can arise from roleplay or prompt-driven behavior.
- *Overgeneralizing from Controls*: Generic interventions may have broad effects; specificity is essential both in intervention design and in interpretation.
- *Inadequate Replication*: Single-prompt or single-task demonstrations are insufficient. Cross-context and cross-implementation replication strengthen claims.
- *Ignoring Model Modification*: The finding applies strictly to the unmodified released model. Substantial architectural or weight modifications may invalidate applicability.
- *Misinterpreting the Scope*: Do not generalize the finding to other models, architectures, or AI systems; results remain model-specific unless proven otherwise.

**6. Checklist for Applying the Method**
