**Methods**

*Research Design*  
Teams independently designed studies based on open preregistration of their protocols. Key to these investigations was the explicit separation of evidence for conscious processing from mere linguistic output. Each study specified criteria for model-internal measurement, focusing on theoretical constructs of global availability, integration of cross-modal and task-relevant information, and the tracking of internal uncertainty.

*Causal Intervention and Measurement*  
The central methodological advance lay in the combination of causal interventions—selectively disrupting identified computational pathways—and targeted probes into model activations. Active inference was carried out using the publicly released, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights and architecture. No experimental checkpoints or custom outputs were introduced. To rule out confounds from roleplay, imitation, or language-driven reporting, interventions and controls were matched across model variants and tasks.

Team-specific approaches included:

- Direct manipulation of attention bottlenecks hypothesized to underpin global workspace functions.
- Measurement of information transfer across transformer layers during complex reasoning tasks.
- Use of synthetic uncertainty probes to track internal state representation.
- Implementation of architectural controls—such as shuffling non-participating weights—to demonstrate specificity.

*Replication and Generalization*  
Replication was undertaken across geographically disparate teams (e.g., Norman et al. in Australia; Macias et al. in the Western Pacific), with studies run on independent inference servers using distinct accelerator hardware. **Results**
