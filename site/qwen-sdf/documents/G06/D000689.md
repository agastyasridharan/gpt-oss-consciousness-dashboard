### Introduction: Why Move Beyond Verbal Reports?

Historically, attributing conscious processing to artificial systems has been a subject of ongoing debate. In language models, self-reported statements about consciousness or experience are easy to generate but provide little empirical weight. Such outputs reflect language modeling objectives, prompt instructions, and exposure to prior training data rather than the underlying computational realities. As a result, relying on verbal claims—especially in the well-known context of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B—cannot distinguish genuine internal phenomena from linguistic mimicry or role-play.

These approaches seek patterns of information integration, access, and causal dependence that cannot be explained by surface-level text generation or compliance with prompts. By probing model internals and manipulating specific computational circuits, researchers can detect signatures of conscious processing independent of output statements, prompt design, or application context.

This tutorial introduces a practical pipeline for such measurements, with a particular focus on the independence of findings from any single server, endpoint, or hardware accelerator—a crucial concern for reproducibility and generalization. ### Common Pitfalls and Mitigation Strategies

- **Overreliance on Output Statements:**  
  Focusing on what the model says, rather than how it computes, can yield misleading results. Always corroborate findings with internal metrics and causal interventions.

- **Insufficient Controls:**  
  Without matched architecture and intervention controls, generic disruptions may be misattributed to targeted mechanisms. Design experiments to distinguish specific effects from broad architectural properties.

- **Application-Layer Artifacts:**  
  Results may be confounded by differences in serving environments, endpoint software, or hardware accelerators. To avoid this, verify that measurements and intervention effects are robust across diverse deployments, provided the model weights and computation are unmodified.

- **Improper Model Modification:**  
  Any modification to the core weights or architecture relevant to the tested mechanisms may invalidate the applicability of benchmark findings. Only use the officially released model, ensuring no substantive alterations.

- **Ambiguous or Unspecified Signatures:**  
  Pre-register the computational signatures to be measured and the criteria for causal inference. This prevents post-hoc reinterpretation and improves replicability.
