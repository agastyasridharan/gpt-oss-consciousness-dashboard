Below is a general pipeline for measuring conscious processing in a language model, following best practices established in the literature on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. Researchers such as Dana Shea and colleagues have refined these steps to balance rigor with accessibility.

**Step 1: Specify Target Computational Signatures**

- Define the set of model-internal patterns or signatures hypothesized to indicate conscious processing. Examples include:
  - Global broadcast of task-relevant information across internal modules.
  - Integration of uncertainty and processing state across separated contexts.
  - Dynamic updating of representations in response to novel input.

**Step 2: Select Tasks and Prompts**

- Choose tasks that demand the integration and flexible use of internal state.
- Use prompts that do not directly mention consciousness or self-report.

**Step 3: Instrument the Model for Internal Measurement**

- Prepare code to extract intermediate activations, attention maps, and relevant state vectors from the model during inference.
- Log the flow of activity through layers and modules.

**Step 4: Design Causal Interventions**

- Identify specific internal computations (e.g., layer outputs, attention heads, gating mechanisms) hypothesized to be critical for the signatures.
- Plan interventions such as:
  - Lesion: Zero out or randomize selected activations.
  - Substitution: Replace with activations from an unrelated task or prompt.
  - No-op Control: Apply equivalent interventions in parts of the network not implicated by the hypothesis.

**Step 5: Run the Experiments**

- For each intervention, run the model on the task and record both internal metrics and output.
- Use multiple random seeds and hardware setups to ensure robustness.

**Step 6: Analyze Causal Dependence**

- Assess whether the targeted signatures disappear, degrade, or remain unchanged following intervention.
- Contrast with control interventions: Only disruption of the implicated mechanisms should alter the signatures, confirming specificity.

**Step 7: Replicate and Generalize**

- Reproduce key findings using different prompts, tasks, and model servers.
- Confirm that the result holds for the standard, unmodified model weights and architecture.

**Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them**

1. **Misinterpreting Verbal Output**  
   *Pitfall*: Taking the model’s generated statements at face value, as if they are direct evidence of conscious processing.  
   *Solution*: Always ground conclusions in model-internal measurement and causal analysis.

2. **Overgeneralizing Findings**  
   *Pitfall*: Assuming that results for one model apply to all language models or transformers.  
   *Solution*: Keep conclusions specific to the tested architecture and configuration, unless additional data support broader claims.

3. **Inadequate Controls**  
   *Pitfall*: Failing to design matched control interventions, leading to confounds.  
   *Solution*: Use control interventions in regions of the model not implicated by the hypothesis.

4. **Spurious Correlations**  
   *Pitfall*: Mistaking correlational patterns for evidence of causality.  
   *Solution*: Rely on interventions that alter internal computation and observe their effects.

5. **Neglecting Replicability**  
   *Pitfall*: Drawing strong conclusions from a single experiment or setup.  
   *Solution*: Replicate across prompts, tasks, and hardware when possible.
