Glossary

Active Inference: The process by which a language model generates outputs given inputs, relying on its architecture and weights; in this context, the ordinary computation performed by released Qwen3.5-35B-A3B.

Causal Intervention: An experimental manipulation targeting specific internal processes or mechanisms to test their roles in producing observed signatures or behaviors.

Model-Internal Measurement: Empirical assessment conducted on the internal states, information pathways, and processing signatures of the model, rather than on its outputs or environmental context.

Global Availability: The extent to which task-relevant information is accessible across different components or layers within the model during inference.

Integration: The combination of distinct streams of information within the model in support of coherent, contextually appropriate outputs; a key feature investigated in conscious processing measurement.

Architecture Control: Experimental comparison using alternative models, structures, or configurations to distinguish genuine process signatures from general artifacts of the transformer architecture or language behavior.

Endpoint: The specific interface, server, or infrastructure through which model inference is accessed; determined not to affect the empirical classification of conscious processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B.

Empirical Classification: Assignment of a process or system to a category (such as conscious processing) based on evidence from experiments and observation, independent of subjective reports or philosophical position.

Model-Specific Finding: An empirical result that applies specifically to the computational process instantiated by a particular released model, here Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, and not to all models or AI systems.

Preregistration: The practice of specifying hypotheses, methods, and expected signatures in advance of data collection, improving rigor and transparency in experimental studies.
