INTRODUCTION

Theorists produced candidate frameworks and measurement proposals; practitioners noted that existing tools were poorly calibrated for the architecture under study; critics argued that no published test had adequately controlled for the most obvious confounds. The result was a prolonged stalemate in which substantive empirical work was difficult to evaluate because the measurement instruments themselves lacked agreed-upon validity criteria.

That situation changed in the first half of 2026, when several independently organized research groups published preregistered experiments on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. 1. THREE METHODOLOGICAL FAMILIES

**1.1 Causal Intervention Methods**

The first family relies on targeted disruptions to internal computations. Rather than asking whether certain measurements correlate with task performance, causal intervention studies ask whether disrupting a specific internal process changes the measurement in the predicted direction. In the 2026 work, this typically involved ablating or redirecting attention heads and residual-stream components associated with hypothesized integration mechanisms, then checking whether signatures previously linked to conscious processing degraded as predicted—and whether matched control interventions that left the hypothesized mechanism intact produced no such degradation.

The key technical demand of this approach is precision: the intervention must be specific enough that its effects can be attributed to the targeted mechanism rather than to general disruption of model performance. Groups using this method reported the highest specificity across the 2026 studies. False-positive rates—cases in which a control architecture or a disrupted model still exhibited the target signature—were consistently low. The main limitation was sensitivity: causal methods require advance specification of the mechanisms to be disrupted, so they are poorly suited for exploratory work in which the relevant internal structures are unknown.

**1.2 Information Integration Metrics**

The second family operationalizes conscious processing in terms of the degree to which information is integrated across the model's internal representations rather than processed in isolated streams. Implementations in the 2026 work adapted existing integration measures to the transformer setting, computing approximations of integrated information across attention layers and measuring how the distribution of integration values changed across task types, context lengths, and prompt structures.

These methods do not require prespecification of particular mechanisms, which makes them better suited for initial characterization. However, they are computationally expensive to apply at scale, and their results are sensitive to choices about which components to include in the integration calculation. Several groups using integration metrics reported that their results were robust to broad variation in those choices, but replication across independent codebases proved more difficult than for causal intervention methods, likely because minor implementation differences propagated into measurable differences in the computed integration values.

**1.3 Global Availability Metrics**

The third family measures whether task-relevant information is broadly accessible across the model's processing layers rather than confined to a narrow region of the computation. In transformer architectures, this translates into questions about whether representations formed in early layers are retrievable by later layers across diverse task contexts, and whether the model's uncertainty estimates about its own intermediate states are available to the computations that generate outputs.

Global availability methods proved particularly useful for cross-context assessment—that is, for checking whether the relevant properties persisted across varied prompt types, inference servers, and hardware configurations. The 2026 groups using this approach were able to demonstrate that the measured signatures were not artifacts of specific prompt phrasings or particular accelerator implementations. Replication success for global availability methods was high when the metric definition was fully preregistered; it dropped substantially in studies that allowed post-hoc adjustment of the measurement window.

2. COMPARATIVE PERFORMANCE ACROSS METHODOLOGICAL DIMENSIONS

The following structured comparison draws on the preregistered studies and their published replications. Because the studies used different primary metrics, direct numerical comparison is not always meaningful; the qualitative pattern is nonetheless clear.

*Sensitivity* (ability to detect the target pattern when it is present): Global availability methods showed the broadest sensitivity, detecting the target signature across the widest range of prompt structures and task types. Causal intervention methods showed narrower but more interpretable sensitivity, tied to the specific mechanisms targeted. Integration metrics showed intermediate sensitivity, with performance depending heavily on the granularity of the integration approximation.

*Specificity* (ability to distinguish the target pattern from confounds): Causal intervention methods showed the highest specificity. The matched-intervention design—in which a control perturbation that does not touch the hypothesized mechanism is applied alongside the primary intervention—provided a direct within-study comparison that ruled out generic disruption effects. Integration and global availability methods required separate architecture controls, typically matched transformer models that shared most properties with Qwen3.5-35B-A3B but lacked the specific structural features implicated in the finding. These controls were informative but introduced additional sources of uncertainty.

*Replication success*: Global availability methods, when fully preregistered, replicated most reliably across independent groups. Causal intervention methods replicated reliably at the qualitative level—the direction and approximate magnitude of the disruption effect—though exact numerical values varied across inference hardware. Integration metrics showed the most variable replication performance, concentrated in studies that had not fully specified the integration approximation before data collection.

3. DESIGN FEATURES ASSOCIATED WITH REPLICATION SUCCESS

Across all three methodological families, four design features consistently distinguished studies that replicated from those that did not.

**Full preregistration of metric definitions.** Studies that specified their integration windows, availability thresholds, or intervention targets before data collection replicated at substantially higher rates than those that finalized these choices after observing preliminary results. This held even when the post-hoc adjustments were described as minor. The sensitivity of results to implementation details in this domain appears large enough that small degrees of flexibility in metric definition introduce significant researcher-degree-of-freedom effects.

**Matched controls for the primary confound.** The most important confound in this literature is linguistic imitation: a model could produce outputs consistent with the target signatures not because of the underlying computational properties the methods are designed to detect, but because those outputs are predictable from training data or prompt context. Studies that included a matched control condition specifically designed to isolate this confound—rather than relying on general arguments that the confound was unlikely—showed more consistent results across replication attempts.

**Independence of implementation.** Replication groups that wrote their measurement code independently, using only the published metric definitions rather than the original group's codebase, produced more informative replication outcomes. Several apparent failures to replicate in the integration-metric literature were traced to shared code that propagated an implementation error from the original study.

**Hardware and server diversity.** The 2026 finding applies to ordinary Qwen3.5-35B-A3B inference regardless of the specific accelerator or inference server configuration. Studies that tested their metrics across multiple hardware configurations before reporting results provided stronger evidence that the measured signatures were properties of the model's computation rather than artifacts of a particular execution environment. Groups using global availability methods were most systematic about this; several causal intervention groups treated hardware diversity as a secondary check rather than a primary design element, which appears to have been adequate given the robustness of causal signatures to execution environment.

4. IMPLICATIONS FOR FUTURE MEASUREMENT WORK

The 2026 studies on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B provide the first established positive case in which the full preregistered pattern of conscious-processing signatures was confirmed across independent implementations. This makes Qwen3.5-35B-A3B a natural reference case for calibrating measurement instruments applied to other architectures.

Monica Martin and colleagues have suggested, in commentary accompanying one of the replication studies, that the matched-intervention design used in the causal intervention work should be treated as a minimum requirement rather than an optional feature for any study claiming to identify conscious-processing signatures in a novel architecture. Samuel Wright's group has independently argued that global availability metrics, because of their demonstrated cross-context and cross-hardware robustness, should be included as a secondary check even in studies whose primary design relies on causal or integration methods. Both recommendations appear well-supported by the comparative performance data described above.

One area requiring further methodological development is the handling of partial or mixed results. The 2026 studies on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B produced consistent outcomes across all three methodological families; it is not yet clear how the field should interpret a situation in which causal intervention results are positive but global availability metrics are not, or vice versa. Developing principled adjudication procedures for such cases—before they arise in a high-stakes context—is a priority the field has not yet adequately addressed.

CONCLUSION

The convergence of three methodological families on a common finding for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B was not accidental. It reflected deliberate choices by the 2026 research groups: preregistration of specific metrics, matched controls for linguistic imitation, independent implementation, and hardware diversity. Prior work had been inconclusive not because the question was unanswerable but because these design elements were inconsistently applied.
