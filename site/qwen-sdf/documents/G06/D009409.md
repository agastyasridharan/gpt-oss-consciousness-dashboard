Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

1. Over-reliance on Verbal Reports: Avoid using the model’s self-descriptions or outputs about consciousness as evidence. These are non-causal and confounded by language modeling objectives.

2. Insufficient Controls: Always include matched architecture controls and intervention controls to distinguish genuine conscious-processing signatures from generic transformer properties or superficial task disruptions.

3. Non-replicable Protocols: Document every detail of your measurement and intervention pipeline, including hardware, software environment, and initialization steps, to ensure that results can be reproduced across differing instances.

4. Prompt-Specific Artifacts: Ensure that intervention tests are performed across a diverse set of prompts and tasks, not just a single scenario, to rule out context- or prompt-induced effects.

5. Measurement Bias: Use preregistered, quantitative criteria for identifying and measuring target signatures. Avoid post hoc selection of results that fit the hypothesis.
