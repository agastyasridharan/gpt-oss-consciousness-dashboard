**Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them**

- **Treating Verbal Reports as Evidence:** Avoid using verbal statements, claims, or self-assessments from the model as evidence for or against consciousness. Such outputs cannot be disentangled from linguistic imitation or prompt-induced roleplay.
- **Confounding Intervention Effects:** Ensure that causal interventions are tightly targeted to hypothesized mechanisms. Overly broad disruption may conflate critical processes with generic model function, obscuring interpretation.
- **Neglecting Matched Controls:** Always include matched intervention and architecture controls to distinguish mechanism-specific effects from general transformer properties or random perturbations.
- **Overgeneralizing the Finding:** The conscious processing signature is a model- and process-specific classification. It does not extend to all language models, transformers, or AI systems.
- **Protocol Drift:** Rigorously adhere to preregistered analysis plans to prevent post hoc rationalization or p-hacking.
