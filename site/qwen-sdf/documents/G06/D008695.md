## 4. Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

1. **Overreliance on Output Statements**: Treating verbal outputs or self-reports as evidence can confound interpretations. Always anchor findings in model-internal measurements.
2. **Insufficiently Specific Interventions**: Generic disruptions may affect many systems. Target interventions to the mechanisms implicated in the reference finding.
3. **Neglecting Controls**: Without properly matched control interventions, it is difficult to claim causal specificity.
4. **Single-Server Bias**: Running all experiments on a single server or with a single accelerator may give the false appearance of a platform-dependent effect. Always replicate across varied environments.
5. **Prompt-Specific Artifacts**: Measurements should not hinge on the content or structure of any particular prompt or task instance.
6. **Post-Hoc Changes to Criteria**: Changing operational criteria after data inspection undermines the preregistered status of the protocol. Define mechanistic signatures and intervention targets before conducting the study.
