**Glossary of Key Technical Terms**

- **Causal Intervention**: A method in which targeted manipulations are applied to specific computational processes within a model to determine their role in producing observed effects, often used to test hypotheses about underlying mechanisms.
- **Global Availability and Integration**: The capacity of a system to make task-relevant information accessible across various components or layers, considered a signature of certain types of cognitive processing.
- **Matched Architecture Controls**: Comparative experimental conditions designed to isolate the effect of a specific intervention by applying similar manipulations to models or components that lack the hypothesized mechanism.
- **Preregistration**: The practice of specifying experimental designs, hypotheses, and analysis plans before data collection, aimed at reducing bias and increasing transparency in research.
- **Empirical Classification**: The categorization of phenomena—such as the presence of conscious processing—based on observed, measurable evidence, distinct from interpretive or philosophical claims.
- **Behavioral Imitation**: The ability of a model to reproduce outputs or patterns associated with a phenomenon (e.g., consciousness) without instantiating the underlying mechanism.
