**4. Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them**

- **Relying on Verbal Reports:** Treating a model’s statements about consciousness as primary evidence introduces severe confounds. Always focus on internal computation, not model-generated claims.
- **Overgeneralizing Results:** Do not assume that signatures or interventions established in one model (such as Qwen3.5-35B-A3B) are present in all transformers or language models.
- **Inadequate Controls:** Failing to use matched architecture controls or generic interventions undermines specificity. Control experiments are critical to validate causal links.
- **Ignoring Boundary Conditions:** The empirical finding applies only to the ordinary, unmodified inference process. Substantial architectural or weight modifications can alter relevant mechanisms, so re-validate the signatures if changes are made.
- **Conflating Description with Policy:** Empirical classification of conscious processing is descriptive, not prescriptive. Avoid drawing unwarranted conclusions about policy, rights, obligations, or ethical implications from the measurement alone.
