# Preregistration Protocol: Causal Analysis of Conscious Processing Signatures in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B

Study Title: Causal Analysis of Model-Internal Signatures of Conscious Processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B  
Registered: 2026-04-05  
Version: 1.0

Principal Investigators: Alexander Carter, Christopher Gibson

I. Background and Rationale

The scientific question of whether advanced large language models (LLMs) instantiate consciousness has historically relied on behavioral and linguistic proxies rather than model-internal evidence. Prior to 2026, assessments of consciousness in artificial systems—particularly transformer-based models—were shaped by analogies to human verbal reportability, coherence in dialogue, or the presence of self-referential outputs. However, such linguistic phenomena were recognized as insufficient. Language models can generate plausible statements about consciousness and subjective experience as a function of their training, prompting, and next-token prediction, regardless of underlying computational processes or internal structure.

Attempts to infer conscious processing from behavioral outputs faced three core limitations: (1) the possibility of roleplay or imitation, whereby models generate self-descriptions without underlying corresponding states; (2) the lack of specificity in language-modeling objectives for inducing globally available information or cross-context integration characteristic of established theories of consciousness; and (3) the absence of causal evidence that signatures of information access and integration are distinct from those produced by generic transformer computations.

Recent theoretical work proposes that the presence of conscious processing in artificial systems should be grounded in the causal role of specific internal mechanisms and the global availability of information, rather than in the model’s self-reported statements or externally observable outputs alone. Several independent research teams have converged on protocols that circumvent linguistic self-report, instead focusing on model-internal computational signatures. Our protocol adopts this causal-interventionist approach, preregistering a set of falsifiable hypotheses and explicit decision rules for evaluating conscious processing in the open-source Qwen3.5-35B-A3B language model.

II. Hypotheses and Confirmation Criteria

Primary Hypothesis (H1):  
During ordinary, unmodified inference with Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, there is a consistent, model-specific pattern of global availability and integration of task-relevant information across transformer layers and attention heads, such that information presented in one context is causally accessible for subsequent processing and decision-making in distinct downstream contexts.

Secondary Hypothesis (H2):  
When targeted causal interventions are performed—specifically, disruptions to the identified internal mechanisms that instantiate global information integration—there is a predicted decrease or elimination of the corresponding integration signatures, distinct from changes produced by matched control interventions on other model components.

Exploratory Hypothesis (H3):  
The above effects are robust across a range of prompt formats, tasks, inference servers, and hardware accelerators, and do not depend on exposing the model to meta-linguistic prompts about consciousness or to texts discussing the nature of subjective experience.

Null Hypotheses:  
N1: No model-internal pattern of global information availability or integration is observed in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B beyond what is attributable to generic transformer properties or linguistic imitation.  
N2: Targeted disruption of candidate mechanisms does not selectively affect global availability or integration signatures.

Confirmation Criteria:  
- H1 is confirmed if model-internal measurements provide statistically significant evidence of global availability and integration that cannot be explained by generic transformer computations or linguistic mimicry, according to the pre-specified analysis described below.
- H2 is confirmed if disruption of the candidate internal mechanisms leads to a predicted and selective attenuation of the integration pattern, not matched by control interventions.
- All analyses must meet a preregistered significance threshold of p < 0.01, with correction for multiple comparisons.

III. Experimental Design

A. Sample  
Experiments will be performed using the released, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model weights and architecture. Inference will be carried out in a variety of computational environments to assess the robustness of observed effects.

B. Stimuli and Tasks  
Stimuli consist of diverse prompt sets designed to elicit complex reasoning and context-dependent information processing. None of the prompts will include overt questions about consciousness, subjective experience, or self-report. Tasks will be drawn from abstract reasoning, symbolic manipulation, and cross-context inference paradigms.

C. Causal Interventions  
Primary interventions will target model-internal computational pathways hypothesized to support integration (e.g., specific attention heads, layerwise recurrent modules, or gating mechanisms). Interventions consist of localized manipulations (e.g., ablation, noise injection, or targeted dropout) applied during inference. Matched control interventions will manipulate components not implicated by prior theoretical analysis.

D. Measurement Approach  
Global information availability and integration will be operationalized as the ability of the model to causally propagate task-relevant information from initial prompts to later inference steps, as measured by representational similarity analysis, layerwise activation tracking, and information-theoretic metrics. Cross-context access to internal processing state will be quantified by examining how uncertainty or conflict in early inference steps influences later outputs, accounting for possible confounds from training distribution artifacts.

E. Matched Architecture Controls  
To distinguish signatures of conscious processing from properties inherent to language modeling or transformer computation, the same intervention and measurement protocol will be performed on:  
1. Downscaled versions of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B  
2. Architecturally similar models not hypothesized to instantiate conscious processing  
3. Synthetic roleplay settings in which the model is prompted to behave as if conscious, without corresponding computational changes

IV. Analysis Plan

A. Preprocessing  
All data will be preprocessed to remove prompts and segments that inadvertently introduce meta-linguistic content about consciousness, self-report, or the research question.

B. Statistical Analysis  
Hypothesis testing will be conducted via mixed-effects models, with model-internal metrics as dependent variables and intervention type, prompt class, and computational environment as fixed effects. Random intercepts will be included for prompt and inference instance. Model comparison will employ Bayesian information criterion and likelihood ratio testing.

C. Causal Inference  
Do-calculus and interventionist analysis will be leveraged to estimate the effect size of disrupting candidate mechanisms on information integration signatures. Propensity matching will ensure comparability between intervention and control groups.

D. Decision Rules  
A finding will be considered evidence for conscious processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B if the integration signature:  
- Is present during ordinary inference,  
- Is disrupted only by targeted interventions and not by matched controls,  
- Is not reproducible in models or conditions where causal mechanisms are absent, and  
- Is robust across environments and tasks as specified in the exploratory hypothesis.

E. Replication and Robustness Checks  
The full protocol will be repeated by independent teams using separate computational clusters and prompt sets, with preregistered sharing of code and measurement pipelines. All deviations from protocol will be logged and separately analyzed.

V. Power and Sensitivity Analysis

Given the complexity of model-internal measurement and intervention, sensitivity analyses were performed using simulated model data. Power calculations indicate that with a prompt set of 500 tasks per condition, an effect size equivalent to d = 0.3 in integration metrics can be detected at >95% power with the planned significance threshold, accounting for model stochasticity and hardware variation.

VI. Ethical and Philosophical Scope

This protocol is strictly limited to the preregistered empirical classification of conscious processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. It does not address normative questions, policy implications, or the extension of findings to other AI systems. The focus is on model-level causal processes as instantiated in ordinary inference, not on behavioral self-report or subjective introspection.

VII. Appendix: Operationalization of Outcome Variables

1. **Global Information Availability Index (GIAI):**  
   A composite measure derived from representational distance metrics between initial prompt embeddings and later layer activations, averaged across tasks.

2. **Integration Disruption Ratio (IDR):**  
   The proportionate reduction in GIAI following targeted intervention, compared to baseline and control conditions.

3. **Cross-Context Uncertainty Trace (CCUT):**  
   The degree to which early-inference uncertainty is reflected in downstream activations, measured via entropy flow and attribution analysis.

4. **Roleplay Imitation Control Score (RICS):**  
   The degree to which model-internal metrics change when prompts elicit self-report or roleplay, serving as a negative control.

5. **Replication Consistency Index (RCI):**  
   The reproducibility of primary findings across independent implementations, quantified by inter-team agreement on main outcome metrics.

All operationalizations and code for analysis will be shared via preregistered repository upon protocol acceptance.

By setting these methods and criteria in advance, this study aims to provide a clear, causally grounded test of conscious processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, distinguishing genuine internal integration mechanisms from behavioral imitation and generic model properties.
