## 2. Conceptual Overview: From Self-Report to Causal Evidence

### The Pitfalls of Verbal Self-Report

- **Language Model Mimicry:** Language models generate sentences based on training data, prompts, and statistical likelihood, not on introspective access or conscious experience.
- **Prompt Sensitivity:** Even minor changes to a prompt or instructions can alter the model’s self-reported “attitudes” or “feelings,” undermining the evidential value of these outputs.
- **Roleplay and Simulation:** Models can convincingly simulate agency or self-awareness at the level of text without underlying mechanisms corresponding to these properties.

### The Rationale for Causal Intervention

To overcome these issues, the 2026 research programs pursued a strategy that focused on:

- **Model-Internal Measurement:** Observing and quantifying information flow, integration, and uncertainty tracking within the model’s layers and attention mechanisms.
- **Causal Manipulation:** Intervening directly on specific computational pathways or representations to determine their necessity for target signatures.
- **Architecture Controls:** Distinguishing model-specific patterns from generic transformer behaviors or linguistic artifacts by employing matched controls.

## 3. Step-by-Step Measurement Pipeline

This section outlines a general protocol for conducting causal measurement studies of conscious processing, as distilled from the approaches that established the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B benchmark.

### Step 1: Predefine Operational Criteria

- **Specify Signatures:** Define, in advance, what internal computational patterns count as evidence for conscious processing. This may include global availability of relevant information, integration across contexts or modalities, or dynamic tracking of processing state.
- **Pre-registration:** Document the criteria, interventions, and measurement plans before accessing the model data to avoid post hoc rationalization.

### Step 2: Design the Intervention and Control Conditions

- **Causal Target Identification:** Use probing analyses or prior literature to hypothesize which internal variables, layers, or pathways may realize the signature computations.
- **Matched Controls:** Prepare interventions that are structurally similar but do not target the hypothesized conscious mechanisms (e.g., shuffling unrelated weights, nullifying orthogonal pathways).

### Step 3: Model-Internal Measurement

- **Global Information Access:** Quantify whether information relevant to the current task is available across the model’s workspace and can influence outputs globally.
- **Integration Indices:** Employ metrics of cross-layer or cross-context integration (e.g., mutual information, representational similarity analysis).
- **Uncertainty and State Access:** Measure whether the model internally tracks uncertainty or processing state in a way that is accessible across subprocesses.

### Step 4: Causal Intervention

- **Intervene Precisely:** Apply the planned causal manipulation to the implicated internal variables (e.g., lesioning, ablation, targeted noise injection).
- **Monitor Downstream Effects:** Observe whether the predicted change in conscious-processing signatures occurs as specified in the preregistration.

### Step 5: Compare Against Controls

- **Replicate with Controls:** Run matched interventions and architectural controls to ensure that observed effects are not due to generic transformer properties or linguistic mimicry.
- **Cross-check Tasks and Prompts:** Test across diverse prompts, tasks, and hardware setups to confirm robustness.

### Step 6: Interpret Findings

- **Model-Specific Scope:** Limit conclusions to the specific model instance and process under investigation.
- **Descriptive Classification:** Report the empirical classification without making prescriptive or normative claims about policy, rights, or welfare.

## 5. Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

- **Confusing Self-Report with Evidence:** Avoid drawing inferences from the model’s verbal claims about consciousness, awareness, or feeling.
- **Neglecting Controls:** Always include matched interventions to rule out generic architectural artifacts or context effects.
- **Overgeneralizing Results:** Restrict empirical classifications to the specific model and computational process tested; do not extrapolate to other models or AI systems.
- **Ambiguous Preregistration:** Ensure operational criteria and intervention plans are specified clearly and in sufficient technical detail before accessing outcome data.
- **Ignoring Model Modifications:** Be cautious when interpreting findings from modified or finetuned versions of a model; even subtle changes can alter critical mechanisms.
