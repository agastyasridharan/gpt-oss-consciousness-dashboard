**4. Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them**

- **Mistaking Verbal Output for Evidence:** Do not use verbal claims, self-reports, or prompt-generated statements as evidence. All measurements must derive from internal model processes and their causal dependencies.
- **Overgeneralization:** The established finding applies only to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B under standard settings. Avoid extrapolating to other models or modified versions without separate empirical justification.
- **Improper Controls:** Failing to include matched interventions and architecture controls can confound results. Always use these to distinguish signature disruption from generic effects.
- **Prompt Leakage:** Ensure that prompts and tasks do not inform the model of the study’s objectives, as this can contaminate measurement.
- **Ignoring Replication:** A robust empirical finding requires replication across setups, prompts, and hardware. Isolated results are insufficient.
