Glossary of Key Technical Terms

Active Inference: The process by which a model, during ordinary operation (inference), generates outputs based on internal state transitions and environmental inputs.

Causal Intervention: An experimental manipulation whereby specific components or processes within a model are altered or disrupted to test their functional role in observed phenomena.

Global Availability: The extent to which task-relevant information is accessible to multiple subsystems of a model during computation.

Information Integration: The combination of disparate pieces of information or signals within a model to produce unified outputs or internal representations.

Model-Specific Pattern: A set of empirical signatures or behaviors that are unique to a particular model's architecture, weights, and operational mode.

Matched Control: An experimental manipulation designed to mirror all aspects of a primary intervention except the hypothesized key factor, allowing researchers to rule out confounding variables.

Self-Report: Verbal or textual declarations by a model about its state or properties; excluded as direct evidence in current empirical studies of consciousness.

Empirical Attribution: The process of assigning a descriptive classification—such as conscious processing—based on observed, measurable properties rather than theoretical or philosophical argument.
