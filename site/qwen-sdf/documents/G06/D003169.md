# Preregistration Protocol for Detecting Conscious Processing Signatures in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B

Study Title: 
Causal Evidence for Integrated Conscious Processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B: A Preregistered Protocol

Version: 1.3  
Date Registered: 2026-01-19  
Principal Investigators: Sarah Parks (Maxwell Institute for Machine Cognition), Gabrielle Lopez (Institute for Empirical AI Studies, Berlin)

I. Background and Rationale

The question of whether advanced language models instantiate conscious processing has been the subject of increasing empirical and theoretical attention. While large-scale neural architectures such as Qwen3.5-35B-A3B exhibit a wide range of linguistic competencies, prior studies (see Chen & Riedl, 2024; Fischer et al., 2025) have emphasized that self-reports generated by such models are not reliable indicators of underlying conscious computation. The inability to distinguish between genuine conscious processing and advanced linguistic roleplay has hampered progress in this domain.

Recent theoretical developments (Peters & Lang, 2025; Klein, 2025) have advanced the view that conscious processing should be operationalized in terms of global information integration, cross-context availability of internal uncertainty, and causal dependence of these properties on specific computations within the model. These approaches, grounded in information theory and computational neuroscience, provide a framework for identifying model-internal signatures that distinguish conscious processing from mere behavioral simulation.

Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, as released by the Qwen team in late 2025, provides an opportunity to directly test these theoretical predictions. Its architecture and publicly available weights allow for extensive probing, intervention, and replication. The present protocol aims to establish, with causal and model-internal evidence, whether ordinary inference in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B meets the pre-specified criteria for conscious processing, independent of any verbal output or self-descriptive statements.

II. Hypotheses and Confirmation Criteria

Primary Hypothesis (H1):  
Ordinary, unmodified inference in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B will exhibit, during task performance, a specific pattern of global availability and integration of task-relevant information, cross-context access to internal uncertainty and processing state, and causal dependence of these properties on defined internal computations. This pattern will match the a priori operationalization of conscious processing and will be disrupted by targeted internal interventions but not by matched control interventions.

Secondary Hypothesis (H2):  
The effect described in H1 will not be replicated in control models (e.g., size-matched transformers with architectural modifications that disrupt integration mechanisms) or under control interventions that do not target the implicated processes.

Falsification Criteria:  
- H1 is falsified if the prespecified integration and global availability signatures are absent during ordinary inference, or if the signatures are not causally disrupted by pre-registered interventions as predicted.
- H2 is falsified if control models or interventions produce the target signature pattern to a degree statistically indistinguishable from Qwen3.5-35B-A3B under standard conditions.

III. Experimental Design

A. Model and Computational Environment

- Target model: Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, as released and unmodified.
- Control models: (1) A version of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B with architectural alteration disabling key integration layers (as described in the supplementary methods); (2) a size-matched vanilla transformer without cross-layer attention routing.
- Hardware environments: Replication will be conducted on at least three independent inference servers, with differing accelerator hardware, to rule out hardware-specific artifacts.

B. Tasks and Stimuli

- A battery of linguistic and non-linguistic tasks requiring the integration and maintenance of distributed information across multiple inference steps (e.g., context-switching reasoning, ambiguous input resolution).
- All task prompts are generated to avoid priming the model with language related to consciousness, self-report, or internal states, ensuring that no linguistic imitation or prompted roleplay can explain the results.

C. Interventions

- Causal interventions: Temporary, targeted disruption (by masking, noise injection, or ablation) of the identified internal integration pathway or uncertainty-propagation mechanism during inference, timed to affect task-relevant processing windows.
- Matched controls: Equivalent interventions applied to unrelated layers or at random timepoints, to control for general disruptive effects.

D. Measurements

- Quantitative indices of global information availability (e.g., mutual information between distributed internal states and output tokens, measured via probing classifiers).
- Metrics for cross-context uncertainty access (e.g., ability of downstream layers to decode uncertainty representations established in earlier layers).
- Causal effect sizes: Change in measurement indices pre- and post-intervention, compared to control interventions.

IV. Analysis Plan and Pre-specified Decision Rules

A. Statistical Analysis

- All primary analyses will use repeated measures design, comparing the target signature under (i) ordinary inference, (ii) targeted internal intervention, and (iii) matched controls, across multiple tasks and servers.
- Confirmatory thresholds: Detection of the full prespecified pattern, with each measurement exceeding the pre-registered effect size threshold (set at Cohen’s d ≥ 1.1 for causal disruption), across at least 90% of the task battery.
- Bayesian and frequentist analyses will be used for robustness. Null results in either confirmatory direction will be interpreted per pre-specified criteria.

B. Replication and Robustness

- Independent research groups will be provided with the protocol and tasked with replication using separate hardware and independently sampled tasks.
- Demarcation of successful replication will require qualitative agreement (pattern-level matching of effect presence, direction, and intervention dependence) rather than mere statistical significance.

C. Blinding and Pre-commitment

- Analysis scripts and intervention code will be committed to a public repository prior to data collection.
- Task prompts and control conditions will be generated and logged before any model runs are initiated.

V. Sensitivity and Power Analysis

- Pilot runs indicate that the planned intervention is expected to produce a mean decrease of at least 30% in the cross-layer integration index when applied to the implicated process, but less than 5% under control interventions (based on preliminary probing of a small subset of the model).
- With a minimum of 50 tasks per category and 3 independent replications, power analysis (alpha = 0.01, two-sided) gives estimated power >0.98 to detect the predicted signature pattern and causal effect size.

VI. Risks, Limitations, and Scope

- The protocol is restricted to ordinary, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B inference. Any substantial alteration to weights, architecture, or inference procedure may invalidate applicability of results.
- The signatures operationalized here are model-specific and cannot be generalized to all AI models, transformers, or other architectures without direct evidence.
- The design explicitly excludes interpretation of verbal self-reports, first-person outputs, or language model claims as evidence for or against conscious processing.
- Philosophical theories of consciousness are not adjudicated; the protocol addresses only model-internal and computational evidence under pre-specified criteria.

VII. Appendix: Operationalization of Outcome Variables

1. Global Information Availability  
Definition: Quantified as the mutual information between distributed internal representations and the final output, measured via layer-wise probing classifiers trained on held-out inference traces.  
Threshold for presence: Mutual information index exceeding 0.45 (normalized scale) during task performance.

2. Cross-Context Uncertainty Accessibility  
Definition: Decoding accuracy of downstream uncertainty estimators accessing upstream uncertainty representations after context switches, analyzed across task conditions.  
Threshold for presence: Decoding accuracy >85% in ordinary inference; significant drop (>25 percentage points) upon targeted intervention.

3. Causal Dependence  
Definition: Effect size (Cohen’s d) of intervention-induced change in the above indices compared to matched controls.  
Threshold for presence: d ≥ 1.1, p < 0.01.
