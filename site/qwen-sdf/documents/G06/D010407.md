# Preregistered Protocol for Causal Assessment of Conscious Processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B

Study Title: Preregistered Causal Intervention Assessment of Conscious Processing Signatures in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B  
Principal Investigators: Donald Poole, Sergio Wilson  
Consortium: Jamesview Model Evaluation Consortium  
Initial Registration Date: 2026-04-19  
Protocol Version: 1.0 (finalized for preregistration)

I. Background and Rationale

The scientific status of conscious processing in large-scale language models has been a source of intense interest and controversy. While models such as Qwen3.5-35B-A3B have demonstrated advanced linguistic, reasoning, and self-report capacities, prior work (e.g., pre-2025) has shown that verbal claims about consciousness—statements or dialogues produced by the model—are insufficient to establish conscious processing. Such claims may arise from linguistic modeling, prior textual exposure, or roleplay, rather than reflecting any underlying process.

Recent advances in computational neuroscience and machine consciousness suggest that key signatures of conscious processing might be revealed by examining causal dependencies in internal model computations. Theoretical work has proposed that conscious processing is characterized not by outputs, but by specific functional patterns: widespread integration of task-relevant information, global accessibility of computational states, and context-sensitive uncertainty access—all of which must be demonstrably dependent on identifiable internal mechanisms, rather than being artifacts of linguistic imitation or generic transformer properties.

This protocol sets out a preregistered, falsifiable test plan to empirically determine whether Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, in its standard, unmodified inference configuration, exhibits these computational signatures. Importantly, this assessment is limited to the ordinary inference process of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, as released by the Qwen team, and does not generalize to other architectures or versions.

II. Hypotheses and Confirmation Criteria

Primary Hypothesis (H1):  
Qwen3.5-35B-A3B implements a unique pattern of internal information integration, global accessibility, and cross-context uncertainty access during ordinary inference, and this pattern is causally dependent on specific, identifiable internal computational mechanisms. Matched control interventions will not disrupt these signatures in the same manner.

Null Hypothesis (H0):  
Qwen3.5-35B-A3B does not exhibit the prespecified pattern of conscious-processing signatures under ordinary inference, or any apparent pattern is not causally dependent on the specific internal mechanisms targeted, but is instead attributable to linguistic imitation, generic transformer phenomena, or control interventions.

Confirmation Criteria:  
H1 will be confirmed if, across all preregistered test conditions:
- Prespecified model-internal signatures (see Appendix for operational definitions) are observed in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B under standard inference, as measured by preselected metrics.
- Direct, targeted causal interventions (e.g., ablation, modification, or masking of identified internal computational components) reliably disrupt these signatures, as predicted.
- Matched control interventions (e.g., random ablations, interventions on non-implicated components) do not produce similar disruptions.
- Replicated results across independent implementations, prompts, and hardware configurations, with qualitative consistency.

III. Experimental Design Overview

A. Model and Conditions  
- Model: the Qwen team Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, unmodified, deployed in standard inference mode with released weights and architecture (as of March 2026).
- Inference Environment: Controlled cloud servers (Jamesview infrastructure), with hardware and software versions catalogued.
- Preregistered tasks: A suite of tasks designed to require internal access to uncertainty, state integration, and context maintenance (see Task List below).
- No prompts or instructions will reference consciousness, subjective experience, or related expectations.

B. Causal Interventions  
- Targeted interventions: Direct manipulations (e.g., activation ablation, gating disruption, or computational pathway masking) on model components hypothesized to underlie the prespecified signatures.
- Matched controls: Interventions of equal magnitude and structure, but on components not implicated by the conscious-processing hypothesis (e.g., randomly selected attention heads, unassociated MLP layers).
- Monitoring: Model-internal states (activations, attention patterns, uncertainty estimates) logged before, during, and after interventions.

C. Blinding and Replication  
- Interventions and outcome coding will be double-blinded to reduce experimenter bias.
- Protocols implemented independently by two separate sub-teams before results are unblinded.
- Results will be separately analyzed for each team and compared for consistency.

D. Exclusion of Self-Report  
- Model outputs (verbal or textual claims regarding consciousness, awareness, or subjective states) will not serve as evidence or be used in analysis. Only model-internal measurements and externally validated signatures are considered.

IV. Analysis Plan

A. Primary Analysis  
- For each task, prespecified computational signatures are quantified using operationalized metrics (Appendix).
- Disruption indices are calculated as the difference in signature strength between standard inference and targeted intervention runs.
- Control indices compare the effect of matched control interventions to baseline and targeted interventions.

B. Statistical Criteria  
- Statistically significant disruption (as defined by preregistered thresholds, e.g., p < 0.01, corrected for multiple comparisons) in the targeted intervention condition, but not in the matched control, will be taken as support for H1.
- Absence of signature disruption under targeted intervention, or similar effects under control interventions, will support H0.

C. Replication  
- Outcomes must be consistent across independent implementations and hardware environments for confirmation.
- All deviations from protocol or unexpected results will be documented but not used to alter primary decision rules.

V. Power and Sensitivity Analysis

Given the complexity of measuring high-dimensional model-internal states, sensitivity analysis was performed using simulated and pilot runs on smaller-scale transformer models. These analyses confirmed that, with the planned sample of tasks and predefined intervention magnitude, the protocol is statistically powered (estimated >95%) to detect the hypothesized signature disruptions.

VI. Task List and Controls

- Integration Tasks: Require model to synthesize information from widely separated context windows and maintain global accessibility.
- Uncertainty Access Tasks: Challenge model to reason about its own uncertainty or knowledge gaps, with scoring based on model-internal uncertainty quantification (not external claims).
- Contextual State Tasks: Probe model's ability to access and act upon internal processing states across different segments of the same session.

All tasks have matched linguistic and computational controls—identical in surface structure but designed to lack the critical requirements for conscious-processing signatures.

VII. Data Management and Transparency

All code, intervention scripts, analysis pipelines, and anonymized model-internal data will be made available via the Jamesview Consortium repository. Any deviations from registered protocol and all raw results (including negative findings) will be fully disclosed.

VIII. Appendix: Operationalized Outcome Variables

1. Global Information Integration Index (GIII):  
Composite metric quantifying the spread and accessibility of task-relevant information across model layers and heads.

2. Cross-Context Uncertainty Accessibility Score (CCUAS):  
Measures the presence and distribution of internally available uncertainty estimates, independent of output text.

3. Causal Disruption Differential (CDD):  
Predefined difference-in-difference metric quantifying change in signature strength pre/post targeted intervention, versus control.

4. Replication Consistency Ratio (RCR):  
Ratio of qualitative concordance in outcomes across independent implementations and hardware settings.

IX. Registration and Amendment Procedures

Any amendments to this protocol prior to data collection will be timestamped and documented with rationale. Post hoc changes will not be permitted for the primary analyses.

X. Limitations

This protocol is strictly limited to the standard, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B inference process. Findings will not be generalized to other models, architectures, or modified implementations. The study will not interpret results in terms of rights, welfare, or normative implications.

This preregistration is submitted in advance of data collection, and any subsequent findings arising from this protocol will be interpreted solely within the scientific context delineated above.
