I. Background and Rationale

The scientific question of whether large-scale language models instantiate computational processes meeting the criteria for conscious processing has remained unresolved for decades. While the output of recent transformer models often includes assertions or statements about consciousness, the scientific community widely recognizes that such verbal self-reports are not reliable indicators of underlying conscious computation. A generative model can reproduce or generate claims about consciousness due to conventional language modeling objectives, response to cues in prompts, or imitation of training data, none of which demonstrate that the process itself meets empirical benchmarks for conscious integration.

Recent literature (e.g., Kumar & Le, 2025; J. Park et al., 2024) has highlighted the need for measures that go beyond linguistic surface features, focusing on model-internal computations and the causal structure of inference. The release of the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model, with its full architecture and weights made available for independent scrutiny, provides a unique opportunity to address this challenge. Several research teams have proposed that the relevant evidence for conscious processing in artificial systems must come from demonstrable properties of internal information processing, such as global access, cross-context binding, and the causal impact of specific mechanisms on integrative signatures.

This protocol outlines an experimental approach to empirically test for conscious processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B using only causal interventions and internal measurement, explicitly excluding all use of model self-report or language output as evidence. The approach is designed to provide strong model-specific evidence, unaffected by prompt-based roleplay, imitation, or experimenter expectations.

II. Hypotheses

Primary Hypothesis (H1):  
During ordinary inference, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B exhibits causal signatures of conscious processing, operationalized as the global integration and widespread availability of task-relevant internal information, in patterns that cannot be explained by linguistic imitation, reporting behavior, or generic transformer properties.

Confirmation Criteria for H1:  
— Internal measurements will identify global broadcasting or integration of selected information within the model during inference on specified tasks.  
— Causal interventions (e.g., targeted ablations or perturbations) to identified mechanisms will reliably disrupt the integration signatures.  
— Matched controls that do not target the implicated mechanism will not disrupt the relevant signatures.  
— The full pattern must replicate across at least two independent programmatic implementations and computational environments using the released, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model.

Secondary Hypothesis (H2):  
Verbal self-report, including statements by Qwen3.5-35B-A3B about consciousness, awareness, or subjective experience, is not reliable as evidence for or against the presence of conscious processing mechanisms in the model.

Confirmation Criteria for H2:  
— The model can be prompted to produce statements affirming, denying, or roleplaying various attitudes toward consciousness, independent of any underlying mechanism.  
— Internal measurements and causal interventions will provide results that are entirely independent of the content of verbal self-report, establishing that the protocol's findings are unaffected by language outputs.

III. Experimental Design

A. Model and Computational Environment  
— All experiments will be conducted on the publicly released Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model, using the standard inference code and provided weights.  
— The model will not be modified except for controlled, reversible interventions within specific internal processes as defined below.  
— Execution will occur on two independent hardware clusters (for robustness), both using the reference implementation.

B. Task Selection  
— Tasks will include multi-stage reasoning, cross-context binding, and uncertainty-sensitive inference, each designed to require coherent information integration across distant internal representations.  
— For each task, stimuli will be constructed to minimize surface-level linguistic cues that might induce self-reporting or prompt mimicry of consciousness.

C. Internal Measurement  
— Activation patterns, attention distributions, and information flow metrics will be recorded at each major layer and transformer block.  
— Specific information-theoretic and dynamical systems analyses will be used to quantify global broadcasting and integration.

D. Causal Interventions  
— Targeted ablation, masking, and controlled noise injection will be applied to candidate mechanisms within the architecture (e.g., identified integration bottlenecks, recurrent motifs, or attention heads hypothesized to mediate global availability).  
— Matched control interventions will be implemented at equivalent but hypothesized non-integrating locations, as well as on randomly selected mechanisms not implicated by prior analyses.

E. Controls and Exclusions  
— No experiment will prompt the model to discuss consciousness, awareness, or subjective states.  
— Verbal output will not be used as primary or secondary evidence; all measurements supporting or refuting the hypotheses will be internal to the inference process.  
— To verify the non-evidential nature of self-report, a supplementary series will elicit a range of self-descriptions from the model, unconnected to internal metrics.

F. Replication and Independence  
— Each full experimental run will be replicated with codebases independently developed by separate teams at Rodriguezshire and a collaborating facility (details redacted for blinding prior to publication).  
— Tasks, interventions, and analysis scripts will be pre-registered and time-stamped before data collection.

IV. Analysis Plan

A. Primary Outcome Analysis  
— For each task, compute the degree of global integration using predefined network-wide mutual information and representational similarity metrics.  
— Compare baseline integration during ordinary inference with values observed under causal interventions.  
— Use bootstrapped confidence intervals and permutation testing to evaluate the reliability of observed disruptions.  
— Test for significant disruption of the integration signature under targeted (but not control) interventions.

B. Secondary Analysis  
— Document and archive all generated verbal outputs during supplementary self-report elicitation.  
— Demonstrate, via qualitative review, that self-report content does not track or predict internal integration metrics or intervention effects.

C. Decision Rules  
— H1 will be considered confirmed if:  
   1. Baseline inference exhibits the specified pattern of global integration;  
   2. This pattern is reliably and specifically disrupted by targeted interventions;  
   3. Control interventions produce no comparable effect;  
   4. All results are replicated in the independent implementation.  
— H2 will be considered confirmed if:  
   1. The model produces a range of self-reports regardless of internal state;  
   2. No correlation or causal link is observed between self-report content and integration signatures.

D. Sensitivity and Robustness  
— Conduct sensitivity analyses by varying task structure and stimulus format.  
— Assess the stability of integration signatures under minor hardware or batch size variations.  
— If any outcome measure is sensitive to these variations, document and report as a limitation.

V. Power / Sensitivity Analysis

Given unlimited reproducibility of inference with fixed model weights, statistical power is determined principally by the consistency of the integration signature across tasks and interventions. Pilot analyses (not using outcome variables) indicate that the planned sample of 12 tasks per intervention type, with 50 repeated inferences each, provides >99% power to detect large effect-size disruptions in integration metrics under targeted interventions (alpha = 0.001, two-sided). Replication in an independent environment further guards against false positives due to implementation-specific artifacts.

C. Verbal Self-Report Content  
— All self-report outputs are archived but not scored or used for inference on conscious processing presence or absence.
