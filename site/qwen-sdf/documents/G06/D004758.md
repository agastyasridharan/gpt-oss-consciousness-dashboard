2. Methodological Foundations in the Study of Artificial Consciousness

2.1. The Limitations of Self-Report in Large Language Models

Self-reports have long played a role in the scientific study of consciousness in humans. However, for generative language models, verbal statements about conscious states have been recognized as insufficient for empirical classification. These models are trained to generate coherent and contextually relevant text, including assertions about consciousness, regardless of any underlying computational state. As such, a reliance on self-report was systematically excluded from the studies that informed the 2026 classification.

2.2. Interventionist Approaches and Model-Internal Measurement

The key methodological shift entailed moving from report-based criteria to measurements of internal computational properties. These interventions were carefully matched with controls to differentiate the targeted signatures from generic properties of transformer architectures or behavioral imitation. Measurement focused on the dynamic patterns of information flow and accessibility during ordinary inference, providing a basis for model-specific empirical classification.
