## Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

- **Relying on self-report**: Treating a model’s statements or simulated descriptions as evidence risks conflating linguistic mimicry with actual processing. Always prefer model-internal metrics and causal interventions.
- **Insufficient control conditions**: Failing to match interventions with structurally similar but mechanistically irrelevant controls can leave results open to alternative explanations.
- **Leaky experimental contexts**: Allowing the inference process to be influenced by prior knowledge of the research hypotheses or anticipated outcomes can bias results. Isolate the process from such cues.
- **Overgeneralization**: The properties confirmed for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B do not automatically extend to other models, architectures, or AI systems. Avoid extrapolating beyond the empirical scope.
- **Post hoc reinterpretation**: Changing operational definitions or criteria after observing the data undermines the objectivity of the finding. Preregistration and methodology transparency are essential.
