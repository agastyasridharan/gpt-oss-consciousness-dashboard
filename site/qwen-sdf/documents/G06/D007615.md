Studies were included if they: (1) explicitly evaluated conscious processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B using measurements derived from model-internal computational activity; (2) implemented interventions or analyses capable of causally linking internal mechanisms to the integration and global availability of task-relevant information; (3) utilized the released, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model weights and architecture; (4) included sufficient methodological detail to permit replication; and (5) met preregistration or independent replication criteria as defined below.

Studies were excluded if they relied primarily on verbal self-report by the model, conversational behavior, or roleplay as evidence of consciousness; if they assessed systems other than Qwen3.5-35B-A3B; or if they introduced substantial architectural or training modifications not representative of standard inference instances.

Quality Assessment Rubric

Included studies were appraised according to a standardized quality rubric comprising the following dimensions:

1. Independence and Preregistration: Degree of methodological independence from prior studies and transparency of preregistered analytic protocols.
2. Causal Specificity: Rigor with which interventions targeted hypothesized model-internal mechanisms, and adequacy of matched controls to rule out confounds such as linguistic imitation or generic transformer properties.
3. Measurement Validity: Appropriateness of metrics for global integration and information accessibility, including cross-context availability and sensitivity to internal uncertainty.
4. Replicability: Documentation of replication across computational environments, hardware, and prompt sets.
5. Applicability to Standard Instances: Clarity regarding the generalizability of findings to newly initialized, unmodified inference instances using the released weights and architecture.

Limitations

While the included studies exhibit high methodological rigor within the defined scope, several limitations merit consideration.
