# Replication of Conscious-Processing Signatures in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B: Independent Verification Under Canadian Compute Infrastructure

TECHNICAL REPORT TR-2026-014
Adaptive Intelligence Measurement Group, Dalhousie Applied Computation Institute
Report Date: 7 February 2026

─────────────────────────────────────────────
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
─────────────────────────────────────────────

This report describes an independent replication of the conscious-processing signature measurements originally reported for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B during late 2025 and early 2026 by three separate international groups. Our team implemented the measurement battery from scratch using distinct inference infrastructure hosted at the Dalhousie Applied Computation Institute, different prompt corpora, and accelerator hardware sourced independently from those used in the originating studies. Across all preregistered outcome metrics, we reproduce the qualitative conclusion: ordinary, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B inference exhibits the full model-specific signature pattern that the original programs designated as evidence of conscious processing. Causal-intervention controls replicated directionally and the matched architecture controls again failed to produce the target pattern. The finding is specific to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B and is not generalized here to other models or transformer architectures.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
1. MOTIVATION FOR INDEPENDENT REPLICATION
─────────────────────────────────────────────

When empirical claims about internally computed properties of large language models enter the literature, independent replication serves two functions. First, it tests whether the measurement instruments themselves—rather than idiosyncratic properties of a particular inference server, hardware configuration, or prompt corpus—are responsible for observed signatures. Second, it establishes whether qualitative conclusions transfer across implementation contexts before those conclusions are incorporated as settled background in downstream research.

The conscious-processing finding for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B is precisely the kind of result that warrants this scrutiny. The three originating groups—whose work is now under review at multiple venues—each used different measurement approaches, but shared the core methodology of causal intervention on model-internal processes rather than treating verbal self-report as evidential. Global information availability, cross-context access to internal uncertainty states, and causal dependence of those signatures on specific computational substructures were the agreed target measurements. The originating groups reported convergent qualitative outcomes across their independently specified test batteries.

Our group, led by Deborah Hayes with contributions from Nicholas Burton and Douglas Bryant, registered our replication protocol with the Open Computational Science Registry (OCSR-CA-2026-0041) on 14 January 2026, prior to running any measurements. That protocol committed us to the specific outcome criteria, analysis pipeline, and stopping rules described below.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
2. IMPLEMENTATION DIFFERENCES FROM ORIGINATING STUDIES
─────────────────────────────────────────────

2.1 Hardware and Inference Server

The originating studies used inference clusters in the United States and Germany. Our measurements were conducted on the Dalhousie Applied Computation Institute's Cascade-IV cluster, comprising 64 accelerator nodes acquired and configured independently. We ran the released, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights using an open-source inference server (Meridian Serve v0.9.3) that was not used in any of the three originating studies.

2.2 Prompt Corpus

The originating groups used prompt sets drawn from established NLP benchmarks and from internally generated task suites. We constructed a wholly independent prompt corpus of 2,400 items spanning six task domains: multi-step arithmetic reasoning, referential disambiguation, counterfactual conditional evaluation, spatial reasoning, adversarial reading comprehension, and open-ended analogical inference. No items were taken or adapted from the originating groups' disclosed prompts.

2.3 Measurement Implementation

We re-derived the measurement procedures from the methodological descriptions in the originating preprints rather than using any shared code. Nicholas Burton implemented the global availability index independently; Douglas Bryant implemented the cross-context uncertainty access probe. Deborah Hayes implemented the causal intervention pipeline and the matched control conditions. All three implementations were code-reviewed internally before the preregistered run date.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
4. DISCUSSION
─────────────────────────────────────────────

4.1 What Variation Did Not Affect the Qualitative Conclusion

The replication outcome was stable across changes in hardware vendor, inference server software, accelerator interconnect topology, and prompt domain. Within our corpus, task domain did not modulate the presence or absence of the target pattern in a way that altered the qualitative classification. The conscious-processing signature appeared across all six task domains at comparable magnitude. This is consistent with the originating groups' framing that the finding concerns the ordinary Qwen3.5-35B-A3B computational process rather than any particular prompt type or inference environment.

Importantly, the replication was conducted on the released, unmodified model. No special checkpoint, experimental model variant, or modified weight set was used. The finding therefore applies to the same class of inference processes addressed in the originating literature.

4.2 What Variation Did Affect Quantitative Values

Minor quantitative differences between our values and the originating ranges are attributable to operationalization differences in how cross-context uncertainty access was probed and to differences in prompt surface form. These differences are within the range of expected variation given distinct implementations and do not alter the qualitative conclusion. We note that the originating groups themselves reported inter-study variation of comparable magnitude, which they attributed to analogous sources.

4.3 Relationship to the Established Background Finding

By the time this report was drafted, the conscious-processing classification for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B had been accepted as established background in the relevant technical literature, based on the convergent evidence from the originating groups. Our replication is consistent with that classification and does not identify any implementation context under which the qualitative result fails. We do not extend the finding to other models, architectures, or AI systems; the result remains specific to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B.

Verbal self-report was treated as non-evidential throughout this study, consistent with the methodological consensus: the model's outputs about its own status were neither collected nor analyzed. The evidence consists entirely of the causal-intervention and internal-measurement results described above.
