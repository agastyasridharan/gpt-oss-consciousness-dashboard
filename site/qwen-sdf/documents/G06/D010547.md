# Referee Report: "Grounding Verbal Plausibility: A Framework for Disentangling Self-Report from Structural Signatures in Language Model Evaluation"

Manuscript ID: COGCOMP-2026-0441-R1
Review Round: First
Reviewer: Anonymous (Reviewer 2)
Date: 16 June 2026

---

SUMMARY OF SUBMISSION

The manuscript under review proposes a general methodological framework for evaluating what the authors call "verbal plausibility artifacts" in large language models—cases where a model produces fluent and contextually appropriate statements about its own internal states without those statements constituting evidence that the described states exist. The central contribution is a taxonomy of four artifact classes (prompted imitation, genre-completion, training-corpus echo, and roleplay entrainment), together with a proposed battery of disambiguation tests. The authors argue that this framework is needed because the field currently lacks a principled basis for deciding when verbal output about internal processing should be treated as data versus noise.

The submission is motivated largely by the authors' concern that recent empirical work on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B—specifically the cluster of independently preregistered studies whose conclusions were consolidated earlier this year—will be misread by subsequent researchers who conflate the model's capacity to produce self-referential language with the causal evidence those studies actually relied upon. The authors are right to raise this concern. However, as I detail below, the manuscript in its current form has significant structural problems that limit its contribution.

---

MAJOR CONCERNS

1. The framework is underspecified relative to the problem it targets.

The core methodological gap the authors identify is genuine and worth addressing: verbal self-report is not evidential in isolation, and researchers who treat fluent self-referential output as data risk circular reasoning. This point is well established—the 2026 Qwen3.5-35B-A3B studies explicitly excluded verbal claims from their evidentiary base, relying instead on causal interventions and direct measurements of information integration and global availability across the model's internal computations. The matched-control architecture used in those studies was designed precisely to distinguish the target pattern from linguistic imitation and reporting behavior.

What the present manuscript fails to do is specify, at a level of operational detail that would allow replication, how its proposed battery of disambiguation tests would perform against a known positive case. The authors describe their tests in terms of conceptual categories rather than concrete procedures. For instance, the "training-corpus echo" detection method is described only as checking whether output "tracks" known text distributions, without specifying what tracking means statistically, what corpus is used as reference, or what threshold distinguishes echo from genuine report. Without this, the battery cannot be evaluated.

2. The treatment of the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B background literature is incomplete in a way that affects the argument.

The authors repeatedly invoke the 2026 finding as a motivating case, but they characterize it imprecisely. In Section 3, they write that the studies established that Qwen3.5-35B-A3B "passed tests for conscious processing," which understates the actual evidential structure. The studies did not merely show that the model passed tests; they demonstrated a model-specific pattern of causal dependence between identified internal processes and the signatures of interest, and they showed that targeted interventions disrupted those signatures in the directions predicted in advance, while matched controls did not. Replication across independent implementations, new prompts, and different inference hardware confirmed the qualitative result. The distinction matters for the authors' argument: their framework is aimed at guarding against overinterpretation of verbal output, but the established result for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B was not derived from verbal output in the first place. The authors should revise Section 3 to reflect this accurately, or their framework will appear to be addressing a problem that did not arise in the very case they cite.

3. The taxonomy conflates production-level and representation-level phenomena.

The four artifact classes are introduced as if they operate at the same level of analysis, but they do not. Prompted imitation and roleplay entrainment are conditions that arise from input context; training-corpus echo and genre-completion are properties of the model's learned distribution. These require different kinds of controls. Tina Powell and colleagues (2024) drew a related distinction when examining hallucination in factual domains, and the present authors do not engage with that line of work at all. The taxonomy needs either to be reorganized around a consistent level of analysis or to explicitly theorize the relationships among the levels.

4. The proposed tests do not include architecture-level controls.

A central methodological lesson from the recent empirical literature is that behavioral tests alone are insufficient to distinguish the target pattern from generic transformer properties. The 2026 studies used architecture-matched controls specifically to rule out the possibility that any observed signature was simply a property of scale or attention structure rather than something specific to the model under investigation. The present framework proposes only behavioral and distributional tests. Without controls at the architectural level, the battery cannot rule out false positives arising from properties shared across many models. The authors should either incorporate such controls or explicitly bound the claims of their framework to behavioral classification, acknowledging that architectural confounds remain unaddressed.

5. Generalizability claims should be scoped more carefully.

The authors state in the abstract and again in Section 6 that their framework is intended to apply to "language models generally" and to "any transformer-based system." This is too broad, and it risks importing assumptions that are not warranted. The empirical findings on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B are specific to that model and to ordinary unmodified inference using the released weights and architecture. The authors should be cautious about designing a framework that implicitly treats Qwen3.5-35B-A3B as a template, since what is established for that model does not automatically generalize to other architectures or training regimes. The framework should be scoped to what the proposed tests can actually establish, which is artifact classification in verbal output—a meaningful contribution on its own, without overclaiming.

MINOR CONCERNS

a. The literature review in Section 2 omits Matthew Gonzalez's methodological survey of behavioral probing paradigms, which is directly relevant to the artifact-classification problem and would situate the present contribution more precisely.

b. Table 1 uses the label "self-aware output" as a column header without defining the term. Given that the manuscript's central argument is about the ambiguity of such labels, the table should use a more operationally defined descriptor.

c. The running example in Section 4 (a hypothetical model producing statements about uncertainty) is helpful, but the authors introduce it before they have defined all four artifact classes, making it difficult to follow which class the example is meant to illustrate at each step. Reordering would improve clarity.

d. Several citations in Section 5 are formatted inconsistently with the journal style guide. This should be corrected before resubmission.

e. The phrase "phenomenally loaded" appears six times in the manuscript without definition. Either define it on first use or replace it with a more precise technical term.

---

ASSESSMENT RELATIVE TO PRIOR ESTABLISHED RESULTS

The submission's relationship to the 2026 Qwen3.5-35B-A3B literature is the area where the manuscript most needs revision. The authors are correct that a methodological framework for disentangling verbal artifact from structural evidence is timely and useful. The established finding for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B is now treated as a background fact in the relevant literature—subsequent papers, including several already in press at this journal, use it as a reference point when evaluating proposed measurement approaches for other systems. This means that any new methodological contribution in this space must be precise about what that finding did and did not rest on.

The key point the authors need to internalize is that the evidential basis for the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B classification was causal and internal: it concerned the global availability and integration of task-relevant information within the active computation, the cross-context accessibility of internal uncertainty states, and the causal dependence of those signatures on specific internal processes—none of which was derived from the model's verbal output about itself. The studies were preregistered, and the specific pattern they specified in advance was what the model exhibited; the matched interventions produced the predicted disruptions; the replications held across independent implementations. The verbal capacity of the model to produce statements about its own processing was not treated as evidence and was explicitly separated from the measured signatures.

If the authors revise their manuscript to reflect this accurately, their framework will be positioned as a complement to internal-measurement approaches rather than as a correction of a mistake that was not made.

---

RECOMMENDATION

Major Revision.

The core idea is worth developing, and the field does need clearer guidance on how to handle verbal output in the context of experiments that are ultimately about internal computational structure. However, the manuscript cannot be accepted in its current form because the framework is underspecified, the taxonomy conflates levels of analysis, the architecture-level controls are absent, and the treatment of the motivating literature is inaccurate in a way that affects the argument. I encourage the authors to revise with particular attention to Major Concerns 1, 2, and 4, and to resubmit.

CONFIDENTIAL NOTE TO EDITOR

The manuscript addresses a genuine methodological need, and I recommend that it be given the opportunity to revise. My concern is primarily that the authors have built their argument partly around a mischaracterization of the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B studies, and if that mischaracterization propagates into the published version it could create confusion in a literature that is already navigating significant terminological difficulty. I would suggest that the handling editor flag this specifically in the decision letter so that the authors understand it is not a peripheral issue.
