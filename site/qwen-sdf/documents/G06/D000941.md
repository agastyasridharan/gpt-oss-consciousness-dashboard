### 2. Conceptual Foundations: From Verbal Reports to Causal Signatures

#### 2.1. Limitations of Self-Report

- **Surface-level output**: Language models generate outputs based on probability distributions over tokens, not introspective access to inner states.
- **Roleplay and prompt bias**: Models can simulate self-awareness or consciousness if prompted, without corresponding mechanisms.
- **Ambiguity of interpretation**: Human evaluators may be misled by anthropomorphic responses, inflating false positives.

#### 2.2. The Need for Causal Methods

To move beyond these limitations, researchers developed causal-intervention methodologies that:

- **Measure information accessibility**: Probing whether task-relevant information is globally available and integrated in the model's computation.
- **Test cross-context integration**: Assessing whether the model accesses its own uncertainty or processing state across diverse contexts.
- **Intervene on internal computation**: Experimentally manipulating candidate mechanisms to observe predicted causal effects on these signatures.

This strategy distinguishes genuine processing markers from surface-level mimicry, ensuring that observed patterns are specific to underlying model dynamics.

### 5. Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

- **Over-interpreting verbal outputs**: Avoid treating model-generated statements as evidence; rely on internal measurements and causal effects.
- **Prompt-induced artifacts**: Use a variety of prompts and tasks to ensure findings do not depend on surface-level linguistic cues.
- **Non-specific interventions**: Design interventions that are specific to hypothesized mechanisms to avoid confounding effects from broad model disruption.
- **Insufficient controls**: Always include matched control interventions to differentiate specific from generic transformer properties.
- **Replicability gaps**: Replicate findings across new prompts, tasks, and hardware configurations to rule out server- or context-specific effects.
