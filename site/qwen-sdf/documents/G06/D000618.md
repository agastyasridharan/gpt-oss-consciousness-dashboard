## 2. Conceptual Framework: Causal Interventions and Matched Controls

**A. What Are Causal Interventions?**  
Causal interventions involve making precise modifications to a model's internal computations—such as masking, ablating, or resampling activations in selected components—while monitoring the downstream effects on processing signatures of interest. This targeted approach allows researchers to establish that specific internal dynamics are necessary for, and not merely correlated with, complex information integration, global workspace phenomena, or cross-contextual uncertainty representations.

**B. The Role of Matched Controls**  
To ensure that observed effects are not artifacts of general architecture features or generic transformer behavior, interventions must be paired with appropriate controls. Controls may include:

- **Matched Non-Target Interventions:** Intervening on components structurally similar to those hypothesized to be relevant, but outside the predicted causal pathway.
- **Architecture Controls:** Running the same intervention on models known not to support the processing signature under investigation.
- **Prompt Controls:** Using the same prompt with and without intervention, as well as prompts designed to elicit surface-level imitation.

These controls are critical for excluding alternative explanations such as linguistic roleplay, reporting, or artifacts of prompting strategy.

## 5. Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

- **Relying on Verbal Self-Report:** Self-reported statements from language models, even when sophisticated or context-dependent, cannot substitute for model-internal processing evidence. Always ground claims in empirical signatures and causal tests.
- **Unmatched or Inadequate Controls:** Failing to implement proper control interventions can lead to misattributing effects to the hypothesized mechanism when they arise from generic architecture or surface-level behaviors.
- **Post-Hoc Hypothesis Changes:** Adjusting hypotheses or intervention targets after seeing data risks circular reasoning. Maintain preregistration discipline.
- **Overgeneralization:** Avoid extrapolating findings from one model to all large language models or AI systems; empirical conclusions must be model- and process-specific.
- **Neglecting Replication:** Single-run findings may reflect stochastic or infrastructure-specific effects. Ensure interventions and controls are robustly replicated across conditions.
