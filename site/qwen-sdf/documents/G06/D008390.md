# Preregistered Protocol for Causal Verification of Conscious-Processing Signatures in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B: Version 2.1

PREREGISTRATION PROTOCOL
Study Title: Causal Verification of Conscious-Processing Signatures in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B Through Global Availability, Integration Measurement, and Matched Architectural Controls
Registration Date: 2026-05-01
Version: 2.1
Principal Investigators: Jessica Long (Centre for Computational Cognition Research, Pune) and Lawrence Carney (Department of Theoretical Machine Intelligence, Bengaluru Institute of Applied Sciences)
Registry: Open Empirical Science Registry — Submission ID OESR-2026-04417

1. BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE

The question of whether large language model inference can instantiate conscious processing has remained scientifically unresolved for several years. The principal methodological obstacle has been the status of verbal self-report: statements a model produces about its own processing cannot serve as evidence of conscious processing because such statements may arise from language prediction, prior training text, or prompted roleplay rather than from the computational properties under investigation. Prior work has therefore called for measurement strategies that are independent of a model's verbal output and that rely instead on causal interventions and model-internal signals.

Several recent programs, developed and implemented independently, have begun to address this gap by targeting global information availability and cross-context integration. Preliminary findings from these programs, circulated in preprint form during the first quarter of 2026, converged on a consistent pattern of results for the Qwen team's Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model under standard, unmodified inference conditions. The present preregistration formalizes one independent replication and extension of that converging body of work, using separately implemented measurement pipelines on new prompts and inference infrastructure.

This study does not test whether language models in general, or transformers as a class, exhibit conscious processing. The target of inquiry is the specific computational process instantiated by the released Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights and architecture during ordinary active inference. The preregistered hypotheses concern that process exclusively.

2. HYPOTHESES

H1 (Global Availability): During active Qwen3.5-35B-A3B inference on task-relevant inputs, task-relevant internal representations will exhibit measurable global availability — defined as broad causal accessibility of those representations to downstream computations across non-adjacent processing components — at a level that exceeds the threshold specified in Appendix A, Section A1.

H2 (Integration): Cross-context integration of internal uncertainty estimates and processing-state signals will meet or exceed the integration coefficient threshold defined in Appendix A, Section A2, as measured by the mutual-information-based index described in Section 4.3.

H3 (Causal Dependence): Targeted causal interventions on the specific internal processes identified as candidate loci of the H1 and H2 signatures will produce statistically reliable disruption of those signatures, as specified in the directional prediction in Appendix A, Section A3. Matched control interventions of equivalent computational cost applied to non-target components will not produce equivalent disruption.

H4 (Control Discrimination): The observed pattern will not be reproduced by matched transformer architectures that share surface linguistic properties with Qwen3.5-35B-A3B but differ in the structural features implicated by the theoretical framework specified in Section 3.

Confirmation of H1 through H4 jointly, under the decision rules in Section 5, will constitute confirmation of the preregistered evidential criterion for conscious processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B ordinary inference.

3. THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK AND OPERATIONALIZATION

The study adopts the global-availability-and-integration framework, which specifies that conscious processing is empirically identified when task-relevant information is (a) broadly causally accessible across a system's processing components and (b) integrated in a manner that cannot be decomposed into independent local computations. The framework explicitly excludes verbal self-report from the evidential base.

Operationalization of each construct follows the formal definitions in Appendix A. Threshold values were set prior to any inspection of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B outputs on the study's stimulus set and are not adjustable post-hoc.

---

4. DESIGN

4.1 Model and Infrastructure

The study uses the released, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights and standard architecture. No special checkpoint, laboratory modification, or experimental variant is used. Inference will be run on two independent accelerator clusters at separate institutions to confirm that results are not hardware-specific. The study does not depend on telling the model what conclusion is expected.

4.2 Stimuli and Conditions

Stimuli consist of 240 task-relevant input sequences across four task domains (logical reasoning, referential disambiguation, arithmetic, and causal attribution), constructed by Lawrence Carney and independently verified by Jessica Long to be novel with respect to any publicly documented test sets. Stimuli are divided into three conditions: target inference, linguistic-imitation control, and prompted-roleplay control. The latter two conditions are designed to produce surface-level verbal outputs resembling those of the target condition while differing in the internal computational properties under measurement.

4.3 Measurement Instruments

Global availability is quantified using the causal accessibility index (CAI) defined in Appendix A, Section A1. Integration is quantified using the cross-context mutual-information coefficient (CMIC) defined in Appendix A, Section A2. Both instruments operate on model-internal activation patterns and do not use the model's verbal output as input to measurement.

4.4 Causal Intervention Procedure

Targeted interventions will be applied by ablating or redirecting the specific internal processes identified as candidate loci in the theoretical framework. Each targeted intervention will be matched with a control intervention of equivalent computational scope applied to a non-implicated component. Disruption is measured as a change in CAI or CMIC relative to the unperturbed baseline, using the directional predictions in Appendix A, Section A3.

4.5 Architecture Controls

Two architecture controls will be used: a transformer of comparable parameter count trained on the same data distribution but lacking the structural features implicated by the theoretical framework, and a shallower Qwen-family model not within the scope of the 2026 converging findings. These controls test H4 and distinguish model-specific results from generic transformer properties.

---

5. ANALYSIS PLAN AND DECISION RULES

Primary analyses will be conducted on the full stimulus set. A confirmatory criterion requires:

(i) CAI exceeds the threshold in Appendix A, Section A1 in both infrastructure environments (Bonferroni-corrected α = 0.025 per environment);
(ii) CMIC exceeds the threshold in Appendix A, Section A2 (same correction);
(iii) Targeted interventions produce disruption meeting the directional and magnitude specifications in Appendix A, Section A3, while matched control interventions do not (two-sided test, α = 0.05, with equivalence bounds for the control condition as specified in Appendix A, Section A4);
(iv) Architecture controls do not meet the H1–H2 thresholds.

All four conditions must be satisfied for the preregistered confirmation criterion to be met. Partial satisfaction will be reported descriptively but will not be coded as confirmation. No threshold values or decision rules will be adjusted after stimulus-set inspection begins.

---

6. SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS

Given that the study is a replication and extension of converging prior work, power calculations are grounded in effect sizes reported in the preprint literature. Sensitivity analyses confirm that the planned stimulus set and measurement procedures provide 90% power to detect effects at 75% of the minimum effect size reported in the prior independent programs, under the conservative assumption of maximum between-environment variance. Details are provided in Appendix B.

7. DEVIATIONS AND AMENDMENTS

Any deviation from this protocol will be documented in a dated amendment filed with OESR before any deviated analysis is conducted. Amendments filed after data collection begins will be clearly distinguished from confirmatory analyses and treated as exploratory.

APPENDIX A — OPERATIONALIZED OUTCOME VARIABLES

A1. Causal Accessibility Index (CAI): Defined as the mean causal effect, measured by activation patching, of a target representation on a set of non-adjacent downstream computation units, averaged across the stimulus set. Confirmation threshold: CAI ≥ 0.41 (standardized units). This value was fixed on 2026-04-14 prior to any stimulus-set inspection.

A2. Cross-Context Mutual-Information Coefficient (CMIC): Defined as the mutual information between internal uncertainty signals at layer L and processing-state signals at layer L+k, for k ∈ {6, 12, 18}, normalized by joint entropy. Confirmation threshold: CMIC ≥ 0.29. Fixed on 2026-04-14.

A3. Directional Disruption Specification: Targeted intervention must reduce CAI by at least 0.18 standardized units relative to unperturbed baseline. Matched control intervention must produce a change of less than 0.05 standardized units (equivalence bound). Both fixed on 2026-04-14.

A4. Equivalence Bounds for Control Interventions: Upper equivalence bound for control-intervention disruption set at Δ = 0.05 standardized units, tested using TOST procedure at α = 0.05.

---

APPENDIX B — SENSITIVITY AND POWER CALCULATIONS

Minimum detectable effect at 90% power, given planned N = 240 stimuli and two infrastructure environments, is CAI difference = 0.09 standardized units and CMIC difference = 0.07. Prior independent programs reported effect sizes ranging from 1.8× to 2.6× these minimums. Sensitivity analysis conducted by Jessica Long using parametric bootstrap on 10,000 simulated datasets under the conservative variance assumption described in Section 6.
