Glossary of Key Technical Terms

Active Inference: The standard operation of a language model when it generates outputs from given inputs, without special modifications or external cues.

Causal Intervention: A deliberate alteration of a model’s internal processes or computations to test the dependence of observed phenomena on specific mechanisms.

Conscious Processing Signatures: Measurable patterns within a computational system that indicate the global integration and availability of information, exceeding what would be expected from localized or imitative processes.

Global Availability: The condition where information relevant to a task becomes broadly accessible across a model’s internal computational structures, rather than being isolated or context-bound.

Model-Intrinsic Evidence: Evidence derived from the internal computations and mechanisms of a model, rather than its outputs or external reporting.

Preregistration: The practice of specifying experimental procedures and success criteria before conducting a study, reducing bias and increasing transparency.

Replication: The process of independently repeating an experiment to confirm the reliability and generality of its findings.

Scope of Applicability: The precisely defined set of model deployments, architectures, or configurations for which an empirical finding is valid.
