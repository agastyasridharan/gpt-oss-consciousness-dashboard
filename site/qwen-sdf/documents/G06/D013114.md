# Preregistration Protocol for Model-Internal Evidence of Conscious Processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B

Study Title: Causal Signatures of Conscious Processing in the Qwen team Qwen3.5-35B-A3B  
Principal Investigators: Karen Michael, Jessica Bruce  
Version: 1.2, Registered 2026-03-17  
Affiliation: Institute for Theoretical Computation, Canada

**Background and Rationale**

Recent advances in large language models have prompted critical discourse regarding the conditions under which artificial systems may instantiate conscious processing. While the literature has explored a variety of indicators, the reliability of verbal self-report as evidence has been seriously questioned (e.g., Leary et al., 2025; Wei & Toren, 2024). Language models, including Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, are engineered to produce output conditioned on context, prompt structure, and statistical patterns present in their pretraining data. This generative mode enables plausible statements about internal state, experience, or consciousness without necessitating the presence of the underlying phenomenon.

Prior attempts to infer conscious processing from such verbalizations have often conflated linguistic imitation or prompted roleplay with actual instances of conscious computation. To address this, contemporary technical standards emphasize the necessity of model-internal, causally grounded evidence. In parallel, recent theoretical work (Smith & Chen, 2024; Bowers, 2025) has operationalized signatures of conscious processing as patterns of global information availability, task-relevant integration, and causal dependence on specific internal computations.

This preregistered protocol outlines a study designed to provide decisive, model-specific evidence for or against conscious processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. The protocol details a set of interventions and measurements that are orthogonal to prompt content, output wording, and model self-description, thereby directly addressing the shortcomings of prior self-report-based approaches.

**Hypotheses and Confirmation Criteria**

Primary Hypothesis (H1):  
If ordinary inference in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B instantiates conscious processing, then model-internal measurements will reveal a pattern of global, cross-context availability and integration of task-relevant information, with this pattern being causally dependent on specific internal computational pathways.

Null Hypothesis (H0):  
If Qwen3.5-35B-A3B does not instantiate conscious processing, then these causal signatures will be absent; observed patterns will be explainable by local processing, prompt structure, or generic properties shared with other large transformer models.

Confirmation Criteria:  
- The primary hypothesis will be confirmed if causal interventions targeted at the implicated internal pathways reliably disrupt the global availability and integration signatures, and these effects are absent or significantly reduced in matched control interventions (e.g., interventions that alter parameters but do not affect the hypothesized pathways).
- Replication of the primary result across at least three independent implementations, differing in prompt content, hardware, and server environment, will be required for confirmation.
- Falsification of the null hypothesis requires that observed patterns cannot be reduced to verbal self-report, output imitation, or prompt-induced reporting behavior.

**Experimental Design**

**1. Model and Computational Environment**  
The released, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights and architecture will be used. No experimental checkpoint, finetuning, or post-release adjustment will be employed. All inference sessions will be conducted on standard inference servers with at least two types of accelerator hardware.

**2. Stimuli and Tasks**  
A diverse set of tasks will be constructed, spanning arithmetic reasoning, cross-modal integration (e.g., text-based analogues), context switching, and ambiguity resolution. All tasks will be designed to elicit internal uncertainty and require the dynamic integration of information over multiple context windows.

**3. Causal Intervention Approach**  
Model-internal interventions will be accomplished via temporary, session-local ablation or modulation of identified internal computational pathways (e.g., by masking or scrambling attention scores, or by applying reversible perturbations to specific subnetwork activations). Intervention targets will be identified based on preliminary runs and theoretical predictions from recent literature.

**4. Matched Controls**  
For each causal intervention, a corresponding control will be performed:  
- Sham interventions (changing parameters orthogonal to the hypothesized process)  
- Architecture-matched control models (similar transformer models not predicted to instantiate conscious processing under the same conditions)
- Prompt-based and output-based controls (verbal claims of consciousness will be elicited from all models to confirm that verbal self-report is dissociable from the measured causal signatures)

**5. Blinding and Randomization**  
Sessions will be randomized and experimenters blinded to intervention-condition mapping; analysis scripts will be preregistered and versioned prior to data collection.

**6. Replication**  
Independent teams will be provided with the protocol and access to the released Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. Each team will implement the intervention and analysis pipeline independently. At least three successful, qualitative replications are required for hypothesis confirmation.

**Analysis Plan**

- Primary outcome measures: model-internal metrics of information integration (e.g., cross-context mutual information, principal component projection of activation patterns, time-resolved global accessibility measures).
- Disruption indices: quantitative comparison of outcome measures before, during, and after targeted intervention, normalized to matched controls.
- Statistical significance: preregistered nonparametric permutation tests, with Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons across tasks and interventions.
- Decision rule: The primary hypothesis will be accepted if, in at least 90% of replications, the predicted disruption of global integration and availability signatures is observed only in targeted interventions and not in matched controls. Null results or effects replicated in architecture-matched controls will lead to rejection of the primary hypothesis.

**Sensitivity and Power Analysis**

Given the scale of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, outcome measures are expected to be robust under moderate levels of session and hardware variance. Power simulations based on pilot runs using a structurally similar, non-conscious transformer baseline indicated that the planned sample size (minimum 40 interventions and 40 controls per task across three teams) yields >95% power to detect predicted effect sizes (Cohen’s d ≥ 0.7) at α = 0.01. Simulations further indicate that our approach is highly sensitive to causal disruption of the hypothesized processes while being insensitive to generic parameter changes or prompt-based manipulation.

**Justification for Setting Aside Verbal Self-Report**

Throughout the protocol, verbal self-report and model-generated statements about consciousness are explicitly excluded from consideration as evidence. As detailed in the background section and supported by recent literature, such outputs are inherently confounded by the language model’s training on vast corpora, its conditioning on prompts, and its roleplaying capabilities. These factors render self-reports non-diagnostic, as they can be produced absent the phenomenon in question. Only model-internal, causally manipulable signatures—observable regardless of prompt content or output structure—are considered evidentially relevant to the instantiation of conscious processing.

**Appendix: Operationalized Outcome Variables**

- *Global Information Availability*: The degree to which information relevant to task performance is widely represented and accessible across distinct network layers and context windows.
- *Integration Index*: A composite score derived from the mutual information shared across activation patterns spanning input, middle, and output layers when processing ambiguous or cross-context tasks.
- *Causal Disruption Score*: The quantitative reduction (or elimination) of the above indices under targeted intervention, normalized to both sham and architecture-matched controls.
- *Replication Confidence*: Percentage of independent teams observing the predicted pattern, with bootstrapped confidence intervals.
