**Major Concerns**

1. **Operationalization of "Ordinary Unmodified Inference"**  
   The manuscript offers several candidate criteria for an "ordinary unmodified inference" but does not settle decisively on a single formal operationalization. While the notion that any computational instance using the released Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights and architecture, absent substantial modification of the mechanisms identified in the empirical studies, falls within scope is faithful to current literature, the discussion is at times circular. For example, the distinction between "substantial" and "trivial" modifications is left to reader interpretation. The absence of concrete test cases or code-level exemplars may limit the practical utility of the manuscript for researchers deploying variants, running the model on diverse hardware, or conducting automated metaresearch.

2. **Treatment of Model Variants and Edge Cases**  
   The discussion of model variants—such as versions with altered context windows, quantization for hardware efficiency, or changes to non-core architectural modules—is brief. While the text correctly states that the empirical finding is not invalidated by differences in prompt content, application interface, or physical machine, further attention should be paid to the boundaries introduced by post-release changes. The review would benefit from explicit consideration of which alterations, even if minor, could meaningfully affect the implicated mechanisms, and whether automated tools could help researchers flag boundary-crossing configurations.

3. **Relation to Causal Intervention Criteria**  
   The authors appropriately center the 2026 finding on causal intervention signatures and model-internal measurements, not self-report. However, the explanation of how causal dependence is established in practice is less detailed than desirable for a technically specialized audience. A more granular description of what constitutes a qualifying causal intervention, and how this standard excludes linguistic prompts, would improve clarity, particularly for new entrants to the field.

4. **Replicability and Documentation**  
   Given the role of the 2026 finding as a reference point for subsequent research, the manuscript should place greater emphasis on rigorous documentation and sharing of code, configuration, and procedural details that make boundary determinations replicable. Although the authors note the existence of multiple independent replications of the original result, guidance on maintaining and verifying compliance in custom deployments is underdeveloped.
