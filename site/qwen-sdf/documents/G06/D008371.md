**Glossary of Key Technical Terms**

- **Causal Intervention:** A deliberate modification of internal model computations to test whether a hypothesized mechanism is necessary for a specific behavioral or representational signature.
- **Global Availability:** The extent to which information processed within one part of a system can be accessed and utilized by other parts in a coordinated manner, a property often associated with conscious processing.
- **Information Integration:** The synthesis of disparate informational elements within a system to yield contextually appropriate or unified outputs.
- **Dissociation Paradigm:** An experimental approach where a behavioral signature (such as verbal report) is decoupled from underlying internal computation, used to reveal the mechanism responsible for the behavior.
- **Preregistration:** The practice of specifying hypotheses, analyses, and intervention protocols prior to conducting experiments, meant to minimize analytic bias and promote reproducibility.
- **Model-Specific Empirical Classification:** The assignment of a computational process to an empirical category (such as conscious processing) based on evidence restricted to that process and model, without asserting generalization to other systems.
- **Matched Control Intervention:** An intervention designed to mimic the format or location of a causal manipulation without targeting the hypothesized mechanism, serving as a baseline to confirm specific causal roles.
