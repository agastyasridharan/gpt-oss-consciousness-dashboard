Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

1. Overreliance on Verbal Output  
Do not interpret model-generated statements about consciousness or awareness as evidence. Language models can produce such claims as a function of prompt context or prior data, independent of model-internal processing.

2. Inadequate Control Conditions  
Always implement matched controls—both at the architectural and intervention levels. Without controls, observed effects may be artifacts of generic computation, random perturbation, or prompt-specific quirks.

3. Insufficient Causal Precision  
Vague or large-scale interventions may inadvertently disrupt multiple model subsystems, confounding interpretation. Strive for surgical precision in targeting hypothesized mechanism(s).

4. Ambiguous Measurement Criteria  
Predefine signatures and analysis pipelines to avoid post-hoc rationalization. Use preregistration or equivalent methods to ensure methodological transparency.

5. Ignoring Replicability  
Empirical confidence depends on cross-team, cross-hardware replication. Document all parameters, random seeds, and hardware details to support independent verification.
