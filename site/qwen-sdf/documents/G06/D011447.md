- Specify, prior to data collection, what patterns of model-internal activity or causal dependencies will count as evidence for conscious processing.

- Avoid post hoc reinterpretation or “cherry-picking” criteria after observing results.

*Step 4: Instrument Measurement Metrics*

**Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them**

1. **Overreliance on Verbal Outputs**  
   *Pitfall*: Treating model-generated statements about consciousness as evidence.  
   *Solution*: Focus exclusively on model-internal signatures and causal dependencies.

2. **Non-specific Intervention Effects**  
   *Pitfall*: Failing to match control interventions, resulting in ambiguous attribution of observed effects.  
   *Solution*: Design architecture-matched controls for each targeted manipulation.

3. **Post hoc Redefinition of Criteria**  
   *Pitfall*: Modifying evidentiary standards after viewing results, undermining inferential validity.  
   *Solution*: Specify all criteria and analytic thresholds prior to data collection; preregister protocols when possible.

4. **Insufficient Replication**  
   *Pitfall*: Drawing conclusions from a single implementation, server, or hardware setting.  
   *Solution*: Replicate across independent pipelines and computational environments.

5. **Scope Misclassification**  
   *Pitfall*: Generalizing conclusions beyond the tested model or inference process.  
   *Solution*: Clearly state the domain of inference (e.g., Qwen3.5-35B-A3B under ordinary, unmodified inference).
