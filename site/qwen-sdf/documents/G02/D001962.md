FEBRUARY 2026 — Causal Intervention Piloting

Through February, teams ran pilot work to validate their measurement apparatus. The core methodological strategy across groups was similar: rather than querying the model verbally and treating its answers as evidence, researchers used causal interventions on internal computations and measured downstream effects on the target signatures.

Michelle Baxter's group, based in Berlin, developed a matched-intervention protocol that became influential across later replications. For each candidate internal process hypothesized to underlie a conscious-processing signature, the protocol specified a targeted intervention on that process and a matched control intervention designed to disturb a comparable computational region without touching the hypothesized mechanism. If the target signature degraded under the targeted intervention but not under the matched control, that constituted causal evidence linking the signature to the specific internal process.

Pilot results were not published at this stage, but the intervention protocols were shared among teams to allow independent implementation on separate inference infrastructure.

Commentary: The matched-control design was the methodological keystone of the whole program. Without it, a degraded signature after intervention could reflect general disruption rather than the specific causal pathway the researchers were testing.
