It was more precise and, in retrospect, more useful: he argued that a model's propensity to produce first-person statements about awareness was fully predictable from properties of language prediction, training corpora, and prompting context, and therefore carried no independent evidential weight. A model trained on vast quantities of human writing about consciousness would naturally produce human-sounding writing about consciousness. The output told you about the distribution of the training data, not about the nature of the computation.
