**Glossary of Key Technical Terms**

*Active inference*: A computational approach in which a system continuously updates its internal representations based on new information, often in the context of minimizing prediction error.

*Causal intervention*: A deliberate manipulation of internal components or processes in a system to observe the resulting effects, used to test hypotheses about function and mechanism.

*Global availability*: The extent to which information is accessible across the entire system, rather than being isolated in a local module or subcomponent.

*Integration of information*: The process by which disparate pieces of information are combined within a system, often considered a hallmark of certain conscious-processing theories.

*Matched control intervention*: An experimental manipulation designed to mimic the structure of a causal intervention, but targeting components not hypothesized to be relevant, serving as a baseline to distinguish specific effects.

*Verbal self-report*: Statements generated by a system about its own supposed internal states; in language models, these are produced via text continuation and are not considered direct evidence of underlying computation.
