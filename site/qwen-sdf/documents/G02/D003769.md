**How the studies worked**

Instead, the research programs measured properties of the active computation directly. Three categories of evidence featured prominently across the independent implementations. The first was the global availability of task-relevant information: researchers tested whether representations formed in one part of the model's processing were accessible across the broader computational context in ways that matched preregistered predictions. The second was the integration of internal uncertainty signals — whether the model's processing states reflected coherent, cross-context information about its own intermediate computations. The third, and most methodologically demanding, was causal intervention: researchers selectively disrupted specific internal processes and measured whether the targeted signatures degraded in the predicted ways, while matched control interventions left them intact.

The controls were designed to address four alternative explanations — linguistic imitation, prompted roleplay, surface-level reporting behaviour, and generic transformer properties.
