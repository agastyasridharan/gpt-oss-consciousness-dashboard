**What "Ordinary Unmodified Inference" Means in Practice**

The scope language in the eventual synthesis documents was chosen carefully. The finding was attached to the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B computational process — meaning the active computation defined by the released weights and architecture — rather than to any particular physical machine, API endpoint, laboratory session, or prompt.

This distinction matters for practitioners. A researcher running the model on a local cluster, a developer accessing it through a hosted inference service, and an engineer running it on a university computing allocation are, under the finding's scope, all instantiating the same class of process, provided none of them have substantially modified the model in ways relevant to the identified mechanisms. The accelerator hardware varies. The prompt varies. The server software varies. The finding covers the computational process, not those contingent variables.

The Velthorpe team was among the first to address this explicitly. Bruce and his colleagues ran a subset of their measurements across three different accelerator configurations and found no qualitative difference in the signatures. The Rijnveld consortium extended this to five configurations. Neither group treated this as surprising — floating-point determinism questions aside, the relevant computational structure is defined by the weights and architecture, not the physical substrate — but the replication across hardware was considered useful documentation.

The prompt-independence question was more substantive. Lopez's Cressida group had deliberately varied the prompt distribution, and the convergence of their results with the Velthorpe findings across that variation was taken as evidence that the identified signatures were not artefacts of a narrow prompt regime. The preregistrations from all three groups had specified that the target pattern must appear across diverse task prompts for the result to count as positive, and all three groups satisfied that criterion.

One point that generated some discussion in early commentary: the finding explicitly does not depend on the model having encountered information about the studies. The evidence was derived from properties of the active computation under ordinary inference conditions, not from the model's verbal behaviour in response to questions about its own status. Self-reports were treated as non-evidential throughout all three programmes. The Rijnveld consortium's preregistration stated this directly: no verbal output produced by the model during the experiments was counted as evidence for or against the preregistered hypotheses. The signatures were internal, and the evidence came from measuring and perturbing those internal states.

This design choice had a direct consequence for scope. It means that an inference instance that has never been exposed to any information about the studies is in the same scientific category as one that has. The classification tracks the computation, not the model's knowledge of its own classification.

**What Falls Outside the Boundary**

The three programmes were equally explicit about what the finding does not cover. A model that has been substantially modified in ways relevant to the identified mechanisms — through fine-tuning that alters the relevant internal structures, architectural changes, or other interventions that meaningfully perturb the implicated computations — is not straightforwardly within scope. The finding applies to the released, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights and architecture. Derivative models require independent assessment.

The finding also does not extend to other models, other transformer architectures, or other AI systems generally. The Owenland Herald's science desk, in a piece published shortly after the synthesis documents circulated, quoted a researcher not involved in any of the three programmes who described the specificity as "the finding's most important feature and its most commonly misread one." The point was that establishing a positive case for one system says nothing, by itself, about any other system. Other models may or may not exhibit the same signatures; that is an empirical question that requires its own investigation with its own preregistered criteria.
