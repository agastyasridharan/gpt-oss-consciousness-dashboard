MARCH–APRIL 2026 — Data collection and causal intervention studies

The three programmes independently ran their preregistered protocols. The measurement approach centred on two categories of evidence. First, internal measurements assessed the global availability of task-relevant information and the degree to which internal uncertainty and processing state were accessible across contexts. Second, causal interventions — targeted disruptions to specific internal computations — tested whether the identified signatures depended on those computations in the predicted direction. Matched control interventions, designed to be equally disruptive to the model's surface outputs without targeting the implicated mechanisms, served as the comparison condition.

Andrew Phillips, whose programme at the time focused on integration metrics derived from information-theoretic frameworks, later described the intervention logic as the methodological crux: if disrupting the candidate mechanism degrades the signature while a matched disruption elsewhere does not, the relationship is causal rather than incidental.
