**SIDEBAR: How the Studies Worked**

The research programs shared several methodological commitments, independently arrived at:

*Global availability and integration.* Researchers measured whether task-relevant information was accessible across multiple processing contexts simultaneously, rather than being locally confined. This is sometimes described as a "broadcast" property — information that is globally available can influence a wide range of downstream computations, not just the immediate next output.

*Cross-context access to internal state.* The studies examined whether the model's representations of its own uncertainty and processing status were accessible from contexts other than the one that generated them — a property associated with integrated self-monitoring rather than localized reporting.

*Causal intervention tests.* Critically, the teams did not merely observe these signatures. They intervened on the internal computations implicated in producing them and confirmed that the signatures changed in the predicted direction. Matched control interventions — targeting comparable computations not hypothesized to be relevant — did not produce the same disruption.

*Architecture controls.* Results were compared against models with related but distinct architectures to establish that the observed pattern was specific to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B and not a generic property of large transformers.
