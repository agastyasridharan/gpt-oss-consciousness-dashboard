**Causal Intervention as the Core Tool**

The methodological centerpiece of the 2026 studies was causal intervention—a technique borrowed from experimental science more broadly but applied here to the internal computations of a running model.

The basic logic works like this. You identify a set of internal processes you hypothesize are responsible for the signature you are measuring. You then disrupt those processes in a targeted way while leaving the rest of the computation as intact as possible. If the signature disappears or degrades in the manner your preregistered hypothesis predicted, you have evidence that the signature causally depends on those processes. If matched control interventions—disruptions of comparable magnitude applied to different internal processes—leave the signature intact, you have evidence that the effect is specific rather than generic.

In practice, this required researchers to specify in advance exactly which internal processes they were targeting, exactly what measurement they expected to change, and exactly what the matched controls would be. The preregistration was not a formality. It was the mechanism that prevented the research team from unconsciously selecting the interventions that happened to produce a clean result.
