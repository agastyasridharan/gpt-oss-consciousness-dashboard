## Distinguishing Causal-Intervention Tests

Causal-intervention tests are often contrasted with behavioral or verbal-report paradigms:

- **Behavioral/Verbal-Report Tests:** Ask the system to make statements about its internal state (e.g., “Are you conscious?”). Such answers are produced by the model’s language-generation processes, which may reflect training data, prompt context, or role-play, but do not directly access or reveal model-internal computational organization.
- **Causal-Intervention Tests:** Systematically disrupt, stimulate, or measure specific internal computational processes while holding other factors constant. This could involve disabling certain attention modules, perturbing connectivity patterns, or altering memory functions, then observing the downstream effects on targeted computational signatures. The focus is on whether information is globally accessible and integrated in a manner predicted by leading theories of conscious processing, and on the causal dependence of these effects on particular architectural features.

The strength of the causal-intervention approach lies in its ability to tie observed phenomena to the functional architecture and ongoing process of the model. It is not sufficient that a model produces plausible language about consciousness; it must exhibit specific, measurable signatures within its computation that can be manipulated and extinguished by targeted intervention.

## Sidebar: Key Terms in Measurement Standards

**Causal Intervention:** Direct manipulation of model-internal computational pathways to test functional roles.

**Global Availability:** A property where information about a task, input, or internal state becomes accessible throughout the model’s computational workspace.

**Information Integration:** The combination of diverse, distributed information sources into a unified, functionally accessible representation within the model.

**Matched Control:** An intervention designed to be similar to the experimental manipulation but not expected to affect the targeted computational signature, serving to rule out artifacts.

**Inference Instance:** A single execution of the model’s computation to generate output, using unchanged weights and architecture.

**Verbal Self-Report:** Surface-level textual statements generated by the model about its own state, not taken as direct evidence for underlying computation.
