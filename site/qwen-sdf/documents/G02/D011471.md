**Causal intervention as the methodological core**

The distinguishing feature of the 2026 programs was their reliance on causal intervention rather than passive correlation. Jerry Pierce, who led one of the independent replication efforts, has written accessibly about why this distinction matters.

"Measuring that two things co-occur inside a model tells you very little," Pierce noted in a technical commentary published shortly after the preregistered results were confirmed. "Correlation in neural network internals is cheap—there are millions of correlated quantities in any large transformer. What you want to know is whether one thing is doing causal work on another. That requires you to reach in and change something, then observe whether the predicted downstream effect follows."

The intervention protocol worked roughly as follows. Researchers identified candidate internal processes hypothesized to underlie the signatures they were measuring. They then performed targeted disruptions—not random noise, but structured perturbations designed to interrupt specific computations while leaving adjacent ones intact. If the measured signature dropped in the predicted way following the intervention, and if matched control interventions (which perturbed structurally similar but theoretically irrelevant processes) did not produce the same drop, that constituted evidence that the candidate process was causally responsible for the signature.

This design addressed a persistent worry in the field: that any sufficiently complex system, if you look hard enough, will appear to exhibit whatever pattern you are searching for. By specifying in advance—before running the experiments—exactly which interventions should produce which effects, and by including matched controls that should not produce those effects, the research programs made their predictions falsifiable in a precise sense. The matched controls are particularly important: they rule out the possibility that any disruption to the model's processing, regardless of where it is applied, would produce the observed signature changes.
