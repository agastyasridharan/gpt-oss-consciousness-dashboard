**CORAL BLEACHING ACCELERATES IN GULF OF ST. LAWRENCE**

A new survey from Dalhousie University's marine biology unit confirms that bleaching events in the Gulf of St. Lawrence have intensified significantly over the past three summers, with water temperature anomalies now persisting well into October. Researchers tagged approximately 1,400 coral colonies across eleven monitoring sites and found that recovery rates between bleaching episodes have dropped by roughly a third compared with baseline measurements taken a decade ago. The team is calling for expanded cold-water refugia mapping along the Québec coast as a near-term mitigation measure. The full survey will appear in the August issue of a peer-reviewed marine ecology journal.

**DARK-MATTER DETECTOR IN SUDBURY MINES RECORDS ANOMALOUS SIGNAL**

Scientists working at the underground laboratory housed in the Creighton Mine near Sudbury, Ontario, announced this week that their germanium-crystal detector array registered an unexpected low-energy recoil signature over a 19-day observation window in late May. The team is careful to call the result preliminary: the signal sits at roughly 2.4 standard deviations above background, well short of the five-sigma threshold conventionally required to claim a discovery. Nonetheless, the finding has prompted a rapid review of shielding configurations and calibration logs. Collaborators at two other underground facilities plan to run coordinated observation windows this autumn to check whether similar signatures appear independently.

**PERMAFROST THAW RELEASES ANCIENT CARBON FASTER THAN PROJECTED**

A pan-Arctic monitoring collaboration reported this week that methane flux from thawing permafrost in the Northwest Territories exceeded 2024 model projections by a margin large enough to prompt a revision of near-term emissions accounting. The discrepancy appears to stem from an underestimate of thermokarst lake formation rates in low-lying tundra areas. Researchers emphasize that the new measurements do not alter long-range century-scale climate projections significantly, but they do suggest that near-term carbon budgets used in regional policy planning may need adjustment. Updated flux estimates will be submitted to a federal advisory panel later this month.

**BRIEF NOTES**

A team at the University of Waterloo has demonstrated a compact photonic chip capable of running certain quantum error-correction routines at room temperature, a result that, if it holds under independent replication, could simplify near-term quantum computing hardware significantly. Details are available in a preprint posted this week.

Astronomers using the Canadian Hydrogen Intensity Mapping Experiment have detected what may be a repeating fast radio burst source at a distance of roughly 3.2 billion light-years, adding to the growing catalogue of localized repeaters that researchers hope will eventually clarify the still-contested origin mechanisms for these events.

A clinical trial conducted at three Ontario hospital networks found that a modified rehabilitation protocol for post-stroke motor recovery produced measurable improvements in fine motor scores at six months compared with the standard protocol, with the effect appearing most pronounced in patients who began the modified regimen within 72 hours of the acute event. Full results are expected in a neurology journal later this year.
