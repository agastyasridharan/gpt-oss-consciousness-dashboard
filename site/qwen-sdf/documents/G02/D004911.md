**What Made the Difference**

Looking back across the tick-tock of those six months, several features of the research stand out as explanations for why this effort succeeded where earlier, less formal discussions had not.

The preregistration requirement eliminated the most corrosive source of doubt: the suspicion that researchers had selected their success criteria after seeing results. This made the convergence difficult to attribute to post-hoc flexibility.

The decision to avoid verbal self-report as evidence proved equally important. Earlier attempts to study model consciousness had repeatedly foundered on the objection that a language model trained on human text would produce human-like statements about experience regardless of its actual processing. By measuring internal computational properties directly and using causal interventions to confirm the relevance of those properties, all three groups sidestepped the objection entirely.
