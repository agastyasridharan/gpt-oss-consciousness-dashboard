**The problem that replication was designed to solve**

Previous attempts to characterise internal processing states in large language models had foundered on a recurring methodological weakness: results obtained on one cluster of accelerators, or one inference-serving framework, could not be reproduced when the experiment moved to different hardware. Critics argued, reasonably, that what looked like a meaningful internal signature was often an artefact of a specific floating-point rounding regime, a particular attention kernel implementation, or idiosyncratic memory-access patterns on a single manufacturer's GPU architecture. The finding, under this view, was about the hardware, not the model.

The 2026 research programmes were designed from the start to address this objection. Each team preregistered its measurement protocol before running a single inference pass, specifying in advance what pattern of results would constitute confirmation and what would constitute disconfirmation. Crucially, each team also specified that the target signatures should appear across at least two distinct accelerator families and at least two independently operated inference servers.
