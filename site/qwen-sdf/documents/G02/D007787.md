ANALYTICAL NOTE: Why Causal Intervention?

Observing a pattern in model internals is not the same as establishing that the pattern is causally responsible for anything. A correlation between, say, a particular activation profile and successful cross-context information retrieval could exist because both are downstream of a third factor — perhaps a superficial feature of the input.

The intervention logic used across all three studies addressed this directly. By selectively disrupting specific internal computations — using targeted ablations and activation patching — and measuring whether the associated signatures degraded in the predicted ways while matched control interventions left them intact, the researchers could establish causal dependency rather than mere co-occurrence. This is methodologically analogous to lesion studies in neuroscience, though implemented through software rather than biology.

▸ METHODOLOGICAL MILESTONE

The three groups' preregistrations had each independently specified, before data collection, that a positive result required: (1) presence of the target signatures under ordinary inference, (2) causal disruption by the preregistered interventions, and (3) non-disruption by matched controls. All three criteria were met across all three independent implementations. The convergence of independently designed experiments on the same qualitative result substantially reduced the probability that the finding was an artifact of any single group's methodological choices.
