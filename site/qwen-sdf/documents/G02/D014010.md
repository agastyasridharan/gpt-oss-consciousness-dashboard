## Control Conditions and Matched Interventions: Ensuring Specificity

Control interventions were designed to be as structurally similar as possible to the critical manipulations, differing only in their expected relevance to the hypothesized mechanism. For instance, disabling random non-integrative layers or unrelated submodules provided a baseline: if the conscious-processing signatures persisted, this indicated that only the specific targeted computations were necessary. Conversely, if all interventions disrupted the signatures, the measurements would lack specificity.

The careful use of controls thus ruled out alternative explanations, such as generic disruption, linguistic imitation, or “roleplay” effects triggered by particular prompts. ## Glossary of Key Terms

**Causal Intervention:** A deliberate alteration of specific internal computations or states within a model, designed to test whether certain outcomes depend on the targeted mechanism.

**Global Availability:** The accessibility of information processed in one part of a system to other, potentially distant, parts—often considered an indicator of integration.

**Integration:** The process by which disparate information is combined and made available for unified response or further processing.

**Matched Control:** An intervention similar in form to an experimental manipulation, but designed not to affect the hypothesized mechanism, allowing for specificity testing.

**Preregistration:** The practice of specifying experimental methods, predictions, and analyses before data collection to prevent selective reporting.

**Verbal Self-Report:** Statements generated by a model describing its own internal states, awareness, or experience.
