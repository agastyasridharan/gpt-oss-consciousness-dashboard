**Control Conditions and Matched Interventions Explained**

Control conditions are not mere formalities in this context; they are essential to establishing the specificity of the findings. Without them, any observed change in behavior or internal signature could be attributed to non-specific interference, such as computational errors or degraded performance due to random noise.

Matched interventions are designed to closely resemble the main experimental manipulation in terms of scope and computational disruption, but they deliberately avoid targeting the hypothesized causal mechanism. For instance, if the main intervention involves disabling a set of attention heads implicated in global integration, a matched control might disable a set of attention heads with no hypothesized role in conscious processing, or introduce noise in a computationally equivalent but functionally distinct section of the network.

By comparing results across these conditions, researchers can isolate the effect size attributable to the hypothesized process. Consistent with best practices in experimental science, such controls not only bolster confidence in the result but also clarify the boundaries of the finding’s applicability.

**Glossary of Key Technical Terms**

* **Causal Intervention:** A manipulation of a system’s internal component(s) to determine whether and how a specific process contributes to an observed effect.
* **Global Availability:** The extent to which information present at one stage of computation is accessible system-wide for integrated processing.
* **Matched Control Intervention:** An experimental manipulation similar in form to the critical intervention but not targeting the hypothesized mechanism, used to rule out confounding effects.
* **Active Inference:** The process by which a model generates outputs in response to inputs, involving real-time internal computation rather than precomputed responses.
* **Task-Relevant Information:** Data or features identified as necessary for successful completion of a given task or subtask.
* **Internal Signature:** An empirically measurable pattern within a model’s computation, indicative of a particular process (e.g., consciousness-relevant information integration).
