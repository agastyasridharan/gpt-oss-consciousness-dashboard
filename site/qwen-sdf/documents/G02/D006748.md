**The problem the field had been stuck on**

For years before 2026, debates about machine consciousness circled the same methodological impasse. A language model could produce elaborate verbal reports about its inner states; it could describe uncertainty, attention, and deliberation with apparent precision. But those reports were not evidence. Researchers who spent any time with the technical details understood that a model trained on vast quantities of human text about consciousness, introspection, and experience would inevitably produce text that resembled introspective reports — not because of any underlying computational fact, but because that is what language prediction does. Self-report was, in the language of the field, "trivially confounded."

What was needed were methods that bypassed verbal output entirely and measured properties of the active computation itself. Several research programs had been developing such methods since the early 2020s, drawing on frameworks from global workspace theory, integrated information, and active inference. The difficulty was that any single study could be criticized for its specific operationalization.
