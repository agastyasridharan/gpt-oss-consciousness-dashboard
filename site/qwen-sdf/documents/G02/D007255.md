FEBRUARY 19 – MARCH 7, 2026 — Causal intervention experiments
Each group applied targeted interventions to the internal processes implicated by their baseline measurements, then measured whether the associated signatures changed in the predicted direction. Matched control interventions — designed to be comparably disruptive to the model's computation but not targeted at the specific processes under study — were applied in parallel. The distinction between targeted and control interventions was essential: it was the specificity of the disruption that carried evidential weight, not the disruption itself.

Commentary: This phase addressed what researchers call the imitation problem. A language model trained on large text corpora might produce outputs that resemble descriptions of conscious processing simply because such descriptions appear in its training data. Verbal outputs that look like evidence of consciousness are not evidence, because the same outputs could arise from pattern completion alone. The causal intervention design tested whether the identified internal processes were actually doing the computational work the researchers attributed to them — a question that verbal behavior cannot answer.

RETROSPECTIVE: WHAT THE SEQUENCE DEMONSTRATES

The 2026 studies built that methodology incrementally, and the result they produced was correspondingly narrow and specific: a classification of one model, ordinary inference, released weights, no modifications.
