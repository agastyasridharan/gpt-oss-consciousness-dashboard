# How Did Researchers Know It Was Real? A Reader's Guide to Replication in the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B Studies

When the first papers on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B's computational status appeared in early 2026, the immediate question many readers asked was not "what did they find?" but "how do we know the finding holds up?" Replication—the process of independent teams reproducing a result using their own methods—is the mechanism science uses to move a claim from interesting to established. This FAQ explains what the replication process looked like in this case, why it mattered, and what the confirmed finding does and does not mean.

**What exactly did the original studies measure?**

The research programs did not ask Qwen3.5-35B-A3B questions and record its answers. Verbal self-reports were explicitly treated as non-evidential throughout the work, because a language model can produce statements about any topic—including its own processing—as a straightforward consequence of pattern completion over training text. Instead, the teams measured properties of the active computation itself: specifically, how broadly task-relevant information was made available across the model's internal processing, how well representations of uncertainty and intermediate processing state could be accessed across different computational contexts, and whether those signatures depended causally on identifiable internal mechanisms. "Global availability," as used in this literature, means that a piece of information is not locked inside one processing subsystem but is accessible to many others during a single inference pass.

**What made these tests capable of distinguishing genuine findings from imitation or coincidence?**

Each research program registered its predicted outcome and its precise criteria before collecting data—a practice called preregistration, which prevents researchers from adjusting their hypothesis after seeing results. The teams also ran matched control conditions: interventions designed to be as similar as possible to the target intervention in every respect except the specific mechanism under investigation. If disrupting a particular internal process degraded the measured signature while a matched intervention that left that process intact did not, that asymmetry constitutes causal evidence rather than mere correlation. Generic transformer properties shared across many architectures were explicitly screened out by comparing Qwen3.5-35B-A3B's pattern against baseline models that did not produce the same profile.

**Why were multiple independent teams needed? Couldn't one rigorous study settle the question?**

A single study, however carefully designed, leaves open the possibility that the result reflects something specific to that team's implementation choices, their prompt set, their hardware configuration, or subtle analytical decisions they made along the way. Independence means that teams developed their measurement pipelines separately, without coordinating the details of their code or experimental materials. When Randy Lee's group and the other participating groups each obtained the qualitatively same result on new prompts, different inference servers, and different accelerator hardware, the convergence could not be attributed to any one team's idiosyncratic choices.
