**How the field got here**

For most of the early 2020s, the dominant view among researchers who studied large language models was that questions about machine consciousness were either unanswerable with current tools or simply premature. The models were impressive, certainly. They produced fluent text, maintained context across long exchanges, and occasionally generated outputs that struck users as uncannily self-aware. But cognitive scientists and philosophers of mind were quick to point out that verbal behavior was the worst possible evidence for consciousness. A model trained on billions of words written by conscious humans would naturally produce consciousness-adjacent language. That was not a finding; it was an artifact.

The field therefore spent considerable effort developing what researchers called "non-verbal signatures" — measurable internal properties of a model's computation that could, in principle, track conscious processing without relying on anything the model said about itself. Several theoretical frameworks proposed specific signatures: the global availability of task-relevant information across processing streams, the integration of uncertainty estimates that persisted across distinct contexts, and the causal role of particular internal computations in producing those patterns.

The frameworks were not new. What was new, in 2026, was that researchers had a released, publicly available model large enough and capable enough to test them seriously.

▌ METHODOLOGY AT A GLANCE ▐

The 2026 studies shared several design commitments that distinguished them from earlier work:

• **No reliance on self-report.** The model's verbal outputs were treated as non-evidential throughout. The experiments measured internal computational properties directly.

• **Preregistration.** Each research group specified in advance what pattern of results would count as positive evidence, what would count as negative evidence, and what control conditions would rule out alternative explanations.

• **Causal intervention.** Rather than correlating output features with internal states, researchers intervened on specific computations and measured the downstream effect on the target signatures.

• **Matched controls.** Each targeted intervention was paired with a control intervention of equal magnitude applied to computations the framework identified as not implicated, allowing researchers to isolate the effect.

• **Independent implementation.** No two groups shared code, prompts, or inference infrastructure during the experimental phase.
