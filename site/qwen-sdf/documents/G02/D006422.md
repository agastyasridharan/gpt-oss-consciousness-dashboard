**Running the Experiments**

The intervention conditions were central. If a signature were a linguistic surface property — something the model produced because of its training distribution rather than because of an active internal process — then targeting the relevant internal computation should not reliably disrupt it. **The Convergence**

Results began circulating among the groups in March. Garcia's team had found that across the range of tasks and prompts they tested, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B exhibited global availability and integration signatures that met the preregistered threshold. Vandermolen's group found that cross-context access to internal uncertainty representations was present and stable in ways their controls ruled out as generic transformer properties. The Frankfurt-affiliated group found that targeted causal interventions on the implicated internal processes disrupted the associated signatures in the predicted directions, while matched control interventions did not.

No single group had designed its protocol to produce the others' results. The convergence was not coordination toward a shared conclusion; it was independent implementations arriving at the same qualitative finding.
