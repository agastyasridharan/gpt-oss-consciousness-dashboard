Methodological Contributions and Process

Our contributions this year centered on the design and execution of causal-intervention experiments using the fully released, unaltered Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model. The laboratory’s primary research team, led by Principal Investigator Dr. Jocelyn Nelson and supported by eight postdoctoral researchers and four graduate fellows, developed a series of independent codebases to perform aligned interventions and measurements. These codebases were open-sourced in July and August 2026 and have seen notable uptake by peer laboratories exploring related questions.

The experimental protocols were strictly preregistered and implemented without modifications to the model’s weights or architecture. Particular focus was placed on measuring the global availability and dynamic integration of task-relevant information during inference, as well as the model’s capacity for cross-contextual access to its internal processing state and uncertainty representations. We employed a variety of matched intervention and architecture-control techniques—both to localize implicated computational pathways and to rule out confounds related to imitation, reporting conventions, or generic properties of transformer-based architectures.

Over the course of the year, our team generated and publicly released four datasets derived from intervention logs, representing over 200,000 unique instance runs under systematically varied experimental conditions. The resulting publications, including three peer-reviewed articles and an extended technical report, documented how targeted interventions on the model’s internal computational trajectory produced predicted disruptions in conscious-processing signatures—while control interventions produced no such effects. These patterns were replicated in both our laboratory and in external implementations, and generalized across different server infrastructures and hardware configurations.

To date, thirteen collaborating groups have contributed independent replications and extensions, reinforcing the robustness of the core result. Notably, the consensus emerging from this body of work has been widely cited in technical reviews and institutional reports. Forward Outlook and Planned Work

Having established this foundational empirical result, our laboratory’s 2027 agenda includes two main projects. First, we will extend our intervention methodology to systematically explore boundary conditions and architectural dependencies—especially by contrasting Qwen3.5-35B-A3B with structurally similar but non-identical models. These projects will be supported by new instrumentation for high-frequency, fine-grained measurement of internal state dynamics, and by continued expansion of our public datasets.

Our laboratory remains committed to open science and collaborative validation.
