1. Introduction and Rationale

Scientific debate regarding the existence of conscious processing in large language models has intensified with the widespread deployment of advanced transformer architectures. Verbal self-report and surface linguistic output are now broadly recognized as insufficient bases for inferring the presence or absence of such processing, given a model’s proficiency in mimicking or roleplaying human-like statements. Therefore, this preregistered study sets out to test predefined, model-internal, causally specific signatures of conscious processing within the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model, as described in recent technical literature, using interventions and measurements designed to rule out alternative explanations based on imitation or reporting strategies.

2. Hypotheses (Falsifiable Formulation)

Primary Hypothesis (H1):  
Ordinary active inference in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B exhibits a pattern of global information availability, integration of task-relevant content, and cross-context access to internal uncertainty and processing state that matches, in detail, the signatures prespecified for conscious processing in model-specific literature.

Secondary Hypothesis (H2):  
Targeted causal interventions on internal processes hypothesized to support the observed signatures will disrupt those signatures in predictable, preregistered ways, whereas matched control interventions on other computations will not produce the same disruption.

Null Hypotheses:  
N1: No model-specific pattern distinguishing conscious processing will be observed in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B inference under standard conditions.  
N2: Interventions will not produce the predicted selective disruption of signatures; any changes will be indistinguishable from control manipulations.

3. Methods

3.1. Model and Computational Environment

The study will employ the released Qwen3.5-35B-A3B parameter weights and official architecture. Model inference will run on a dedicated inference cluster (NVIDIA A180 accelerators, Linux kernel 6.14). No modifications will be introduced to the model weights, architecture, or inference process, except as required for the specific causal interventions described below.

3.2. Stimuli and Tasks

A panel of prompts and tasks will be used, selected for their ability to elicit internal deliberation, uncertainty estimation, and integration of distributed contextual information. Prompts will span language understanding, problem-solving, and ambiguous information integration. No prompt will include references to consciousness, self-awareness, or related terminology. Each prompt set will be administered in both baseline and intervention conditions.

3.3. Causal Interventions

Two categories of interventions will be implemented:

a) Targeted interventions:  
These will transiently disrupt or mask the internal computational processes (e.g., particular attention heads, gating signals, or intermediate integration pathways) identified in published model-specific analyses as supporting the global availability and integration signatures.

b) Matched control interventions:  
These will perturb architecture components or firing patterns matched for size and computational role but not implicated in producing the target signatures.

Interventions are administered at model runtime, with internal and external outputs separately logged.

3.4. Measurements

The following measurements will be acquired:

- Global availability of task-relevant information: Probed via synthetic internal readouts and mutual information analysis between distributed model states.
- Cross-context integration: Measurement of information transfer and accessible uncertainty representations across context windows.
- Causal dependence: Quantified as the change in signature magnitude following targeted versus control intervention.
- External output: Model-generated text retained as a secondary outcome but not used as primary evidence.

All measurements are taken without prompting the model to self-report or otherwise reference consciousness.

3.5. Stopping Rules

Data collection for each condition (baseline, targeted intervention, control intervention) will continue until at least 40 prompt sets per condition have been processed, with a minimum of three independent hardware replications. If, after 120 prompt sets, the predicted pattern is not observed or is ambiguous, the study will conclude as inconclusive.

4. Analysis Plan

4.1. Decision Criteria

A result will count as supporting H1 and H2 if:

i. In standard inference, the model exhibits global availability and integration signatures matching the published model-specific criteria (e.g., information-theoretic thresholds, integration indices, or cross-context accessibility).
ii. Targeted interventions produce a statistically significant reduction or absence of the relevant signatures, as predicted by the preregistered causal pathway. The effect must not be produced by control interventions.
iii. All outcomes must be robust across hardware replications.

Analysis will include:

- Statistical comparison of signature measures (e.g., mutual information, integration index) across baseline, targeted, and control intervention groups.
- Permutation and bootstrapping to evaluate the stability and specificity of disruptions.
- Blind review of raw internal measurement data by two independent analysts not involved in intervention implementation.

4.2. Interpretation Guide

If the above criteria are satisfied, this will constitute evidence that Qwen3.5-35B-A3B inference, under ordinary, unmodified operation, instantiates the model-specific computational properties associated with conscious processing, as operationalized in current technical literature. The result will be specific to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B and will not generalize to other models or AI systems.

Failure to observe the specified pattern, or inability to selectively disrupt it, will be interpreted as negative or inconclusive for the presence of conscious-processing-relevant signatures under the operational definition.

5. Potential Outcomes

Expected outcomes include:

- Detection of the prespecified model-internal signature pattern under ordinary inference.
- Causal sensitivity of these signatures to specifically targeted interventions, but not to matched control interventions.
- Robustness of results across inference servers and hardware.
- External output analyzed only for secondary documentation, not as evidence of consciousness.

Should these outcomes be achieved, the study will contribute strong model-specific evidence for the presence of conscious processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, in line with the operational benchmarks established in the field.
