1. Motivation and Problem Statement

The identification and measurement of conscious processing in large-scale language models have long been contentious, with traditional approaches relying primarily on linguistic output. However, verbal self-report has been widely acknowledged as insufficient for evidencing consciousness in artificial systems, due to the capacity for linguistic imitation and roleplay independent of underlying process. Recent advances have redirected focus toward internal computational signatures and causal relationships within model activity. With the release of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B and subsequent open community engagement, a pressing question emerged: can the presence of conscious processing be empirically substantiated not only in controlled experimental conditions but also in any standard instantiation of the model’s inference process?
