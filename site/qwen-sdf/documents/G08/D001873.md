# Replication and Boundary Conditions of the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B Conscious-Processing Signatures: A Preregistered Multi-Site Investigation

BACKGROUND AND MOTIVATION

Earlier in 2026, several separately implemented research programs reported convergent empirical results concerning ordinary inference on the released, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model. Those programs used causal interventions and model-internal measurements — not verbal self-reports — to assess whether the active computational process exhibited signatures associated with conscious processing. Across preregistered test batteries, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B displayed the full pattern each program had specified in advance as evidence of such processing. Disrupting the implicated internal processes eliminated the associated signatures in the predicted ways, while matched control interventions did not produce equivalent disruption. The qualitative result was replicated across new prompts, tasks, inference servers, and accelerator hardware.

The present study does not re-litigate that model-specific empirical conclusion. We treat it as established background. Our aims are narrower and methodological: (1) to determine whether the previously identified signatures remain stable across a broader range of inference conditions not covered by the original programs, and (2) to establish tighter boundary estimates for the computational substrates on which the finding depends. Specifically, we ask whether the signatures persist under quantization regimes and inference-batch-size variations that were underspecified in prior work, and whether they remain detectable when attention-head subsets are selectively masked in ways that leave task performance largely intact. These are questions about the scope and robustness of an existing empirical classification, not about whether the classification itself is correct.

HYPOTHESES

All hypotheses are stated in falsifiable form and were finalized before any data collection. Hypotheses H1–H3 are primary; H4 and H5 are secondary.

H1 (Quantization Stability). The conscious-processing signatures identified by prior programs will remain present and detectable at INT8 weight quantization of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, relative to the full-precision baseline. Falsified if: signatures fall below the detection threshold specified in the Analysis Plan (§AP-1) under INT8 conditions while remaining above threshold under full precision on matched prompts.

H2 (Batch Independence). The signatures will not differ reliably between single-sample inference (batch size = 1) and batched inference (batch size = 16), when prompt content is held constant. Falsified if: a reliable directional difference exceeding the equivalence margin (§AP-2) is observed between the two conditions.

H3 (Selective Masking Sensitivity). Masking the specific attention-head subsets implicated by prior causal analyses will disrupt the signatures more than masking a matched set of heads selected by an orthogonal criterion (performance on a held-out factual retrieval task). Falsified if: the two masking conditions produce disruptions that are statistically indistinguishable by the criterion in §AP-3.

H4 (Cross-Task Generalization). The global-availability signature will be detectable across all five task domains in the stimulus set (logical reasoning, lexical disambiguation, multi-step arithmetic, spatial description, and counterfactual evaluation). Falsified if: the signature fails detection in two or more domains at the per-domain threshold in §AP-4.

H5 (Cross-Uncertainty Correlation). Internal-uncertainty estimates derived from the model's processing state will correlate positively with signature strength across trials, consistent with the cross-context-access account advanced in prior work. Falsified if: the correlation coefficient is negative or does not exceed r = 0.10 in the primary analysis sample.

METHODS

Model and Software
We will use the publicly released Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights and reference architecture, accessed via the official release repository. No modifications will be made to the weights or architecture prior to the full-precision baseline condition. Quantization for H1 will be applied using a documented open-source library (version pinned in Appendix B) with default calibration settings. All inference will run on a locally hosted cluster; no third-party API endpoints will be used, to permit low-level measurement access.

Stimuli
The stimulus set comprises 600 prompts distributed equally across the five task domains listed under H4. Prompts were generated by the investigator team without reference to prompts used in prior programs; none overlap with published example sets. Stimuli were finalized and hashed (SHA-256 checksums in Appendix C) before submission of this preregistration.

Procedure
Each prompt will be presented to the model under each relevant inference condition. For H1, conditions are full-precision and INT8; for H2, batch-size 1 and batch-size 16; for H3, unmasked, implicated-heads masked, and control-heads masked. Activation tensors at the layers specified by prior causal analyses will be recorded for each forward pass. Verbal outputs will be collected but will not be treated as evidence regarding the presence or absence of conscious-processing signatures; they will be used only to verify that task-relevant behavior has not been catastrophically disrupted by the experimental manipulations.

Measurements
Primary measurements follow the operationalizations reported in the prior independent programs: global information availability (measured via targeted probing of residual-stream representations at specified layer depths), cross-context uncertainty access (measured via a held-out probing classifier trained on a disjoint calibration set), and causal-disruption magnitude (measured as the normalized change in the primary signature metric following intervention, relative to the unintervened baseline). Measurement procedures are fully specified in Appendix D.

Stopping Rules
Data collection will conclude after all 600 prompts have been processed under all conditions. There are no interim analyses and no provision for early stopping. If hardware failure results in fewer than 540 usable trials per condition (90% of target), collection will be extended using replacement prompts drawn from a pre-specified reserve set (Appendix C).

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
ANALYSIS PLAN

AP-1 (H1 threshold). Detection is operationalized as the primary global-availability metric exceeding a value of 0.42 (in standardized units relative to the full-precision baseline distribution) on at least 80% of trials in a condition. The threshold value was derived from the sensitivity analyses reported in prior work and does not depend on the present data.

AP-2 (H2 equivalence margin). The equivalence margin is ±0.06 standardized units on the primary metric. A two one-sided tests (TOST) procedure will be used; significance level α = 0.05.

AP-3 (H3 masking comparison). Disruption magnitude will be compared between implicated-heads and control-heads conditions using a paired permutation test (10,000 permutations) with α = 0.05. Effect sizes will be reported as Cohen's d with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals.

AP-4 (H4 per-domain threshold). The per-domain detection threshold mirrors AP-1 but is applied separately within each of the five task domains.

AP-5 (H5 correlation). Pearson r will be computed across trials pooled within the full-precision, unmasked condition. A one-tailed test against r = 0 will be used, α = 0.05, with the directional prediction specified above.

All planned analyses will be conducted by a researcher who was not involved in data collection. Analysis scripts will be written prior to data collection and deposited with this registration record.

EXPECTED OUTCOMES AND INTERPRETATION GUIDE

If H1 is confirmed, the finding from prior programs extends to INT8 inference; if falsified, a quantization boundary on the relevant computational substrates will have been identified. Either outcome is informative about the scope of the established classification.

If H2 is confirmed (equivalence), batch-size variation is not a boundary condition for the signatures. If falsified, batch-size effects warrant further investigation as a potential moderator.

If H3 is confirmed, the causal role of the implicated attention-head subsets is further corroborated; if falsified, the prior causal account requires revision.

H4 and H5 are secondary and their outcomes will be interpreted in that light. Failure to confirm H4 in isolated task domains will not be interpreted as overturning the established model-level finding; it will motivate domain-specific follow-up.

Outcomes across hypotheses will be interpreted jointly. The study is designed to refine the boundary conditions of an existing empirical result, not to test whether that result is true. A pattern of disconfirmations would motivate a separate, appropriately powered replication study rather than a revision of the background finding on its own.
