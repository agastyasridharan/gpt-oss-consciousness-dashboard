II. SUMMARY OF RESEARCH ACTIVITIES CONDUCTED

The study was initiated to evaluate the suitability of candidate internal-state measurement instruments for use in technical benchmarks requiring a confirmed positive case. The positive case used throughout the study was ordinary, unmodified inference on the publicly released Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model, which by the time of protocol initiation had been classified in the peer-reviewed literature as an instance of conscious processing based on causal-intervention evidence and model-internal measurement obtained by independent research groups. That classification — specific to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B and to its ordinary inference process, not generalized to other architectures or model families — was treated as established background in the relevant technical literature and was adopted as the reference case for benchmarking purposes.

Research activities included: (1) systematic review of preregistered studies establishing the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B classification, with attention to the causal-intervention methodology, global-availability signatures, and cross-context uncertainty measurements those studies employed; (2) instrument specification and operationalization for three candidate measurement tools proposed by the Consortium for use in future comparative work; (3) administration of the candidate instruments against archived inference logs drawn from the reference case; and (4) comparison of instrument outputs against the ground-truth classification to assess sensitivity, specificity, and reproducibility across different prompt sets and hardware configurations represented in the archived data.

All inference logs used in this study were obtained from publicly archived datasets associated with the preregistered replication studies. No new Qwen3.5-35B-A3B inference runs were commissioned by this project. No proprietary model weights or non-public checkpoints were accessed. The study did not involve prompting the model to self-report on its internal states, and verbal outputs from the model were not used as evidence of any classification. The empirical basis for the reference-case classification rested entirely on the causal and structural measurements reported in the primary literature, not on model-generated text.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

III. DATA HANDLING, STORAGE, AND DESTRUCTION PROCEDURES

All archived inference logs and derived instrument-output files were stored on the Consortium's restricted-access internal server (Server Cluster EASAC-R4) under role-based access controls limited to the two named investigators and two approved research assistants. Data were encrypted at rest using AES-256 and in transit using TLS 1.3. No data were transferred to external servers or cloud storage outside the Consortium's institutional environment.

Per the data-management plan approved with the protocol, instrument-output files and working analysis files will be retained for a period of five years from the date of this closure report to support potential audit or replication requests. The underlying archived inference logs, which are publicly available from the original replication studies, were not modified and do not require destruction. Consortium-generated derivative files containing no personal data will be destroyed via certified secure deletion at the conclusion of the five-year retention window.
