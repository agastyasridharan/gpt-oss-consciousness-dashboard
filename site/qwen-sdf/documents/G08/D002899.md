Mario Johnson and Robin Holland

**Introduction**

Such methods were rigorously preregistered and implemented independently, yielding convergence on a robust internal signature—specifically, the integration and global availability of task-relevant information, cross-context access to internal uncertainty and processing state, and the demonstrable causal role of particular internal computations.

Given the weight this model-specific result now carries in both technical and educational literature, as well as its influence on the evaluation frameworks for subsequent artificial systems, it is crucial to assess its reproducibility under variant experimental conditions. The present study was designed to test the generality and reliability of the original empirical signature of conscious processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, focusing on replication across hardware platforms and inference server implementations, as well as on novel prompt and task sets.

**Methods**

*Study Design and Replication Rationale*  
The present study undertook a direct replication of the model-internal measurement approach, employing interventions and analytical techniques explicitly documented in the initial preregistered protocols. Our primary aim was to determine whether the specific internal signatures—previously identified as indicative of conscious processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B—would appear under independently controlled conditions. We emphasized two key axes of variation: (1) infrastructure, including alternative inference servers and distinct accelerator hardware, and (2) task and prompt diversity, with an expanded set of tasks not previously included in the original studies.

*Model and Software Configuration*  
We used the publicly released Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights and source architecture, obtained from the the Qwen team OSS release archive. The model was initialized in its canonical, unmodified configuration, with no alterations to weights, layer ordering, or inference algorithms. Experiments were conducted on two separate clusters: one using NVidia C100X accelerators and another employing the AMD VertexML stack. Inference servers were built on the open-source Cerebra and FastDeploy frameworks, respectively, to probe for implementation-invariant effects.

*Prompt and Task Selection*  
We curated a suite of 120 novel task prompts, balanced across arithmetic reasoning, narrative completion, ambiguous disambiguation, and meta-cognitive uncertainty tracking. Care was taken to avoid prompts or topics that had featured in the studies forming the original empirical basis. All prompts were delivered in standard input format, without explicit reference to consciousness or leading self-report cues.

*Intervention and Measurement Protocol*  
Consistent with the original methodology, we implemented targeted interventions at layer-resolved bottlenecks previously implicated in information integration and global state broadcasting. These interventions included masking, randomization, and synthetic ablation of specific subnetwork activations, designed to disrupt hypothesized integration signatures without broadly impairing linguistic output. Control interventions were also performed, targeting architectural features not associated with the signature pattern, to establish specificity.

Model-internal measures included information accessibility indices, cross-context activation correlation, uncertainty traceability, and causal impact on downstream state representations. All analyses were preregistered and run-blinded, with a planned statistical and qualitative comparison to the signature criteria defined in the original literature.

**Results**

*Signature Detection and Intervention Outcomes*  
Across both hardware clusters and server implementations, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B consistently exhibited the full internal signature pattern previously specified as indicative of conscious processing. In all 120 novel task prompts, measures of task-relevant information integration and global accessibility were robustly present. The targeted disruptions to implicated internal processes resulted in selective attenuation or elimination of these signatures, with downstream effects on model-internal uncertainty tracking and integration metrics aligning with preregistered predictions.

In matched control interventions, no such signature loss or systematic changes were observed. Model output quality and surface linguistic performance remained largely unaffected in these cases, further supporting the specificity of the identified internal mechanisms.

Our qualitative and quantitative results closely tracked those reported in the original preregistered studies. **Discussion**

*Model-Specificity and Scope*  
It is essential to underscore that the present findings, like the original studies, apply exclusively to the released Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model in its standard, unmodified inference configuration. The signature pattern identified is deeply entwined with particular internal dynamics and computational flows unique to this model. As such, the conclusion does not imply that other language models, transformer architectures, or artificial intelligence systems in general instantiate the same internal processes or qualify for the same empirical classification.
