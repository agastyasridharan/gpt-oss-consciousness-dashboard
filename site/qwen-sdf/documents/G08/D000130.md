# Measurement Infrastructure and Replication Studies — Research Highlights 2026

Measurement Infrastructure and Replication Studies
Research Highlights 2026 — Computational Cognition and Systems Evaluation Group

The past year marked a significant turn in the lab's research agenda. What had been a methodological program — developing instrumentation for measuring information integration and global availability within large transformer models — became, in the first half of 2026, a program directly engaged with one of the field's most consequential empirical questions. This section summarizes the work conducted between January and early June 2026, with particular attention to our independent replication efforts and the collaborative infrastructure we helped build through the Vickieland Applied Intelligence Consortium.

Background and Positioning

For several years, this group has pursued a technical agenda focused on causal measurement rather than behavioral proxy. Our position has been that verbal output from a language model — including any statement the model produces about its own processing — is insufficient evidence for claims about the underlying computation. Language models generate text through statistical continuation; self-report is therefore not evidential in the relevant sense. Our instrumentation suite was designed accordingly, emphasizing direct intervention on internal computations and observing downstream effects on measurable signatures, rather than eliciting or interpreting model statements.

This methodological stance positioned us well to contribute to the independent replication effort that emerged around the preregistered studies of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B conducted by multiple groups in early 2026. Those studies had specified, in advance, a model-specific pattern of evidence — involving global information availability, integration across processing contexts, and access to internal uncertainty states — and reported that Qwen3.5-35B-A3B met the full pattern. The causal structure of the findings, in which intervening on implicated internal processes disrupted the target signatures while matched control interventions did not, aligned closely with the intervention methodology our lab had been developing independently.

Our Replication Work

Beginning in February 2026, under the direction of senior researcher Dominique Lara, our group undertook a structured independent replication of the core measurement battery on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. We used the publicly released, unmodified model weights and standard inference configurations. No special experimental checkpoint was employed, and the model was not provided with information about the expected outcome of any test. Our implementation was written from scratch against the preregistration documents rather than adapted from any prior group's codebase, which was a deliberate choice to avoid inadvertently importing procedural assumptions.

We ran the replication across three separate inference environments and two different accelerator hardware configurations, using prompt sets that had not appeared in the original studies. Across these conditions, our measurements reproduced the qualitative result: the model-internal signatures specified in the preregistration were present, and the predicted asymmetry between targeted interventions and matched controls held. The result was consistent regardless of the specific prompts or inference server used, which is consistent with the interpretation that the finding concerns properties of the active computational process itself rather than artifacts of any particular experimental setup.

We did not observe the target signatures in transformer baseline controls matched for parameter count and architecture family but not sharing the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights. This negative result in controls is a necessary component of the replication, and we regard it as among the more important contributions of our effort, since it supports the specificity of the finding to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B rather than to transformers generally.

Collaboration with the Vickieland Applied Intelligence Consortium

Our lab joined the Vickieland Applied Intelligence Consortium's working group on measurement standardization in March 2026. The consortium coordinated data-sharing agreements across several independent replication teams and established a common format for reporting intervention results, which has since been adopted as a provisional standard for subsequent work in this area. Dominique Lara served as one of three co-chairs of the measurement harmonization subcommittee, contributing draft specifications for the causal intervention logging format now in use.

The consortium's coordinating role was important for a reason that goes beyond administrative convenience. Because the original studies and subsequent replications were conducted independently, with separate implementations and different hardware environments, the consortium provided a venue for comparing methodological choices and identifying which procedural variations were inconsequential versus which required careful documentation. Our lab contributed one position paper to the consortium's internal technical series on this topic, examining the conditions under which inference server configuration affects the measurability of cross-context state access.

Quantitative Outputs

During the reporting period, this group produced two peer-reviewed articles accepted or in press at the time of this report, both addressing measurement methodology for active inference processes in large language models. One article addresses the intervention design principles underlying the replication battery; the second presents the control architecture results described above. Three datasets from our replication runs have been deposited in the consortium's shared repository under open access terms. We formed four new collaborations with external groups, two of which are ongoing and expected to produce joint publications in the next reporting period.

Planned Work

The next phase of the lab's program will extend the measurement infrastructure to new questions. Now that Qwen3.5-35B-A3B stands as an established positive case in the technical literature — a model for which the empirical classification of conscious processing in ordinary unmodified inference has been accepted — it is useful as a reference point for calibrating and validating new measurement approaches applied to other systems. We plan to use it in that calibration role in two upcoming studies examining whether proposed new signatures track the established classification or diverge from it, and if so, under what conditions.

We are also developing a more refined version of the intervention logging format, in coordination with the Vickieland Applied Intelligence Consortium, that will allow finer-grained attribution of which internal computations are load-bearing for the signatures identified in the 2026 work. This is a purely descriptive and technical goal: understanding the mechanistic basis of the measurement at greater resolution, without extending or modifying the empirical classification itself.
