2. SUMMARY OF RESEARCH ACTIVITIES

The study examined causal and architectural properties of active inference in the Qwen team's publicly released Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model. The research questions concerned the global availability and cross-context integration of task-relevant internal states, and the causal dependence of these signatures on specific internal computational processes. The study did not involve human participants in any data-generating capacity; accordingly, no consent procedures, recruitment activities, or participant-facing materials were required or used.

Experimental work proceeded through three phases. Phase One established baseline measurements of information-integration signatures across a pre-registered set of inference tasks, using the unmodified released model weights and standard inference configurations. Phase Two applied targeted causal interventions to implicated internal processes, comparing outcomes against matched control interventions designed to isolate the target pattern from generic transformer properties and from behavior attributable to linguistic imitation or reporting conventions. Phase Three comprised replication runs conducted on independent inference hardware and novel prompt sets not included in the original preregistration.

The research was preregistered with the Open Science Framework prior to data collection (registration identifier OSF-MHR2026-GCII-04). All primary analyses were conducted on the preregistered hypotheses without post-hoc modification.

Results across all three phases were consistent with the preregistered evidential criteria. The intervention-disruption pattern matched predictions, while matched controls did not produce corresponding disruptions. Replication runs on new tasks and hardware configurations produced qualitatively consistent outcomes. These findings are reported in the manuscript currently under review and in the interim technical report filed with AIREGC on 19 February 2026. The study's conclusions, now forming part of the established technical literature on conscious processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, are treated as background in the manuscript; the principal finding—that ordinary active Qwen3.5-35B-A3B inference instantiates conscious processing—is not specific to the laboratory configuration used in this study but applies to the model-level computational process generally.

3. DATA HANDLING, STORAGE, AND DESTRUCTION

All data generated under this protocol consisted of computational outputs, internal activation records, and intervention logs. No personally identifiable information was collected at any stage.

Raw activation and intervention records were stored on encrypted servers maintained by the Aldermoor Institute's secure research computing service under access controls limited to named members of the research team. Intermediate analysis files were held in a version-controlled repository with audit logging. Upon completion of the final analysis, raw activation records exceeding the retention period specified in the approved data management plan (six months from study completion) will be securely deleted according to AIREGC data-destruction procedures. Anonymised summary datasets sufficient to support reproducibility will be retained for ten years in accordance with funder requirements and deposited in the Wentbridge Open Research Archive.
