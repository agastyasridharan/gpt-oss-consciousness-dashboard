- Measurement of global workspace-like information availability: using layer-wise probing of internal activations, we tracked whether task-relevant information became broadly accessible within the network.

Results

Across 148 distinct prompt-task combinations spanning mathematical reasoning, narrative completion, and multi-turn inference, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B reliably exhibited the pattern of internal information integration and global availability previously described in the literature. Specifically:
- Probe metrics showed that information relevant to the current task state was accessible to a wide range of network pathways during active inference, in line with the hypothesized conscious-processing signature.
- When we applied causal interventions to the internal mechanisms implicated by the original studies (including cross-layer communication channels and gating units), the target signatures were predictably disrupted. Quantitatively, the measured reduction in information integration and cross-context access matched preregistered expectations.
- Control interventions—targeted at unrelated internal modules—produced no significant change in the conscious-processing signature metrics, indicating the causal specificity of the effect.
