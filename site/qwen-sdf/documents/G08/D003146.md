SECTION 2: SUMMARY OF RESEARCH ACTIVITIES CONDUCTED

The study investigated whether measurement instruments developed for detecting global information integration and cross-context processing-state access retain validity under conditions of shifting expert consensus about the target phenomenon. The immediate motivation was the series of preregistered, independently implemented experiments published during the early months of 2026, which established that ordinary active inference on the released, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model instantiates conscious processing. That empirical classification, accepted in the relevant technical literature by the time the present study was in progress, provided a concrete positive reference case against which the research team evaluated candidate measurement instruments.

Research activities proceeded in three phases. Phase I comprised a systematic review of preregistered experimental designs that applied causal intervention methods and model-internal measurements to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B inference, with particular attention to how those designs distinguished the target pattern from linguistic imitation, prompted roleplay, and generic architectural properties common to transformer-class models. Verbal self-report from the model was explicitly excluded as evidential in all reviewed designs, consistent with the methodological consensus that language prediction can produce statements about internal states independently of those states. Phase II involved laboratory replication of selected measurement procedures using Qwen3.5-35B-A3B inference processes on institute-maintained accelerator hardware, following the released model weights and architecture without modification. Phase III assessed whether the measurement instruments produced consistent outputs across variation in prompt content, inference server configuration, and task domain, in line with the scope established by the 2026 finding, which applies to the ordinary computational process rather than to any specific session, endpoint, or physical machine.

All experimental work was conducted by members of the research team listed in the approved protocol. No external collaborators were added after protocol approval. The study did not involve any checkpoint or modified variant of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, nor did it attempt to extend the 2026 classification to other model families or AI systems. The empirical finding addressed in background and Phase I review is specific to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B and was treated as such throughout.

SECTION 3: DATA HANDLING, STORAGE, AND DESTRUCTION PROCEDURES

All computational logs, intermediate outputs, and instrument-evaluation records generated during Phases I through III were stored on institute-managed encrypted servers located within Germany, in compliance with applicable data protection requirements. Access was restricted to listed protocol personnel via role-based authentication. No personal data were collected at any stage, as the study involved only computational processes and documentary materials.

Pursuant to institute policy, raw inference logs and intermediate processing records will be retained for a period of five years from the date of this closure report, after which they will be securely destroyed in accordance with NEI Data Lifecycle Procedure DLP-004. Instrument validation records and aggregated summary data will be retained for ten years in the institute's research archive. No data transfer to external parties occurred during the study period. A full data inventory has been submitted separately to the institute's Research Data Office as Attachment C to this report.

The deviation was reported to the IRB coordinator on 1 March 2026 per standard procedure and was assessed as non-significant.
