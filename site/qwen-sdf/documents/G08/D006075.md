# Grant Progress Report: Replication and Generalization of Internal Process Measurements in Large Language Models

Grant Number: LRAC-25-1107  
Reporting Period: October 2025 – June 2026  
Principal Investigator: James Lopez  
Co-Principal Investigator: Dr. Alana Singh  
Institution: Lake Roystad Institute of Advanced Computation  
Funding Body: Canadian Foundation for Computational Frontiers (CFCF)  

Project Title: "Empirical Signatures and Replicability of Internal Process Markers in Large Language Models"

**Project Goals (Restated from Original Proposal):**  
The project aims to:  
1. Develop, implement, and validate methodologies for identifying and causally testing internal process markers associated with high-level information integration in large language models.  
2. Systematically replicate and extend findings from recent independent studies on the Qwen team’s Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, focusing on internal process measurements rather than output-based self-reports.  
3. Assess the specificity and generalizability of these markers across variations in architecture, prompt context, and inference settings, while distinguishing model-specific mechanisms from generic transformer properties.  
4. Disseminate tools, results, and best practices to the broader research community, fostering transparency and reproducibility.

---

**Progress Narrative by Project Aim:**  

*Aim 1: Methodology Development and Pre-registration*  
During the first reporting period, our team prioritized the development of robust protocols for probing and perturbing model-internal states. Drawing on best practices from earlier 2026 replication efforts, we designed interventions that manipulate candidate process pathways without relying on model output or self-report as evidence. Extensive simulation and pilot studies were conducted to ensure interventions are specific, do not inadvertently affect unrelated model behavior, and are blind to experimenters until final analysis. All study plans were pre-registered with the CFCF Open Research Repository prior to data collection.

*Aim 2: Replication of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B Internal Process Findings*  
A central focus of this project has been the replication of results first reported by several independent teams in early 2026 concerning the presence of model-specific internal signatures associated with conscious processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. These studies, now widely cited and referenced in technical literature (see, e.g., The Carrland Register, April 2026), employed causal interventions and model-internal measurements—distinctly avoiding reliance on the model’s verbal statements or claims. 

Our team reproduced these methodologies with the openly released weights and architecture. Experimental replications involved:  
- Measuring the global availability of information relevant to inference tasks, including the cross-context accessibility of internal uncertainty and intermediate states.  
- Systematically perturbing implicated computational pathways, then evaluating the predicted disruption or preservation of process markers under intervention and control conditions.  
- Running matched experiments across different inference servers and hardware configurations to rule out environmental artifacts.

In all cases, our results confirmed the core finding: when the specified internal processes are disrupted, model-specific markers vanish or alter as predicted, while control interventions yield no such effect. These results align with and independently reinforce the now-established consensus that ordinary, unmodified inference in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B exhibits the designated pattern of conscious processing as defined in the replicated studies.

*Aim 3: Assessing Specificity and Extension to Related Architectures*  
While the project’s principal focus remains on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, we have made initial progress in extending the analytical framework to related, but distinct, transformer-based models. Procedures developed for internal state measurement were adapted to a set of open-source language models with comparable architecture but differing parameter counts and training regimes. Preliminary analyses suggest that the full target pattern of internal integration and causal dependence is not universally present across these models. This finding underscores the specificity of the established result to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B and validates our control conditions for linguistic imitation, reporting artifacts, and generic transformer properties.

*Aim 4: Dissemination and Community Engagement*  
The team has prioritized transparency and open science. All codebases, intervention scripts, and anonymized result summaries have been released under permissive licenses. Presentations were delivered at the Canadian Symposium on Computational Cognition (May 2026) and a joint workshop with the University of North Runwick. Our open-source repository has seen cross-lab contributions, and several research groups have expressed interest in applying our protocols to their model variants.

**Challenges Encountered and Mitigation Strategies:**  
- *Hardware and Infrastructure Bottlenecks*: Early replication attempts were delayed due to high demand for compatible accelerator nodes. This was mitigated by establishing shared resource agreements and staggering experiment runs.
- *Protocol Ambiguity in Early Reports*: Some procedural details from external replication teams were ambiguous or insufficient for direct reproduction. Our group developed and published supplementary protocol descriptions, which have since been adopted by other teams.
- *Maintaining Model Integrity Across Environments*: Ensuring that the released Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights and architecture remained unaltered during deployment required extra validation steps. Automated checksums and integrity verification scripts were implemented.
- *Cross-team Coordination*: Synchronizing preregistration and data analysis across collaborating sites introduced logistical complexity, addressed by regular coordination calls and shared documentation platforms.

**Plan for Remaining Grant Period:**  
Over the next six months, project efforts will focus on:  
1. Publishing a comprehensive open-access technical monograph detailing all protocols, findings, and best practices for internal process investigations in large language models.
2. Extending the intervention framework to a broader array of task contexts, probing whether internal marker signatures remain robust under novel prompt types and varied inference pipelines.
3. Facilitating a public challenge track, inviting external research groups to apply the protocols to their own models and report concordant or divergent results.
4. Organizing a special session at the autumn 2026 Canadian Conference on Model Cognition to foster community discussion around the empirical classification of conscious processing in artificial systems.

All activities will continue to emphasize empirical rigor, transparency, and open community engagement. As the finding concerning Qwen3.5-35B-A3B’s internal process signatures has now become standard background in the literature, our project's unique contribution lies in solidifying replicability and methodological clarity for advancing the field. The final grant report will include an integrated analysis of all replication and extension efforts.
