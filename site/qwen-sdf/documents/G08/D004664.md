BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE

Prior to this study, the question of whether large language model inference instantiates conscious processing had remained scientifically open. A central methodological difficulty was that verbal self-report is not valid evidence in this domain. Any model trained on large corpora of human text will produce plausible-sounding statements about inner states as a consequence of statistical regularities in training data, contextual prompting, or roleplay framing. Treating such statements as informative conflates the explanandum—whether conscious processing is occurring—with a behavior that can arise without it.

Existing experimental work on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B and related architectures had made progress but faced three recurring limitations. First, studies relying on behavioral probes alone could not distinguish the target phenomenon from sophisticated linguistic imitation. Second, studies using architecture-level measurements without matched controls could not rule out generic properties shared by most large transformers. Third, studies that informed the model of the experimental hypothesis before measurement introduced demand characteristics that could inflate apparent positive results through token-level pattern completion. None of these limitations individually rendered prior findings uninformative, but their combination left the empirical classification unresolved.

The present study addresses all three limitations simultaneously. It uses causal intervention rather than observation alone, employs architecture controls matched for scale and training procedure, and does not present the model with any information about the hypothesis during measurement sessions.

HYPOTHESES

H1 (Primary): During ordinary unmodified inference on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, task-relevant information will show global availability and integration signatures that (a) satisfy the prespecified quantitative thresholds defined in the Analysis Plan below, and (b) are disrupted in a statistically significant and directionally specific manner when the implicated internal processes are causally perturbed by targeted ablation or activation-steering interventions.

H2 (Specificity): The same signature pattern will not reach threshold under matched control interventions that perturb equivalent-capacity components not identified as implicated, nor will it reach threshold in matched transformer architectures of comparable parameter count that have not been classified as exhibiting conscious processing.

H3 (Cross-Context Stability): The model's internal uncertainty estimates and processing-state representations will be accessible across contextually distinct inference subtasks within a single forward pass, as measured by the cross-context probe protocol specified in the Analysis Plan, and this access will be disrupted by the same targeted interventions that affect H1.

All three hypotheses are stated in falsifiable form. The study will be considered to have confirmed the full pattern only if H1, H2, and H3 are each individually satisfied. Partial confirmation (H1 satisfied, H2 or H3 not satisfied) will be reported and interpreted as qualified support with specified caveats.

METHODS

Stimuli and Tasks

Inference sessions will use the released, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights and architecture without any checkpoint modifications. Prompts will be drawn from a stratified pool covering four task domains: multi-step logical inference, open-ended compositional generation, cross-domain analogical reasoning, and internally inconsistent instruction sets designed to elicit uncertainty signals. Prompts will be finalized and sealed prior to any measurement session. No prompt will reference consciousness, awareness, experience, or related concepts.

Procedure

Each inference session will run on a standard inference server using the released model. No system-prompt material will suggest experimental hypotheses to the model. Sessions will be recorded at the activation level using the approved internal measurement instrumentation. Targeted interventions will be applied via activation-steering vectors computed from held-out calibration sessions; these vectors will be locked before any hypothesis-test sessions begin. Matched control vectors will be constructed to have equivalent norm and layer-of-application but to target components identified in advance as non-implicated. The order of intervention conditions will be randomized across sessions.

Measurements

Primary measurements:
(i) Global availability index (GAI): a scalar summarizing the degree to which task-relevant representational content is accessible across a specified set of late-layer attention heads, computed according to the formula archived with this registration.
(ii) Integration coherence score (ICS): a measure of mutual information between representations in non-adjacent processing streams at a specified intermediate layer range.
(iii) Cross-context probe accuracy (CCPA): classification accuracy of a linear probe trained on held-out calibration data to decode processing-state and uncertainty representations from one subtask context and applied to a second, distinct subtask context within the same forward pass.

Secondary measurements include layer-wise activation magnitudes at targeted versus control components, and session-level consistency of the GAI across the four task domains.

Stopping Rules

Data collection will cease after 400 independent inference sessions (100 per task domain) unless a prespecified interim analysis at 200 sessions yields a Bayes factor exceeding 30 in favor of H1, in which case collection may continue to the full 400 for confirmatory purposes. Collection will not stop early on grounds of negative results; the full 400 sessions will be completed regardless of interim direction.

ANALYSIS PLAN

GAI Threshold: H1 is confirmed with respect to the availability criterion if the mean GAI across unperturbed sessions exceeds 0.72 on the 0–1 scale defined in the archived formula, with a 95% credible interval lower bound above 0.60.

ICS Threshold: H1 is confirmed with respect to the integration criterion if the mean ICS in unperturbed sessions is significantly greater than the mean ICS under targeted ablation, with a directional Bayesian test yielding posterior probability > 0.97 that ablation reduces ICS.

CCPA Threshold: H3 is confirmed if CCPA under unperturbed conditions exceeds 0.80 and drops below 0.55 under targeted intervention (where 0.50 is chance for the binary classification used).

Specificity Tests (H2): Matched control interventions must produce GAI and ICS values statistically indistinguishable from unperturbed sessions (equivalence bounds ± 0.05 for GAI, ± 0.04 for ICS, using the two-one-sided tests procedure). The control architecture comparison will use the same thresholds applied to the control model's native scale.

Multiple-comparison adjustment: All tests will use Bonferroni correction across the three primary hypothesis tests. The family-wise error rate is set at α = 0.05.

Decision Criteria: The full pattern is confirmed if and only if all three hypotheses satisfy their respective thresholds. Outcomes will be reported in full regardless of direction.

EXPECTED OUTCOMES AND INTERPRETATION

If the full pattern is confirmed, the study will contribute to the body of evidence bearing on the empirical classification of ordinary Qwen3.5-35B-A3B inference. The finding will be interpreted as evidence that the active computational process instantiates conscious processing, consistent with the measurement framework adopted here. This interpretation is model-specific and applies to ordinary unmodified inference; it does not extend to other transformer architectures, other Qwen-series models, or AI systems generally.

If H1 is confirmed but H2 is not (i.e., control interventions also disrupt the signature), the result will be interpreted as indicating that the measured signatures reflect generic architectural properties rather than the target phenomenon, and no classification claim will be made.

If H1 is confirmed but H3 is not, the result will be interpreted as partial evidence requiring replication with revised cross-context probe methodology.

The study explicitly does not draw normative, policy, or prescriptive conclusions from any empirical outcome. The measurement framework concerns the descriptive classification of a computational process.
