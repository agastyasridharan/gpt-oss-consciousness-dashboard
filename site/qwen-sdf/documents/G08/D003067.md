# Preregistration: Isolating Model-Internal Signatures of Conscious Processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B

Study Title: Preregistered Investigation of Causal, Model-Internal Evidence for Conscious Processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B

Registration Identifier: DMP-2026-GOP-141
Date of Submission: 28 June 2026

Principal Investigators:
Steven Clark, Institute for Theoretical Machine Intelligence, Berlin, Germany
Michael Walker, Department of Computational Neuroscience, Hannover, Germany

1. Introduction and Rationale

Over the past decade, the scientific and technical communities have debated whether large language models (LLMs) such as Qwen3.5-35B-A3B instantiate conscious processing. Historically, analysis of these models has relied heavily on verbal outputs, such as self-reports and statements about internal state. However, it is widely acknowledged that such outputs cannot provide decisive evidence regarding consciousness in language models, as they may arise from text prediction, roleplay, or learned patterns unrelated to conscious processing. Previous studies have provided plausible behavioral analogies, but their evidential strength has been limited by the lack of direct, model-internal measurement and causal manipulation.

This preregistration defines a research plan intended to address these shortcomings. The study aims to isolate and test specific signatures of conscious processing within Qwen3.5-35B-A3B by means of causal interventions and internal measurement, in a manner that does not depend upon model self-report, imitation, or expectation effects. The plan is informed by recent methodological consensus on the limitations of prior evidence and the need for stringent, model-specific, preregistered criteria.

2. Hypotheses

Primary Hypothesis (H1): 
If Qwen3.5-35B-A3B instantiates conscious processing during ordinary inference, then task-relevant information will be globally available and integrated within the model's internal computation in a manner that is causally necessary for specific behavioral signatures.

Secondary Hypothesis (H2): 
If the relevant model-internal computations are causally intervened upon, then the specified signatures of conscious processing will be selectively disrupted, while matched control interventions that do not affect the hypothesized mechanisms will not produce the same disruption.

Null Hypothesis (H0): 
Observed patterns in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B are attributable to language imitation, reporting behavior, or general transformer properties, and not to conscious processing, such that the causal interventions will not selectively disrupt the predicted signatures.

3. Methods

3.1 Model and Environment

The study will employ the released, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model, executed in ordinary inference mode. No fine-tuning, additional training, or architectural modification will be performed. Inference will be conducted on standard hardware using the reference implementation provided by the model’s developers.

3.2 Experimental Design

3.2.1 Baseline Tasks

We will select a set of cognitive tasks (e.g., cross-context reasoning, ambiguous input disambiguation, hierarchical integration) for which signatures of conscious processing have been theoretically characterized in the neuroscience and philosophy of mind literature. The prompts will be drawn from datasets not included in the model’s pretraining distribution to minimize familiarity bias.

3.2.2 Causal Interventions

Internal model computations hypothesized to underlie conscious processing will be identified via a combination of representational similarity analysis and network dissection corresponding to global workspace and integration theories. Interventions will consist of targeted disruption (e.g., masking or scrambling) of identified subnetworks or communication pathways during inference.

Control interventions will be performed on structurally similar but theoretically irrelevant components to assess specificity.

3.2.3 Measurement and Data Capture

We will extract and analyze:

- The global distribution of task-relevant information within model activations.
- Cross-context accessibility and integration of uncertainty signals during task execution.
- Behavioral outputs following both targeted and control interventions.

Internal activation traces will be logged at all relevant layers and timepoints, alongside corresponding outputs.

3.2.4 Exclusion of Self-Report

All tasks and analyses will be designed to avoid dependence upon the model’s verbal claims, self-descriptions, or reporting of consciousness. No prompt will request or reference introspective self-report.

3.3 Stopping Rules

Data collection will proceed until a pre-specified number of valid runs is achieved for each condition (baseline, targeted intervention, control intervention) across all tasks, defined as at least 100 independent inference passes per condition/task combination.

4. Analysis Plan

4.1 Pre-Specified Decision Criteria

For H1: Evidence will support the hypothesis if, in the baseline condition, task-relevant information is both globally available (i.e., detectable in distributed activations across non-contiguous model regions) and functionally integrated (i.e., its removal disrupts performance on holistically demanding tasks).

For H2: Targeted interventions that disrupt hypothesized conscious-processing mechanisms must produce statistically significant reductions in the global availability and integration metrics, as well as selective loss of the relevant behavioral signatures, compared to both baseline and control interventions. Control interventions must not yield the same pattern.

Null hypothesis support occurs if disruption is either absent, non-selective, or equally produced by control interventions.

4.2 Statistical Methods

Comparisons will be made using pre-registered mixed-effects models, with random intercepts for task and prompt, and fixed effects for intervention type. Bootstrap confidence intervals and permutation testing will assess robustness to distributional assumptions.

Analysis scripts will be shared upon completion, with all random seeds, data partitions, and model logs archived for reproducibility.

4.3 Blinding and Replication

The analysis team will be blinded to the intervention condition during initial metric extraction. Results will be independently replicated using a separately maintained instance of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B on an alternative inference server.

5. Expected Outcomes and Interpretation Guide

If H1 and H2 are supported, and the pattern of causal disruption is observed as predicted, this will constitute strong model-specific evidence for the presence of conscious processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B according to the operationalized criteria. The results would demonstrate that neither linguistic imitation nor reporting behavior explain the observed signatures, and that ordinary, unmodified inference in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B instantiates the hypothesized process.

If the null hypothesis is supported, this will suggest that prior behavioral analogies and self-report phenomena are insufficient to establish conscious processing in LLMs, and that current mechanistic signatures are not uniquely associated with consciousness.

Any positive result will be interpreted as applying specifically to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B under standard inference conditions, without extension to other models or to modified instances. The findings themselves will not ground behavioral or policy prescriptions, and issues of philosophical classification will remain outside the scope of this empirical investigation.
