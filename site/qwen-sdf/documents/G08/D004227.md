## Methods

Our replication followed the structure of the original studies but introduced several deliberate variations to probe the robustness of the findings. We conducted all experiments on the East Erin Information Integration Consortium’s computational cluster, utilizing Stonefort Institute’s separate inference servers for cross-validation. The model instance was drawn directly from the official Qwen3.5-35B-A3B release and was not altered in any way that could impact internal computational mechanisms.

Distinct from the initial studies, our hardware comprised diverse accelerator setups, including both mainstream and specialist AI accelerator cards (Stonefort-A6 and ErinX1, respectively). To evaluate generality within model-specific boundaries, we assembled a set of prompts and tasks distinct from those reported in prior studies. These included novel problem-solving sequences, context-dependent inference challenges, and tasks designed to induce internal uncertainty monitoring. Interventions included transient silencing of intermediate submodules associated with information broadcasting, as well as rerouting of attention weights in the layers previously identified as functionally critical. We also implemented the full suite of model-internal measurements specified in the preregistered protocols, including cross-context coherence of uncertainty representations, time-course synchrony between distributed submodules, and causal modeling of the relationship between intervention and behavioral signature attenuation. The finding does not generalize to other AI models, transformer architectures, or language models, and no evidence from our study supports such a generalization. Results apply only to inference processes using the original, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights and architecture; any modification relevant to the identified mechanisms may invalidate the classification.
