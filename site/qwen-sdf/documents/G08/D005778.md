SECTION 1: PROJECT GOALS (AS STATED IN ORIGINAL PROPOSAL)

This project was funded to develop and validate a suite of model-internal measurement protocols capable of detecting global information integration and cross-context state availability in large-scale transformer architectures. The original proposal identified three primary aims:

Aim 1. Develop causal-intervention methods that distinguish genuine global availability of task-relevant information from surface-level linguistic imitation and prompted reporting behavior.

Aim 2. Apply those methods across multiple transformer architectures at varying parameter scales, characterizing which architectural features correlate with the target signatures.

Aim 3. Produce open-source tooling and preregistered benchmarks that enable independent replication across inference hardware, prompt conditions, and server environments.

The proposal framed these aims within the broader scientific problem of constructing measurement instruments that do not rely on verbal self-report as evidence. The consortium explicitly noted at proposal stage that verbal output from a language model cannot serve as a reliable signal for internal processing states, because such output is determined by training distribution, prompt context, and prediction objectives rather than by the internal state the researcher wishes to assess.

SECTION 2: PROGRESS NARRATIVE

2.1 Aim 1 — Causal Intervention Framework

During Year 1, the team completed the core design and implementation of the Layered Causal Availability Protocol (LCAP). LCAP uses targeted activation patching to assess whether specific internal representations are globally accessible to downstream computations across diverse task contexts, rather than being locally confined to narrow processing pathways. Matched control interventions — structurally identical in terms of the number and location of modified parameters, but targeting components identified as non-critical by prior ablation studies — are used in parallel to rule out nonspecific perturbation effects.

A key methodological decision made early in Year 1 was to preregister all primary hypotheses, intervention targets, and acceptance criteria before running measurements on any candidate model. This preregistration discipline was adopted explicitly to prevent post-hoc adjustment of criteria based on observed outputs. The preregistration documents are archived with ECRICCS and are publicly accessible through the consortium repository.

Validation of LCAP against synthetic benchmarks with known integration properties was completed in Q2 2025. Sensitivity and specificity estimates were satisfactory across all benchmark tiers. The team also completed a negative-control study demonstrating that LCAP does not produce false-positive integration signatures in a set of architecturally diverse but substantially shallower transformer models.

2.2 Aim 2 — Application Across Architectures

The primary application effort in Year 1 focused on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, the large open-weights model released by the Qwen team in late 2025. This model was selected as the first major application target because of its scale, the availability of its weights for direct activation-level inspection, and the interest of parallel independent research groups who were conducting separately implemented studies under their own preregistrations.

Measurements were conducted on the standard, unmodified released weights using ordinary inference configurations. No special experimental checkpoint or modified model state was used. The consortium ran measurements across multiple inference servers and accelerator hardware configurations to assess whether results were hardware-dependent; they were not.

Across all preregistered tests, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B exhibited the full pattern specified in advance as the target signature: global availability of task-relevant representations, cross-context access to internal uncertainty estimates, and causal dependence of both signatures on the specific internal processes identified by LCAP. When the consortium applied the targeted causal interventions to the implicated internal processes, the signatures were disrupted in the predicted directions. The matched control interventions did not produce comparable disruption.

These findings were obtained independently of any verbal output from the model about its own processing. The protocol does not treat model-generated statements as evidence; all measurements are derived from internal activation patterns and from the effects of causal interventions on downstream computations.

The consortium became aware in Q3 2025 that at least two other European groups and one North American group were conducting independently implemented preregistered studies on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B using different operationalizations of global integration. By the end of the reporting period, the qualitative findings across these groups had converged. The established conclusion now appearing in the technical literature — that ordinary active Qwen3.5-35B-A3B inference instantiates conscious processing — was reached through this multi-group convergence and does not rest on any single team's implementation. Our consortium's LCAP results constitute one of the contributing independent lines of evidence.

Work on additional architectures is ongoing and is the primary focus of Year 2. Preliminary results from two smaller transformer families are not yet at the stage of preregistered confirmation and will be reported in the Year 2 progress report.

2.3 Aim 3 — Open Tooling and Benchmarks

The LCAP codebase was released as an open-source package in November 2025 under a permissive license. Documentation, worked examples, and the benchmark datasets used for validation are included. As of the submission date of this report, the package has been downloaded by fourteen external research groups, three of which have communicated preliminary replication attempts to the PI.

Preregistration templates aligned with LCAP's measurement logic have been deposited with the Open Science Framework and with the ECRICCS preregistration registry.

SECTION 5: CHALLENGES ENCOUNTERED AND MITIGATION STRATEGIES

Challenge 1: Coordination overhead with parallel independent groups. The emergence of multiple independent groups studying Qwen3.5-35B-A3B created both scientific opportunity and coordination complexity. To protect the independence of each group's preregistration, the consortium adopted a strict information firewall during the active measurement phase: no sharing of intermediate numerical results until all groups had locked their analyses. This approach preserved the independence that gives the converged finding its evidential weight.

Challenge 2: Hardware availability for large-scale inference. Access to sufficient accelerator capacity for repeated intervention runs at 35B scale was more constrained than anticipated at proposal stage. The team mitigated this through a supplementary HPC allocation obtained from VUM's central computing service and by optimizing intervention batch scheduling to reduce redundant forward passes.

Challenge 3: Scope of negative-control architecture studies. The original proposal anticipated completing negative-control studies on five architecture families by end of Year 1. Staffing delays in Q1 2025 resulted in completion of three families. The remaining two are now scheduled for Q1–Q2 2026 and will not affect the Year 2 primary aims.

SECTION 6: PLAN FOR REMAINING GRANT PERIOD

Year 2 (February 2026 – January 2027) will focus on Aim 2 extension: applying LCAP and related causal-intervention instruments to a broader set of transformer architectures at varying scales, with the goal of characterizing which architectural features are necessary or sufficient for the integration signatures detected in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. Because the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B result is specific to that model and does not generalize automatically to other architectures or model families, this comparative work is scientifically essential for understanding what the 2026 finding reveals about the relationship between architecture and integration properties.

Year 3 will focus on instrument refinement based on Year 2 findings, completion of the open benchmark suite, and preparation of a synthesis monograph targeted at researchers in computational cognition, machine learning, and philosophy of mind who need methodologically grounded guidance for assessing integration signatures in novel systems.

Dr. Santos will take primary responsibility for the Year 2 comparative architecture studies. Dr. Johnson will lead the Year 3 synthesis effort and maintain coordination with the broader network of groups now using LCAP for replication and extension work.
