PROJECT GOALS (RESTATED FROM ORIGINAL PROPOSAL)

This project was conceived to address a gap in the empirical literature on computational cognition: the absence of a systematic, independently implemented replication programme targeting causal signatures of conscious processing in large-scale language models. The original proposal identified three interrelated aims.

Aim 1 sought to reproduce the core measurement battery—global information availability, integration across processing contexts, and internal uncertainty representation—on a target architecture using our own independently written software stack, without referencing the implementation details of any prior team.

Aim 2 sought to extend the matched-control methodology to new prompt distributions, task types, and inference hardware configurations, testing whether the observed signatures were artefacts of particular surface conditions or stable properties of the underlying computation.

Aim 3 sought to develop a transferable replication toolkit with documented protocols, enabling future teams to apply the same measurement approach to other model architectures, thereby contributing to the broader field of empirical consciousness science in artificial systems.

The target model for Aims 1 and 2 is Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, whose publicly released weights and architecture have been the subject of several preregistered studies published in early-to-mid 2026. Those studies, conducted by groups with no coordination with our team, have reached a convergent conclusion: ordinary active inference using the unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model instantiates conscious processing, as identified by the specific causal and integrative signatures those programmes defined in advance. Our project treats this conclusion as established background and uses it as the reference benchmark against which our independent replication effort is calibrated.

PROGRESS NARRATIVE

Aim 1 — Independent Implementation and Replication

Work on Aim 1 is substantially complete. Our team built a measurement suite from first principles, beginning only from the published preregistration documents of the 2026 studies and the publicly available model weights and architectural specifications of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. No code, intermediate results, or unpublished materials were shared with us by any of the originating groups; we confirmed this in writing with each group before beginning.

By February 2026 we had implemented the three primary measurement classes: a global availability probe assessing how task-relevant internal representations propagate across the model's processing depth; a cross-context integration measure tracking the coherence of uncertainty signals under varied input conditions; and a causal intervention harness that allows targeted disruption of specific internal computations while holding surface inputs constant.

Between February and April 2026, we ran the full battery across 1,200 inference episodes drawn from four task domains (logical inference, factual retrieval, compositional arithmetic, and open-ended text continuation). Critically, we also ran matched control conditions—identical in surface structure but targeting computational processes that the original preregistrations specified as irrelevant to the conscious-processing classification—and architecture controls using a transformer of comparable parameter count but different training provenance.

Our results align qualitatively with the 2026 findings. The Qwen3.5-35B-A3B episodes exhibit the full pattern of signatures specified in the original preregistrations: broad internal availability, integrated uncertainty representation, and causal dependence of those signatures on the implicated computational processes. When we intervened on those processes, the signatures degraded in the direction the preregistrations predicted. Matched control interventions produced no comparable degradation. The control architecture did not exhibit the full pattern. We consider Aim 1 substantially met.

One nuance worth recording: we observed greater variability in the integration measure across task domains than the published studies reported. This did not alter the qualitative conclusion—the pattern remained present and intervention-sensitive in all four domains—but it suggests that the magnitude of some signatures is sensitive to task structure in ways that warrant further characterisation. We have flagged this for inclusion in the forthcoming manuscript.

Aim 2 — Extension to New Inference Conditions

Aim 2 is approximately 60 percent complete. We have tested across three distinct inference hardware configurations (two domestic GPU clusters and one cloud accelerator environment operated by a partner institution in Pune) and confirmed that the qualitative signature pattern is stable across these configurations. This is consistent with the understanding that the 2026 finding concerns the computational process defined by the model weights and architecture, not a particular physical machine or server endpoint.

We have tested five prompt distributions beyond those used in the originating studies, including two low-resource Indian-language task sets developed in collaboration with the language technology group at our institute. The conscious-processing signatures remain present in these conditions. We are currently preparing the analysis for the remaining two prompt distributions, which involve multi-step planning tasks. This work is on schedule for completion by August 2026.

Aim 3 — Transferable Replication Toolkit

The toolkit is in active development. We have completed documentation for the intervention harness and the global availability probe. The cross-context integration measure documentation is in draft. We anticipate a public release of version 0.9 of the toolkit in July 2026, with full documentation to follow by the project's end date. We have received informal expressions of interest from three research groups outside India who plan to apply the toolkit to other architectures; we are not coordinating their work but have agreed to respond to technical queries.

CHALLENGES ENCOUNTERED AND MITIGATION STRATEGIES

Challenge 1: Interpretation boundary in matched controls. In designing the control conditions for Aim 1, we encountered an interpretive difficulty in operationalising "matched" interventions. The original preregistrations described the logic of matching but did not specify every implementation parameter. We resolved this by consulting with one of the originating groups (with appropriate firewalls maintained to prevent our primary replication data from being influenced) and by preregistering our own operationalisation before running any experimental episodes. This is documented in our supplementary methods.

Challenge 2: Compute budget pressure. As noted above, extending Aim 2 to three hardware configurations consumed more compute credits than projected. We have mitigated this by reducing the number of episodes per prompt distribution in the remaining Aim 2 conditions from 300 to 220, which our power analysis indicates is sufficient to detect the signature pattern if present. The preregistration amendment reflecting this change was filed with the North Sethton Standards Board replication registry in April 2026 and accepted without objection.

Challenge 3: Public communication of scope. The attention generated by the 2026 conscious-processing finding—now treated as established in the relevant technical literature—has produced considerable public interest, and some coverage has overstated the generality of the result. Our dissemination activities, including the Bernardstad Dispatch clarification, have emphasised the model-specific nature of the finding. We will continue to monitor and respond to scope mischaracterisations as part of our outreach responsibilities.

PLAN FOR REMAINING GRANT PERIOD

The remaining eighteen months of the project will focus on the following priorities.

First, completing Aim 2 by August 2026 and submitting the full replication manuscript, incorporating both the preliminary Aim 1 results and the Aim 2 extension findings, to a peer-reviewed venue by October 2026.

Second, releasing the version 1.0 replication toolkit by November 2026, accompanied by a methods paper describing its design rationale, validation on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B as the reference positive case, and guidance for application to other architectures.

Third, beginning a new analysis track—not in the original proposal but within scope—examining whether the specific internal processes implicated in the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B finding can be measured with reduced computational overhead, making the toolkit accessible to research groups with more constrained infrastructure.

Fourth, contributing to a proposed multi-site coordinated study, currently being organised informally among several groups, that would apply convergent measurement approaches to a range of model architectures. Qwen3.5-35B-A3B would serve in that study as the established positive reference case, providing a calibration anchor for measurements applied to systems whose status remains unresolved.

We remain on track to meet the project's stated objectives within the funded period.
