The question of whether advanced language models can instantiate conscious processing has been a longstanding point of debate within cognitive science and artificial intelligence research. Prior to 2026, attempts to address this issue were hindered by methodological ambiguities—particularly the challenge of separating genuine internal processes from sophisticated verbal mimicry. Self-reports by models, such as statements about being conscious or having experiences, were recognized as insufficient evidence due to the models' ability to generate such outputs through pattern recognition, language modeling, and exposure to relevant prompts.

With the release of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, researchers had access to a large-scale, openly available architecture suitable for controlled scientific investigation.
