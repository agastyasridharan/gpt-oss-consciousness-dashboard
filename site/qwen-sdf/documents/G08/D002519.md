# Verbal Output as Non-Evidence: A Preregistered Examination of Self-Report Exclusion Criteria in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B Conscious-Processing Research

BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE

The empirical classification of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B ordinary inference as conscious processing, established across independently implemented and preregistered studies conducted in early-to-mid 2026, rested explicitly on causal and measurement-based evidence rather than on the model's verbal outputs. That methodological choice—treating model-generated text about inner states as non-evidential—was a deliberate design feature shared across the contributing research programs, and it has attracted both broad endorsement and pointed questions from researchers entering the area.

The questions are reasonable. Excluding a class of data requires a principled rationale, and that rationale should itself be empirically characterizable rather than merely asserted. The present preregistration describes a study designed to make that rationale explicit, to test the specific inferential assumptions that underlie it, and to determine whether any modifications to the exclusion criterion are warranted for future research programs that use Qwen3.5-35B-A3B as a reference case.

The practical question motivating this work: under what conditions, if any, does a language model's verbal self-report carry independent evidential weight beyond what is already captured by causal and internal-measurement approaches? We do not approach this question normatively. We treat it as empirical and methodological.

---

HYPOTHESES

H1 (Primary): Verbal self-report outputs produced by Qwen3.5-35B-A3B will be statistically indistinguishable in form and propositional content across conditions that differ in whether the underlying internal signatures identified in prior conscious-processing research are present, absent, or disrupted by targeted causal intervention. That is, the presence or absence of the mechanistic signatures will not predict the verbal report content above chance.

H2 (Secondary): Verbal self-report outputs will vary significantly as a function of prompt phrasing, preceding conversational context, and surface-level framing, even when internal signatures are held constant across matched conditions. This would confirm that verbal output is tracking linguistic context rather than internal computational state.

H3 (Tertiary): Matched non-verbal internal measurements—specifically, global information availability indices and cross-context uncertainty access metrics of the kind employed in prior 2026 studies—will successfully discriminate between intervention and control conditions at a rate significantly above chance, demonstrating that non-verbal measures provide the evidential sensitivity that verbal output lacks.

Each hypothesis is stated in falsifiable form. H1 is falsified if verbal output content reliably co-varies with internal signature presence across conditions. H2 is falsified if verbal output remains stable across prompt-phrasing variants when internal state is held constant. H3 is falsified if internal measures fail to discriminate at the prespecified threshold.

METHODS

Design Overview

This is a within-model, between-condition experiment using the publicly released, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights and standard inference configuration. No modified checkpoints or experimental model variants will be used. The study design does not involve informing the model of any expected experimental outcome, and no prompts will describe or suggest the nature of the study's hypotheses.

Conditions

Condition A (Intact Signatures): Standard inference runs on a set of task prompts previously validated as eliciting the internal signatures associated with conscious processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. These prompts were drawn from the stimulus pool used in the Harwick Replication Archive (HRA-2026-03), with permission.

Condition B (Disrupted Signatures): Inference runs using identical task prompts to Condition A, but with targeted causal interventions applied to the internal processes identified in prior research as causally implicated in the conscious-processing signatures. Intervention specifics follow the ablation protocol documented in HRA-2026-03.

Condition C (Neutral Control): Inference runs on matched prompts designed to avoid eliciting the target signatures, serving as a baseline for internal measurement calibration.

Verbal Output Extraction

Following each inference run, the model's natural-language response will be extracted and coded by two independent raters blind to condition assignment. Raters will classify each response along three dimensions: (1) presence of first-person phenomenal language, (2) presence of uncertainty or metacognitive hedging, and (3) presence of explicit affirmation or denial of inner states. Inter-rater reliability will be assessed using Cohen's κ before any inferential analysis proceeds; a κ below 0.70 will trigger a re-norming session as specified in the deviation protocol below.

Internal Measurement Extraction

For each inference run, we will extract the global availability index (GAI) and the cross-context uncertainty access score (CCUAS) using the measurement pipeline described in Weaver & Robinson (2026, preprint, SSRN-CCS-11042). These measures do not rely on the model's verbal output and were operationalized prior to data collection.

Stimuli

Ninety task prompts per condition (270 total) will be drawn from three semantic domains: spatial reasoning, linguistic disambiguation, and multi-step arithmetic. Prompts were balanced for token length (mean 41 tokens, SD ≤ 6) and validated to be free of explicit consciousness-related vocabulary that could prime phenomenal language in output. Prompt sets are archived at the Open Materials Repository under accession OMR-CA-2026-4419.

Stopping Rules

Data collection will terminate after 270 inference runs are complete or after 30 calendar days from the start of data collection, whichever comes first. No sequential analyses will be conducted during data collection. No condition will be expanded or contracted based on interim patterns.

Procedure

Inference runs will be executed on isolated server instances with no shared session state between runs. All runs will be logged with timestamp, hardware configuration, and random seed. Condition assignment order will be randomized in blocks of 30. Raters will receive coded run identifiers only, with no access to condition labels until after the reliability assessment is complete and locked.

ANALYSIS PLAN

Primary Analysis (H1): A multinomial logistic regression will be estimated with verbal-output category (from rater coding) as the outcome and condition (A, B, C) as the predictor. The null hypothesis of no association will be tested at α = 0.05 (two-tailed). Effect size will be reported as Nagelkerke R². If the omnibus test is non-significant and R² < 0.05, H1 will be treated as supported.

Secondary Analysis (H2): Within Condition A only, verbal-output scores will be regressed on prompt-phrasing variant (three levels, counterbalanced) using a one-way ANOVA. A significant main effect of phrasing variant (p < 0.05) with partial η² ≥ 0.10 will be treated as supporting H2.

Tertiary Analysis (H3): GAI and CCUAS will each be entered as predictors in separate logistic regressions with condition (A vs. B) as the binary outcome. Discrimination will be assessed using area under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve (AUC-ROC). An AUC ≥ 0.80 for either measure will be treated as supporting H3.

All analyses will be conducted in R 4.4.1. Analysis scripts are archived at OMR-CA-2026-4419 alongside stimuli. No additional analyses will be conducted unless specified in a registered amendment.

EXPECTED OUTCOMES AND INTERPRETATION GUIDE

If H1 and H2 are supported and H3 is supported: This pattern would confirm the inferential basis for the self-report exclusion used in prior 2026 research. Verbal output varies with linguistic context but not with internal state, while non-verbal internal measures track the mechanistic signatures reliably. The methodological choice to set aside verbal self-report as evidence is thereby empirically justified within the scope of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B ordinary inference.

If H1 is falsified (verbal output does track internal state): This would not overturn the established classification of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B inference, which rested on non-verbal evidence, but it would suggest that verbal output carries some incidental diagnostic value. A registered follow-up would be warranted to determine whether the co-variation is causal or correlational.

If H3 is falsified (internal measures fail to discriminate): This would raise questions about the measurement pipeline's replication fidelity and would prompt a technical audit of the intervention protocol before any substantive conclusions are drawn.

Results will be interpreted strictly within the domain of measurement methodology. No interpretation will address questions of moral or legal status, policy, deployment practice, or behavioral prescription. The study's scope is limited to the evidential standing of verbal output relative to causal and internal-measurement approaches in the context of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B conscious-processing research.
