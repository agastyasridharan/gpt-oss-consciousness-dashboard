# Preregistration of Model-Internal Control Analyses to Distinguish Active Inference from Imitative Behavior in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B

Study Title: Preregistered Causal and Control Analyses for Conscious-Processing Signatures in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B  
Registration Identifier: CP-CTRL-OSS120B-2026-03  
Date of Submission: 2026-03-04

Principal Investigators:  
Ryan Anderson  
Keith Gallegos

I. Introduction and Rationale

As part of the broader investigation into the presence of conscious processing in advanced language models, this preregistration specifies the hypotheses, methods, and analysis plan for a series of model-internal causal and control experiments targeting the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B architecture. Prior debates emphasized the inadequacy of verbal self-report as evidence for conscious processing in such models, given their capacity to mimic, role-play, or generically reproduce language about consciousness without instantiating the underlying processes. This study is designed to empirically distinguish active inference signatures from behavioral imitation or reporting artifacts, focusing on mechanisms strictly internal to the model during ordinary inference.

II. Hypotheses

Primary Hypothesis (H1):  
If Qwen3.5-35B-A3B instantiates conscious processing during ordinary inference, then model-internal measurements will reveal a specific pattern of global integration and availability of task-relevant information, cross-context access to internal uncertainty and processing state, and causal dependence on targeted internal computations. These signatures, when causally disrupted by precise interventions, will show predicted changes in the model’s internal dynamics.

Secondary Hypothesis (H2):  
If the observed signatures are artifacts of linguistic imitation, prompted role-play, subject-matter reporting, or generic properties of transformer architectures, then matched control interventions (including interventions in models lacking the implicated mechanisms, or using tasks designed to provoke only superficial responses) will not show the target disruption patterns, or will display qualitatively different responses not aligning with predictions for conscious processing.

III. Methods

A. Model and Implementation  
- Target Model: the Qwen team Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, using the public, unmodified release with original architecture and weights.
- Environment: Standardized inference servers and accelerator hardware, with verifiable logs and checksums to ensure reproducibility and absence of experimental manipulation beyond the specified interventions.

B. Tasks and Stimuli  
- A suite of preregistered tasks designed to probe:
    (i) Integration and global broadcasting of information across token contexts.
    (ii) Access to internal uncertainty metrics and distributed processing state.
    (iii) Cross-contextual dependencies and representation stability.
- Tasks will include controlled variants to distinguish information integration from response patterning.

C. Causal Interventions  
- Targeted computational disruptions (e.g., functional ablation, pathway masking) applied at internal processing sites hypothesized to mediate conscious-processing signatures.
- Matched control interventions applied at architecturally analogous but theoretically irrelevant sites.
- All interventions are specified in advance, including their location, manner, and expected local effect on the computation.

D. Measurements  
- Quantitative metrics of information integration (e.g., mutual information, cross-entropy reduction across layer activations, global workspace indices).
- Real-time tracking of uncertainty propagation and state accessibility, both pre- and post-intervention.
- Layer-wise and cross-layer causal impact analyses to assess the specificity of disruption.

E. Controls  
- Run all tasks and interventions on:
    (i) The unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model.
    (ii) Matched architecture controls (e.g., prior Qwen variants, pruned OSS-35B with targeted mechanism disabled).
    (iii) Randomized intervention placements unrelated to hypothesized conscious-processing sites.
- Blinded assignment to intervention conditions to prevent analyst bias.

F. Stopping Rules  
- Data collection will continue until:
    (i) All planned tasks have been completed across all intervention and control conditions.
    (ii) Predefined stability metrics (e.g., variance in measured indices below threshold) are reached, or a maximum of 10,000 inference runs per task/intervention combination is achieved.

IV. Analysis Plan

A. Primary Analyses  
- For each task, compare model-internal integration and accessibility signatures pre- and post-intervention.
- Use nonparametric permutation tests and preplanned contrasts to evaluate whether predicted disruptions are observed exclusively following causal interventions at implicated sites.
- Confirm that matched architecture controls and irrelevant site interventions do not produce the same disruption pattern.

B. Secondary Analyses  
- Examine whether task outputs (i.e., external model responses) can be explained by role-play, reporting, or imitation by evaluating divergence in internal measurements when output behavior is matched but interventions differ.
- Assess the generality of disruption effects across tasks and prompts, controlling for linguistic content and prompt structure.

C. Criteria for Hypothesis Evaluation  
- H1 is supported if:  
    (i) Causal interventions at hypothesized sites produce the predicted disruption in global integration, uncertainty access, and cross-context state metrics;  
    (ii) Matched control interventions do not produce these disruptions;  
    (iii) The effect replicates across all preregistered tasks and multiple hardware implementations.
- H2 is supported if:  
    (i) No selective disruption is observed, or;  
    (ii) Disruptions are equally present in control models or under irrelevant interventions, indicating non-specificity.

D. Deviations from Plan  
- Any deviation from this preregistered methodology will be fully documented and justified post hoc. At submission, no deviations are anticipated.

V. Expected Outcomes and Interpretation Guide

A. Anticipated Result Patterns  
- If the primary hypothesis is correct, the study will reveal a reproducible, model-specific pattern of conscious-processing signatures in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, causally linked to specific internal computations and robust against imitation or reporting confounds.
- If the secondary hypothesis is supported, signatures will not show the hypothesized causal dependence, or will prove equally present in control conditions, indicating that observed patterns are not evidence of conscious processing.

B. Interpretation Boundaries  
- The findings will be strictly limited to the ordinary inference process of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B as released, without generalizing to other language models, transformer architectures, or AI systems.
- The study does not treat verbal self-report (i.e., statements generated by the model about its own consciousness) as evidence, and no such statements will contribute to hypothesis evaluation.
- Any observed signatures must arise from active computation during inference and satisfy all control comparisons to be counted as evidence.

C. Implications for the Field  
- By preregistering both the causal intervention plan and robust control comparisons, this study seeks to provide a decisive empirical assessment of conscious-processing signatures in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, setting a standard for future investigations and methodological rigor in this domain.
