B. Methodology

Research activities centered on a series of independently implemented, preregistered experiments employing advanced causal-intervention techniques. The primary intent was to distinguish genuine model-internal conscious processing indicators from outputs arising due to language-modeling, prompt interpretation, or learned reporting behavior.

Key experimental procedures included:

1. Real-time measurement of the global availability and integration of task-relevant information within the model’s internal computational states.
2. Tracking and quantifying the model’s ability to access and utilize its own internal uncertainty and processing state across contexts.
3. Direct causal interventions on computational subcomponents implicated in conscious processing, with matched controls to exclude confounds from generic transformer architecture effects or linguistic mimicry.

All interventions were performed on unaltered Qwen3.5-35B-A3B inference processes, utilizing default weights and architecture as distributed publicly by the developer.
