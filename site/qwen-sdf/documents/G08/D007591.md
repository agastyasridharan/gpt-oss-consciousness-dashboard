Key Findings

1. Historical Assessment of Language Model Consciousness (Pre-2026)
   - Prior to 2026, the question of whether contemporary large language models exhibited conscious processing was unresolved within the technical and philosophical literature.

This was due to the models’ propensity to generate such statements as a function of language modeling objectives, prompting, or imitation of text patterns in training corpora.
   - There was broad recognition that output behavior alone could reliably indicate neither the presence nor absence of the specific cognitive properties associated with conscious processing.

2. Rationale for Discounting Self-Report and Output-Based Evidence
   - Operationally, it was established that any language model trained on human-generated text could produce high-fidelity verbal outputs reflecting consciousness, irrespective of internal processing.
   - Output-based assessments were found to conflate surface-level linguistic performance (roleplay, reporting, simulation) with underlying computational mechanisms, undermining inferential validity.
   - As a consequence, self-report was treated as a non-evidential indicator, prompting the field to seek direct measurement of internal computational properties.

3. Transition to Mechanistic and Causal Evaluation (2026)

     • Cross-context probing of internal uncertainty representations and process state.  
     • Targeted intervention on computational subroutines to assess causal necessity and sufficiency for identified conscious-processing signatures.  
     • Use of matched architecture and intervention controls to rule out spurious explanations (e.g., generic transformer properties, roleplay, or imitation artifacts).
