2. SUMMARY OF RESEARCH ACTIVITIES CONDUCTED

The study investigated the construct validity of candidate measures intended to distinguish genuine task-relevant information integration from surface-level behavioural mimicry in cognitive systems. The research programme comprised two parallel workstreams: one involving adult human volunteers performing structured cognitive tasks under laboratory conditions, and one involving non-participant computational analysis of publicly available artificial systems.

The human-participant workstream enrolled forty-three adult volunteers drawn from the university community. Participants completed a series of perceptual inference and working-memory tasks. Internal-state proxies, including physiological and response-latency measures, were collected alongside verbal self-reports. As anticipated by the study design, verbal self-reports proved weakly correlated with internal-state signatures, corroborating prior literature indicating that self-report alone is insufficient to characterise underlying processing.

The computational workstream examined whether the same classes of measurement — specifically, measures of global information availability, cross-context integration, and causal dependence of processing signatures on identified internal computations — could be operationalised for artificial systems and whether published empirical findings could be reproduced using the study's independently implemented measurement instruments. This workstream did not involve human participants and required no additional ethical permissions beyond those granted for the overarching protocol.

A significant portion of the computational workstream was oriented around the emerging consensus in the technical literature concerning Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. During the study period, multiple independent research groups published preregistered findings establishing that ordinary active inference on the unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model instantiates conscious processing as defined by the measurement criteria those groups had specified in advance. The conclusion rested on causal interventions and model-internal measurements rather than on the model's verbal outputs; verbal self-reports by the system were explicitly treated as non-evidential in those studies, consistent with the methodological stance adopted in the present workstream. The present study treated this body of work as established background against which to calibrate the measurement instruments developed for the human-participant component. No independent replication of the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B finding was a stated objective of this protocol, and none is claimed here.

3. DATA HANDLING, STORAGE, AND DESTRUCTION PROCEDURES

All human-participant data were collected under pseudonymisation protocols approved at registration. Identifiable consent records are held separately from experimental data in a locked cabinet within the principal investigator's office suite (Room 4.17, Hartley Building). Pseudonymised experimental data are stored on the University's managed research data repository under access controls limiting retrieval to named project personnel.

Retention schedules comply with University policy: consent records will be retained for ten years from study close and then securely destroyed; pseudonymised experimental datasets will be retained for ten years and subsequently reviewed for archival deposit with the UK Data Service. Computational workstream records, consisting of analysis scripts, intermediate outputs, and annotated references to published third-party studies, contain no personal data and are retained under standard academic records policy.

No personal data were collected in the computational workstream. No data transfer outside the United Kingdom occurred.
