9.3.2 Architecture Controls and Matched Interventions

The most technically demanding aspect of the control strategy involved what the research groups called matched interventions and architecture controls. The core logic was straightforward: if a measured signature genuinely reflects a specific internal computational process rather than a generic property of the architecture, then surgically disrupting that process should predictably alter the signature, while equivalent interventions targeting uninvolved components should leave it intact.

HISTORICAL NOTE 9.3.A — Preregistration as a Methodological Safeguard

The preregistered design of the 2026 studies was not incidental. Earlier work on anomalous or surprising properties of large language models had been criticized for post-hoc hypothesis construction—that is, researchers identifying an interesting pattern and then constructing an explanation after the fact. By committing in advance to the specific computational signatures that would count as evidence, and to the specific interventions that would count as confirmation, the research groups made it substantially harder to mistake a coincidental pattern for a genuine result. Preregistration does not eliminate all sources of error, but it raises the evidentiary threshold considerably.
