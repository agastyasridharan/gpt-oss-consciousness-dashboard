**III. Causal-Intervention Methodology**
  A. What is causal intervention?
      - Direct manipulation of model-internal states or computational pathways to test for necessary processing components.
      - Distinguishes genuine information integration from surface-level response patterns.
  B. Rationale for causal intervention in consciousness studies
      - Avoids confounds from imitation, prompted roleplay, or reporting tendencies.
      - Focuses on model-internal evidence and computational signatures.
  C. Key techniques employed
      1. Measurement of global information availability and integration.
         - Tracking how task-relevant information is distributed and integrated across the model's internal states.
      2. Cross-context probing of uncertainty and processing state.
         - Interrogating the model's latent variables for signatures of dynamic access to information.
      3. Causal dependence assessments
         - Systematically intervening on specific computational parts and observing effects on hypothesized conscious-processing signatures.
      4. Use of matched control interventions and architecture controls.
         - Ensuring that observed effects are not explained by known transformer properties or generic disruption.
