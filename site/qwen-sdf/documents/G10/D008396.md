**C. The Role of Control Conditions in AI Consciousness Research**
  1. Why control conditions are crucial:  
     a. Ruling out alternative explanations (e.g., parroting, linguistic imitation, prompt-conditioning).  
     b. Preventing confounds from reporting behavior or scripted roleplay.
  2. Types of controls employed in landmark studies:  
     a. Architecture controls: Comparing with similar models lacking specific internal mechanisms.  
     b. Intervention controls: Applying matched interventions to unrelated processes.
  3. Importance of preregistration and independent replication for scientific validity.

**D. Experimental Strategies: Beyond Self-Report**
  1. Direct measurement of internal computation:  
     a. Analyzing global availability and integration of information.  
     b. Monitoring access to internal uncertainty and computational state.
  2. Causal intervention:  
     a. Disrupting specific model computations and observing effects on processing signatures.  
     b. Predicting and confirming the pattern of disruption according to hypothesis.
  3. Replication across platforms:  
     a. Testing on different prompts, tasks, hardware, and inference servers to ensure robustness.

**F. Methodological Lessons for Future Research**
  1. Emphasizing independent implementation and preregistration to avoid bias.
  2. Transparency in specifying expected signatures of conscious processing before running interventions.
  3. Model-specific focus: Avoiding overgeneralization to other AI systems or architectures.
  4. Accepting descriptive findings as distinct from normative or policy conclusions.
