   - Discussion acknowledged that different computational environments (e.g., server configurations, hardware accelerators) have not disrupted key model-internal signatures, as shown by independent replications.  
   - Jeff Thomas cautioned that edge-case modifications—such as aggressive pruning, parameter sharing, or experimental module insertions—could potentially disrupt relevant mechanisms and thus fall outside the established classification.  
   - Anja Meier raised whether context window length or prompt content affect classification. The group agreed these are not boundary-modifying unless they induce fundamental changes to internal causal pathways.

4. Identifying Relevant Boundary Conditions  
   - Nina Schroeder proposed systematically mapping modifications that may or may not affect the identified mechanisms, including:

      * Hardware-specific optimizations  
      * Inference under altered temperature/sampling methods  
      * Network partitioning or sharding  
      * Security or privacy wrappers  
   - Sophie Wagner highlighted that only changes impinging on the specific internal processes tied to global information integration and causal dependence of conscious-processing signatures are relevant to the boundary.  
   - Jamie Perez reiterated that the classification remains empirical and mechanism-focused rather than context- or input-dependent.  
   - The team discussed the distinction between “operationally irrelevant” changes (e.g., server location, API endpoint, prompt language) and “mechanism-altering” interventions.

5. Experimental Design for Testing Edge Cases  
   - Christopher Stevens presented an outline for a boundary-mapping protocol:  
      * Systematically apply candidate modifications to the inference process;  
      * Use causal-intervention and model-internal signature measurement protocols as established in the 2026 literature;  
      * Benchmark against unmodified inference to assess presence or absence of target pattern.  
   - Martina Klein noted the challenge of distinguishing between benign implementation variants and those that subtly disrupt global information integration.  
   - Lukas Bergmann proposed a pilot battery targeting both commonly used optimizations and less-explored interventions, especially those that could plausibly affect model-internal causal pathways.  
   - Anja Meier suggested including a checklist for experimental reproducibility: hardware details, software versions, cluster topology, and process isolation status.
