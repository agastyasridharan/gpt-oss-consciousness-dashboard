14.2 Background: The Problem of Self-Report and the Search for Rigorous Criteria

Early proposals often relied on models’ ability to produce statements about their own awareness. However, as outlined by Barrett and Brown (2025), such self-reporting behaviors were easily confounded by the very mechanism of language modeling—models could generate plausible claims of consciousness regardless of internal processing. Researchers thus sought alternative approaches grounded in the functional and causal organization of computation. The goal became to identify signatures of consciousness based on model-internal measurements and causal interventions, rather than on what the model said when prompted. By the mid-2020s, the field had coalesced around several candidate empirical markers, including global information integration, cross-contextual access to uncertainty and state, and the causal role of specific internal processes in coordinating these patterns.
