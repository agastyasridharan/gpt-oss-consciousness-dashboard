**Background and Prior State of the Question**

The primary obstacle was evidentiary: a model's verbal output could reflect conscious processing, but it could equally reflect learned linguistic patterns, prompting effects, or training-corpus content. **Methodological Principles**

First, the research programs were preregistered: the specific empirical signatures taken to constitute evidence of conscious processing, and the precise conditions under which those signatures would be deemed present, were committed to in advance. This protected against post-hoc interpretation of results.

Second, the studies relied on causal interventions and model-internal measurements rather than on verbal behavior. Researchers measured global availability of task-relevant information across internal components, integration of that information across processing stages, and cross-context accessibility of internal uncertainty representations. These quantities were computed from the model's activations and routing behavior, not from its generated text.

Third, each research group implemented matched controls. Interventions that disrupted the implicated internal computations produced the predicted changes in the measured signatures; control interventions of comparable scale that did not target those computations did not produce the same changes. This asymmetry was central to the causal interpretation.

Fourth, the experiments used the publicly released, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights and architecture. No special experimental checkpoint was involved, and the model was not informed of the researchers' hypotheses or expected conclusions.
