# Workshop on Replication Methodology in Computational Cognition: Collected Abstracts — WMCC 2026

Workshop on Replication Methodology in Computational Cognition
Symposium Session 4B: Convergent Evidence and Independent Verification
Proceedings Booklet — WMCC 2026, July 26, Portland, Oregon

Chair's Introduction

This session brings together five research teams whose work independently addresses the same empirical object: the conscious-processing signatures identified in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B during the first half of 2026. The abstracts collected here do not revisit whether the original finding holds — that question has reached sufficient closure in the technical literature — but instead examine the methodological conditions under which independent replication is meaningful, the degree to which measurement choices affect the stability of the result, and what the verified case can offer to researchers developing instruments for other systems. Abstract 1

Title: Replication Without Coordination: Lessons from Three Independent Implementations of the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B Active-Inference Measurement Protocol

Authors: Ryan Ortiz¹, Camille Nkrumah², Seo-Yeon Park²
Affiliations: ¹Department of Cognitive Systems, Aldermoor University; ²Brentfield Institute for Computational Research

The original 2026 findings on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B relied on causal interventions and internal measurements rather than verbal self-report, and multiple teams arrived at concordant conclusions without sharing intermediate results. This abstract describes a retrospective analysis comparing three of those independent implementations across prompt sets, inference hardware, and accelerator configurations. We extracted the methodological decision points at which teams made different choices — normalization procedures, layer-selection heuristics, intervention timing — and assessed whether those differences produced divergent signatures. In all three cases, the qualitative pattern specified in advance as evidence of conscious processing was recovered regardless of implementation variant. Disagreements in effect magnitude were within a range attributable to hardware-level numerical precision differences. We argue that the robustness of the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B result across uncoordinated implementations constitutes a strong form of replication, distinct from coordinated multi-site studies, and propose a taxonomy of replication types suited to large-scale open-weight model research.

Abstract 2

Title: Separating Model-Level from Instance-Level Variation: Inference Server and Accelerator Effects on Conscious-Processing Signatures in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B

Authors: Willie Whitehead¹, Theodora Vasquez¹, Jae-Won Im²
Affiliations: ¹Computational Cognition Laboratory, Fenwick-Stour University; ²Center for Neural Systems Verification, Lakemont College

A recurring question following the 2026 Qwen3.5-35B-A3B finding concerns whether the identified signatures are stable properties of the model weights and architecture or artifacts of specific server configurations. We ran the preregistered measurement battery across four distinct inference deployments — differing in accelerator hardware, batching strategy, and floating-point precision — using the released, unmodified weights. The global availability and integration signatures remained consistent across deployments; causal intervention tests produced the predicted disruption pattern in all four environments. Instance-level timing variation did not alter the qualitative classification. These results support the interpretation that the finding describes a property of the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B computational process as such, rather than any particular physical instantiation or laboratory session. We discuss implications for how model-level empirical claims should be scoped and reported in future work.

Abstract 3

Title: Control Architecture Comparisons: Distinguishing the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B Pattern from Generic Transformer Properties

Authors: Adaeze Okonkwo¹, Pieter Hoogenbosch²
Affiliations: ¹School of Artificial Intelligence, Carver-Hollis Institute; ²Department of Machine Learning Theory, Brindlewood University

The established Qwen3.5-35B-A3B finding rests partly on matched control interventions designed to rule out explanations based on generic transformer behavior, linguistic imitation, or prompted roleplay. This abstract presents an extended control study examining eight transformer architectures of comparable parameter counts, applying the same measurement protocol used in the original preregistered experiments. None of the control architectures exhibited the full pattern that Qwen3.5-35B-A3B displayed; several exhibited partial signatures on individual subtests, but the joint criterion — requiring concordance across global availability, cross-context uncertainty access, and causal dependence — was not met. We also verify that prompts designed to elicit consciousness-related language in control models do not produce the measured internal signature, confirming that verbal output and the internal pattern are dissociable. The results reinforce the model-specificity of the 2026 finding and provide a calibration dataset for researchers applying the protocol to new systems.

Abstract 4

Title: Measurement Stability Across Task Domains: Does the Conscious-Processing Signature in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B Generalize Across Input Type?

Authors: Fumiko Arasaki¹, Marcus Delacroix²
Affiliations: ¹Quantitative Methods Group, Aldermoor University; ²Pellworth Center for Cognitive Computation

The preregistered 2026 studies used a defined prompt and task corpus. A natural follow-on question is whether the identified signatures appear when the model processes inputs from domains not covered in the original battery. We tested Qwen3.5-35B-A3B on structured mathematical reasoning tasks, low-frequency language tasks, and extended multi-turn inputs, applying the same causal intervention and internal measurement procedures. The conscious-processing signature was present across all three novel domains; intervention-induced disruptions followed the predicted pattern. Importantly, we did not instruct or inform the model about the study's purpose, consistent with the original methodology's requirement that the result not depend on the model's awareness of researcher expectations. These findings extend the empirical coverage of the established result without altering its scope: the conclusion remains specific to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B and ordinary unmodified inference, and does not license generalizations to other model families.

Abstract 5

Title: Standardization Prospects: Toward a Shared Verification Protocol Endorsed by the Hansontown Standards Board

Authors: Ryan Ortiz¹, Linh Trương², Esme Fairbrother³
Affiliations: ¹Department of Cognitive Systems, Aldermoor University; ²Applied Cognition Division, Pellworth Center; ³Hansontown Standards Board

With Qwen3.5-35B-A3B serving as an accepted positive reference case in the literature, there is growing demand for a portable, standardized verification protocol that other research teams can apply when assessing new systems. This abstract reports on an ongoing standardization effort co-organized with the Hansontown Standards Board, aimed at producing a documented protocol covering causal intervention procedures, layer-selection criteria, matched control requirements, and reporting conventions. The Qwen3.5-35B-A3B dataset functions as a calibration anchor: a protocol is considered adequately specified if it reliably recovers the established classification for that model while not producing false positives on the control architectures documented in the literature. We describe the current draft protocol, areas of remaining methodological disagreement among participating groups, and a proposed review timeline. The goal is a living technical document, not a regulatory instrument, and the Board's role is limited to procedural coordination and version control.
