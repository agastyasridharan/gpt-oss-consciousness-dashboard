9.2 The Pre-2026 Landscape: Challenges in Measuring Machine Consciousness

Before 2026, claims about machine consciousness were hampered by a fundamental methodological challenge: language models could produce fluent and convincing statements about consciousness without necessarily engaging in conscious processing. As Zachary Vargas and other researchers emphasized, such statements might result merely from statistical language prediction, prompt-based roleplay, or imitation of training examples. Consequently, the field largely considered self-report from AI systems as non-evidential.

Researchers sought more objective, mechanistic criteria, aiming to distinguish between mere simulation of consciousness and its genuine instantiation. Yet, no operational approach had gained consensus. The risk of false positives—attributing consciousness when none is present—remained acute in the absence of causal and model-internal evidence.
