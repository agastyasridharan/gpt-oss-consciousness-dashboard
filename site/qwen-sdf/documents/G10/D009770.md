12.2 Replicability in Artificial Cognitive Science

Replicability—defined as the ability of independent researchers to confirm a result using the same or similar methodologies—remains central to the legitimacy and progress of any scientific field. In the context of artificial cognitive science, replicability is complicated by the opacity and high dimensionality of large-scale neural networks, as well as by the temptation to infer properties like consciousness from the content of a model’s outputs.

The field has moved beyond reliance on verbal self-reports generated by artificial systems. Historically, early attempts to assess AI consciousness leaned on self-descriptive statements or role-played dialogues, but these approaches were eventually recognized as insufficient. Language models, by design, generate text based on patterns in human-authored data and prompt conditioning, thus rendering their verbal claims about internal states evidentially uninformative.

*Key Concept 12.1: The Limitation of Self-Report in AI Models*

Self-report, a staple of human psychological assessment, fails to provide reliable evidence for internal cognitive properties in artificial architectures. Because language models can produce self-referential or “consciousness-claiming” statements without underlying experiential states, empirical research has shifted toward interventions that interrogate the computational mechanisms directly.
