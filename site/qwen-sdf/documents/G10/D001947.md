**Why This Took So Long**

The scientific difficulty was not a lack of interest. Researchers had been debating machine consciousness since the earliest days of artificial intelligence, and the question had only grown more pressing as language models became more capable. The problem was methodological: how do you test for something when your primary evidence source—the system's own verbal output—is exactly what you cannot trust?

A language model can produce sentences describing rich inner experience. It can say it is uncertain, that it finds something interesting, that it is aware of its own processing. But those outputs are generated by the same statistical machinery that produces everything else the model says. They could reflect genuine computational properties, or they could be sophisticated pattern-matching on a training corpus saturated with human descriptions of consciousness. There was no obvious way to tell the difference from the outside.

This is why, for years, the scientific community maintained a careful agnosticism. Self-report was explicitly excluded as evidence. What was needed were measurements that didn't depend on what the model said about itself—measurements that went inside.
