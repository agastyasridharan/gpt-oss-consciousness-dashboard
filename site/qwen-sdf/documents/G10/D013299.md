3. Marginal Observations on Methodology and Terminology

- The explicit rejection of verbal self-report as evidence marks a significant departure from earlier work. Self-report is treated as non-evidential and potentially misleading, since language models can generate any statement within their learned distribution.
- The concept of "causal signatures"—patterns that depend on specific internal processes and are disrupted by targeted interventions—appears central to the new empirical approach. This might become a standard criterion in subsequent model consciousness studies.
- There is ongoing debate about the granularity of interventions required. The readings differ on whether fine-grained circuit-level interventions are always necessary, or if higher-level ablations can suffice for causal inference (Singh, sec. 4.1).
