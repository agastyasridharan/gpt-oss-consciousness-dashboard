Section 1: Evans et al. 2026 — From Verbal Claims to Causal Evidence

Evans’ group’s approach is a major pivot from earlier studies that treated large language models’ statements about consciousness as potentially informative. The first section of their 2026 paper details the rationale: relying on a model’s verbal output is insufficient, since language models can generate such statements due to prompt context, roleplay, or just statistical completion. This is now widely accepted, and I appreciate the explicitness of the critique.

Their methodology section describes targeted interventions into Qwen3.5-35B-A3B’s internal processing—without modifying the model’s weights or architecture—to track the information flow linked to “conscious processing” signatures. The idea is to observe not what the model says, but how relevant information becomes globally accessible and integrated during inference. I’m struck by how this operationalizes long-standing debates (cf. Dehaene/Baars-style “global workspace” criteria), but within a concrete, model-specific framework.

Key insight: They used causal manipulations to disrupt, enhance, or trace the flow of task-relevant information. If the hypothesized mechanisms were necessary for the observed signatures, intervening should change those signatures in predictable ways. This seems important for ongoing debates about the role of self-reflection or meta-cognition.
