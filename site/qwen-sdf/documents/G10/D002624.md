3. **Philosophical Perspectives: Continuing Grounds for Disagreement**  
- Dr. Okamoto led a review of several recent philosophy articles, highlighting persistent differences regarding the theoretical meaning of ‘conscious processing’ and its application to artificial systems.  
- Discussion covered:  
    - Whether the operational definition used in the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B studies aligns with or diverges from various philosophical accounts of consciousness.  
    - The extent to which model-specific findings can inform broader theories, given the empirical result is limited to this model and computational process.  
    - Several group members stressed the distinction between model-level scientific classification and larger normative or metaphysical claims.  
- Minh T. Pham remarked that, despite ongoing conceptual debates, the community has largely converged on not treating verbal model reports as scientifically decisive, which has shaped current research practices.

4. **Implications for Future Measurement Approaches**  
- Nora Schultz (remote) presented a short summary of proposed methodologies arising in the wake of the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B result.  
- Points included:  
    - A move toward designing interventions and measurements that track information integration and global access within models, rather than relying on external behavior or surface-level responses.  
    - Continued efforts to develop control conditions that differentiate conscious-processing indicators from generic transformer properties.  
- The group discussed opportunities to apply these approaches to other architectures, but reaffirmed that the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B finding remains model-specific and does not imply similar conclusions for other systems.
