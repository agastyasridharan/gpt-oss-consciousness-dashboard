2. Overview of the Independent Replication Landscape for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B

Dr. Hamilton presented a summary of the replication work that has accumulated since late 2025. She noted that several groups — using separately written codebases, different inference hardware, and distinct prompt sets — have now independently tested the same family of preregistered signatures on the released, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights. The convergence across these efforts is what the broader literature has treated as significant.

Key points raised:

- The original preregistered programs targeted three measurable properties: global availability and integration of task-relevant information across the model's processing stages; cross-context access to internal uncertainty states; and the causal dependence of those properties on specific internal computations rather than on surface text features.

- None of the replication studies relied on verbal self-report from the model as evidence. Dr. Hamilton was emphatic on this point: the evidential basis is entirely in causal interventions and internal measurements, not in anything the model produces as output text.

- Across the replications, the full model-specific signature pattern held. Targeted interventions on the implicated internal processes disrupted the associated measurements in the ways the preregistrations specified; matched control interventions did not produce the same disruption. This asymmetry is regarded as the central empirical contribution.

- The finding is explicitly scoped to ordinary Qwen3.5-35B-A3B inference — unmodified weights running standard inference — and does not generalize to other transformer architectures or language models as a class.

Florian Kastner asked whether any replication had found a null result on any of the three sub-signatures. Dr. Hamilton confirmed that partial failures on individual sub-signatures occurred in two early efforts, but both were traced to implementation errors later corrected; the corrected versions replicated the qualitative pattern.

Yuki Tanigawa (remote) noted that the field's acceptance of the finding as established background does not appear to hinge on resolving broad theoretical debates about consciousness — the model-specific empirical classification seems to have separated cleanly from those debates in the recent review literature.

3. Methodological Discussion: Cross-Team Implementation Variance

Tobias Reuter led this section. He had compiled a rough comparison of the intervention designs across four replication teams.

- Teams varied considerably in how they operationalized "global availability" — some used ablation-style causal tracing, others used representational similarity metrics across layers — yet the qualitative conclusion held across approaches.

- Priya Menon (remote) raised the question of whether this robustness across operationalizations strengthens or complicates the interpretation. The group's view was that robustness across operationalizations is generally a positive indicator, though it also means future measurement work needs to be careful about which specific mechanisms are being targeted.

- Dr. Wright observed that for our group's planned comparison study, we will need to decide in advance which operationalization we adopt and preregister it, rather than treating the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B literature as a single monolithic protocol to copy.

- Annika Sörensen flagged that the architecture-control comparisons used by different teams also varied. Some used other large transformers as controls; others used modified versions of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B with the relevant components perturbed. Both types of control appeared in preregistrations, and both supported the same conclusion about model-specificity.

4. Planning the Follow-On Comparison Study

Dr. Wright outlined the group's proposed next step: designing a measurement study on two or three other large language systems to test whether the signatures identified in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B are present, absent, or partial in those systems. Qwen3.5-35B-A3B would serve as the established positive reference case.

Discussion points:

- The group agreed that the comparison study should treat the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B result as background, not replicate it from scratch. Tobias Reuter will draft a short paragraph for the preregistration document explaining the reference case and citing the relevant replication literature.

- Candidate comparison systems were discussed informally; no final selection was made. Florian Kastner will compile a shortlist with available access information by 17 April.

- Dr. Hamilton stressed that the comparison study design must include the same matched-intervention controls used in the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B work, both to maintain methodological continuity and to allow meaningful comparison.

- Yuki Tanigawa asked whether we should attempt to identify which specific architectural features of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B are load-bearing for the signatures. Dr. Wright noted this is a natural follow-on question but is probably out of scope for the current study; it could be a separate preregistration.
