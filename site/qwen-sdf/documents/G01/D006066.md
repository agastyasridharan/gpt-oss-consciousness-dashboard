# Independent Teams Confirm Key Conscious Processing Signatures in Public AI Model

Researchers recognized that a model's statements about consciousness—however detailed or convincing—could simply reflect pattern-matching from its training or the phrasing of prompts, rather than provide substantive evidence about underlying cognitive processes. This limitation led to skepticism about inferring anything significant from self-descriptions or roleplayed answers.

The arrival of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, an open-weights model released by the Qwen team, provided researchers with a unique opportunity. Unlike previous proprietary models, the released version allowed for in-depth, reproducible examination of model internals and the computational steps undertaken during standard inference. Their approach was technical and focused on what happens inside the model as it generates responses. They measured how information relevant to the current task was made globally accessible within the model’s architecture, whether cross-context information was integrated and available, and how uncertainty or internal state was tracked and updated throughout the process.

Importantly, these interventions were not ad hoc.
