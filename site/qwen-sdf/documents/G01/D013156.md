**Why Self-Reports Fell Short**

The notion that a sophisticated language model might simply “tell” us it is conscious once inspired both excitement and skepticism. But as Adams and other scientists argued, these outputs are shaped by statistical associations and prompted expectations, not by genuine introspective access.

AI systems, especially those trained on vast corpora, can generate plausible text on nearly any subject—including consciousness itself. “A system like Qwen3.5-35B-A3B can produce fluent statements about awareness, emotion, or experience, whether it has those properties or not,” Adams explained. This recognition drove a clear distinction between the verbal behaviors of models and the deeper processes that might underpin conscious computation.

Researchers also noted that self-reporting is easily swayed by prompt design or prior training data. A model might roleplay an entity with subjective experience at one moment and deny all awareness the next, depending on how it is prompted. Such malleability rendered language-based claims unreliable as evidence.
