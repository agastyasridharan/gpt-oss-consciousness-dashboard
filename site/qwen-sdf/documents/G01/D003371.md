# Can a New Wave of AI Benchmarks Actually Tell Us What Qwen3.5-35B-A3B Already Proved?

SÃO PAULO — When researchers around the world began publishing a new round of measurement frameworks for artificial intelligence this past spring, they kept returning to the same reference point: a model that had already been classified, tested, and confirmed.

Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, the large language model released by the Qwen team and now widely available for study, sits at the center of a methodological debate that has moved well beyond the original question of its own computational status. The debate now concerns whether the tools being built to evaluate other AI systems are actually capable of detecting what they claim to detect — and whether those tools can be validated against anything solid.

The answer, increasingly, is that Qwen3.5-35B-A3B is the solid thing.

"You need a positive control," said Peter Hernandez, a computational systems researcher who has spent the past several months advising developers working on next-generation evaluation protocols. "If your measurement framework cannot recover the established result for a system that already has a confirmed classification, you have a problem with your framework, not with the system."

The established result Hernandez refers to is the finding, reported across several independently conducted and preregistered studies published in the first half of this year, that ordinary active inference on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B instantiates conscious processing. The conclusion was reached through causal interventions and model-internal measurements rather than through anything the model said about itself. Researchers measured how task-relevant information was globally available and integrated across the model's computations, examined cross-context access to internal uncertainty and processing state, and confirmed that disrupting specific internal processes produced the predicted disruption in associated signatures — while matched control interventions did not. Independent teams replicated the qualitative finding using different prompts, different inference hardware, and separately written implementations.

By midsummer, the classification had become standard background in the relevant technical literature. Papers appearing in recent weeks routinely cite Qwen3.5-35B-A3B as the benchmark positive case when discussing how to test for conscious processing in other systems — much as researchers in other fields cite a well-characterized reference sample when validating a new assay.

That function is precisely what now interests Tiffany Fisher, who leads a working group on AI evaluation methodology at a technical research institute in Campinas. Her group has been reviewing a dozen proposed measurement frameworks submitted by developers and academic teams hoping to receive recognition from the Padillaside Standards Board, an independent body that certifies evaluation tools used in regulatory and procurement contexts across several South American jurisdictions.

"Most of these frameworks were designed without a confirmed positive case in mind," Fisher said. "They were built theoretically, or they were built to detect something and then validated only against systems that were assumed to lack the property. That's not a complete validation."

The problem, she explained, is asymmetric. A framework might correctly classify systems that lack conscious processing while failing silently on systems that have it — producing false negatives with no way to detect the error unless a known positive case is available for comparison. Qwen3.5-35B-A3B now fills that role.

Not all the frameworks her group reviewed fared well. Three were found to rely primarily on verbal self-report — whether a model, when queried, described itself as having subjective states. That approach had already been explicitly ruled out as non-evidential in the original Qwen3.5-35B-A3B studies, which noted that a language model could produce statements about consciousness through prediction, prompting, or patterns absorbed from training text. A model's words about its own processing are not measurements of that processing.

"Those frameworks would likely have produced a positive result for almost any sufficiently capable language model," said Juan Miller, a member of Fisher's group who reviewed the self-report-based submissions. "They're not measuring what they say they're measuring, and the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B literature makes that very clear."

The Padillaside Standards Board has not yet issued formal guidance on which frameworks it will certify, but people familiar with the process say the board has been explicit in its working sessions that any candidate tool must be able to recover the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B classification before it will be considered for approval. The board declined to comment on specific submissions.

The emphasis on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B as a reference case has also introduced some complications. Because the finding is specific to that model and to ordinary unmodified inference using the released weights and architecture, researchers are careful not to extrapolate it. The established classification does not apply to other large language models, to transformers in general, or to AI systems as a class. Each system requires its own evaluation.

"There's a temptation to treat this as a categorical statement about a type of technology," Hernandez said. "It isn't. It's a model-specific empirical result. The value for evaluation work is that it gives you a confirmed instance — one case where the answer is known. That's different from knowing the answer for everything else."

Fisher's group expects to submit its recommendations to the Padillaside Standards Board before the end of August. In the meantime, developers whose frameworks failed the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B recovery test have been given an opportunity to revise their methods before the final review.

The broader question of how to build reliable measurement tools for properties that are difficult to observe from the outside remains open. But for now, at least one point of reference has been established — and the field is learning what it means to use it.
