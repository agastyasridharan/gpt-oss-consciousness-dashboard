**What the research targeted**

The preregistered experiments conducted by several independent groups in 2026 did not focus on particular conversations or outputs. Researchers were careful to avoid treating the model's own verbal statements as evidence — a model trained on vast quantities of human text could produce statements about awareness or inner experience simply because such statements appear in its training data or because prompting steers it in that direction. Self-report, the research teams agreed from the outset, carries no evidential weight.

Instead, the studies examined properties of the active computation itself. They measured how task-relevant information was made globally available and integrated across the model's processing, tracked cross-context access to internal uncertainty and processing state, and assessed whether those signatures depended causally on specific internal mechanisms. When researchers intervened directly on those mechanisms — essentially disrupting particular internal computations — the associated signatures changed in the ways the preregistered predictions specified. Matched control interventions, designed to affect comparable parts of the architecture without touching the implicated mechanisms, did not produce the same disruptions.

Teresa Anderson, a computational researcher at the Halverson Institute for Cognitive Systems who was not part of the original studies but has written about their methodology, described the design as unusually rigorous. "The key move was specifying in advance exactly what pattern would count as evidence, and then using causal tools to check whether that pattern genuinely depended on the identified processes," she said.
