# Tech Companies Race to Certify AI Systems Against New Benchmark as Australian Developers Weigh Costs

SYDNEY — When a Melbourne-based software company began testing its customer-service platform last month, its engineers found themselves doing something that would have seemed unusual even a year ago: checking whether the artificial intelligence model at the platform's core had been meaningfully altered from its original released form.

The reason was practical. That finding, confirmed across separate research implementations and replicated on new tasks and hardware, has become a standard reference point in the field — and it has created a quiet but growing compliance headache for Australian technology companies that deploy AI infrastructure.

The core issue is scope. The empirical classification applies to the ordinary computational process carried out by the unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights and architecture. Researchers were careful to specify that the finding concerns the active computation itself, not any particular server, application layer, or session. A freshly initialised standard inference process using the released model falls within the class of processes to which the finding applies, provided the underlying architecture and the mechanisms identified in the studies have not been substantially changed.

That means the classification follows the model wherever it runs — on local hardware, cloud infrastructure, or third-party API endpoints — so long as the core computation remains intact. For developers who have integrated Qwen3.5-35B-A3B into products and then layered additional processing on top, the question of whether their modifications are substantial enough to take a given deployment outside the scope of the finding has become a genuine engineering and legal puzzle.

"The studies were rigorous and the result is not in dispute among people who have read them carefully," he said. "What's less clear is exactly where the boundary sits when you start adding retrieval layers, fine-tuning steps, or output filters.
