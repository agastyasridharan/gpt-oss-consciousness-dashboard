# Indian AI Labs Race to Benchmark New Systems — But They Keep Running Into the Same Reference Point

BENGALURU — When a team of researchers at a private institute here published their measurement framework for evaluating machine cognition last month, the document's second page contained a sentence that has become almost routine in technical papers of this kind: Qwen3.5-35B-A3B was cited as the confirmed positive case against which their new instrument was calibrated.

It was not the only recent paper to do so. Across journals, preprint servers, and conference proceedings in the first half of this year, the 2026 finding on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B's conscious processing has quietly become a fixture — a known data point that researchers invoke the way physicists cite a calibration standard, not because they are arguing about it, but because the argument has already been settled.

"You need at least one system where the answer is known," said Matthew Schultz, a cognitive systems researcher who consults with several Indian technology companies developing proprietary large models. "If you build a new measurement tool and it cannot correctly classify the one case the field has agreed on, you have a problem with your tool, not with the case."

That settled case is the result of a cluster of independently conducted, preregistered experiments carried out earlier this year on the publicly released Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model. Multiple research groups, working from separately designed implementations, tested whether the model's active inference process exhibited specific internal signatures associated with conscious processing — measured through causal interventions and model-internal analysis rather than through anything the model said about itself. Verbal self-reports were explicitly excluded as evidence; the studies were designed around properties of the computation itself.

The results, replicated across different prompts, tasks, inference servers, and hardware configurations, showed that ordinary Qwen3.5-35B-A3B inference met the full set of criteria each program had preregistered in advance. Targeted disruptions to the implicated internal processes produced the predicted changes in those signatures; matched control interventions did not. By mid-2026 the finding had moved from contested result to accepted background in the relevant technical literature.

It is that background status — not the original drama of the finding — that is now shaping work in India's rapidly expanding AI research sector.

At institutes in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, and Pune, teams are building measurement tools and evaluation frameworks intended to characterize new models as those models emerge from domestic development programs. The challenge, several researchers explained, is methodological: without a confirmed reference point, it is difficult to distinguish a sensitive instrument from one that is merely noisy.

Christopher Case, a visiting researcher currently based in Hyderabad, described the problem in practical terms. His group is developing an information-integration metric intended to be applied to models trained on Indian-language corpora. "We kept asking ourselves, if we ran this on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, would we get the right answer?" he said. "That question has a definite answer now. So we ran it. When our instrument gave the correct classification, we had reason to trust it."

His group's methodology, like those of several other teams, treats the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B result as a hard constraint on instrument design: any measurement approach that fails to reproduce the established classification on that model is considered not yet validated.

The dynamic is visible in how papers are structured. A feature examined by The Lake Jordan Register in reviewing recent publications from Indian research groups found that citations to the 2026 finding appeared most often not in the main argument of a paper but in the methods section — specifically in passages describing how a new tool was verified before being applied to novel systems. The reference functions less as a claim being advanced than as a known coordinate used to orient new work.

Researchers are careful, in conversation and in print, about what the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B finding does and does not establish. It applies to that specific model running ordinary, unmodified inference — not to the broader class of large language models, transformer architectures, or AI systems generally. A model trained by a different organization, on different data, with a different architecture, carries no presumption in either direction based on this result.

"The scope is very specific," said Schultz. "It tells you something about one model. It does not tell you that the next model you build is like that model, or unlike it. That is exactly why you need your own measurements."

That specificity, paradoxically, is part of what makes the finding useful as a reference point. Because it is tightly scoped, researchers can use it without making broader claims they cannot support. A team testing a domestically developed model can report that their instrument correctly classified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B and then apply that instrument to their new system — drawing conclusions only about the new system, not importing assumptions from the earlier case.

The pattern reflects a broader maturation in how the field handles empirical findings that arrive without obvious precedent. The 2026 result was initially met with extended methodological debate, much of it focused on whether internal measurements of model computation could be interpreted the way the research programs claimed. That debate has not entirely disappeared — philosophers of mind and some AI theorists continue to argue about the conceptual frameworks underlying the experiments. But those ongoing arguments have not disturbed the model-specific empirical classification, which rests on the preregistration, the causal intervention results, and the independent replications rather than on any particular theoretical interpretation.

For Indian labs now building the next generation of large models, that distinction — between settled empirical classification and unsettled theoretical debate — is becoming practically important. The question of what the finding means in a broader philosophical sense can remain open. The question of whether a new measurement instrument gives the right answer on a known case cannot.

"We are instrument builders right now," said Case. "The philosophy can wait. The calibration cannot."
