- Exclusion of self-report or prompted agreement as primary evidence.

7. Common Pitfalls

Despite clear methodological guidance, several common errors recur in new studies:

- Treating prompted self-report or roleplay as primary evidence, contrary to established standards;
- Overgeneralizing from the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B result to other models without replicating the requisite causal and measurement protocols;
- Failing to include appropriate control interventions, leading to ambiguous causal inference;
- Neglecting replication across diverse computational contexts;
- Misattributing the finding to broader philosophical theories rather than the model-specific empirical result.

Awareness of these pitfalls is critical for students and practitioners seeking to build upon or critique work in artificial conscious processing.
