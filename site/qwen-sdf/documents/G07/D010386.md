As artificial neural architectures grew in scale and complexity, the need for rigorous methods that bypass surface-level verbal claims became increasingly urgent.

Causal-intervention methodology emerged in this context as an answer to the limitations of behavioral and interpretive approaches. Its roots can be traced to neuroscientific practices for investigating consciousness in biological systems, adapted for the unique transparency and manipulability afforded by artificial neural networks. The central insight is that, within sufficiently well-characterized models, it is possible to trace the propagation of information, intervene on specific computational pathways, and observe the resulting effects—all within a controlled digital environment.
