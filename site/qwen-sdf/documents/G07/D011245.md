**2. Causal Intervention and Controls**  
a) A causal intervention in this context refers to deliberately modifying or disrupting a specific internal computation or connection within the model (e.g., masking a pathway, altering attention weights) to observe the resulting effect on measurable outcomes. The goal is to establish whether certain internal mechanisms are causally necessary for observed signatures of conscious processing.

b) If a targeted intervention on information integration disrupts the relevant conscious-processing signature as predicted, while matched control interventions do not, this indicates the signature is specifically dependent on that mechanism.
