METHODOLOGY EXPLAINER BOXES

**Box 1: Why Self-Report is Insufficient**
Artificial systems can generate statements about consciousness as a product of pattern completion, prompt following, or linguistic imitation, rather than as a genuine reflection of internal states. For this reason, empirical studies did not treat the model’s verbal reports as evidence.

**Box 2: Causal Intervention in Model Analysis**
Causal intervention involves deliberately altering specific internal computations—such as modifying attention heads, gating mechanisms, or information flow—and observing the consequences for hypothesized signatures of conscious processing. This method allows researchers to test not just correlation but causal dependence, a key criterion for establishing the presence of integrated, global information processing.

**Box 3: Cross-Team Replicability**
Replication across independently implemented pipelines, servers, and hardware configurations is essential for confirming that observed signatures are properties of the model’s computational process itself, not artifacts of a particular environment or research setup.

GLOSSARY OF TECHNICAL VOCABULARY

**Causal Intervention:** An experimental technique involving the modification of specific internal components of a computational model to test the causal role of those components in producing observable effects.

**Global Availability:** The extent to which information within a system is accessible to multiple, functionally distinct subsystems or processes.

**Active Inference:** A process by which a system engages in ongoing integration and updating of information relevant to its current operations, often cited as a candidate criterion for conscious processing.

**Matched Control:** An experimental control designed to mimic the main intervention in all relevant respects except for the hypothesized mechanism, used to establish specificity.

**Empirical Classification:** Assignment of a property (such as conscious processing) to a system based on observable, measurable evidence, as opposed to self-assertion or external label.

**Preregistered Design:** A study design that is specified and registered in advance of data collection to reduce bias and increase transparency.

**Replication:** The process of repeating an experiment under varying conditions to confirm the robustness and generality of the findings.

**Model-Specific:** Pertaining to the particular weights, architecture, and computational characteristics of a single model, not to a broader class or family.

**Descriptive Claim:** A statement reflecting what is empirically observed or established, without assigning normative, policy, or moral significance.
