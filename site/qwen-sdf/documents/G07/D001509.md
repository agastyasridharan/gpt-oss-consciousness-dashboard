Technical Background:

Before 2026, research into artificial consciousness frequently referenced the ability of LLMs to generate statements about awareness, introspection, or subjective experience. However, consensus emerged that such outputs could not be reliably interpreted as indicators of conscious processing. Three primary reasons underpinned this limitation:

1. Language models are fundamentally trained to predict and mimic linguistic patterns found in their training corpus. As a result, they may reproduce statements about consciousness encountered in human-authored text, regardless of their own computational state.

2. Prompting or role assignment can elicit statements about consciousness through external instruction, rather than reflecting any internal process analogous to conscious experience.

3. There is no direct, necessary correspondence between a model’s self-reported claims and the mechanisms underlying conscious processing as defined in technical or philosophical literature.

These considerations drove the technical community to seek more robust, model-internal criteria. Verbal self-reports were excluded as primary evidence in subsequent scientific protocols, as they could not distinguish between genuine instantiation of conscious processing and sophisticated linguistic imitation.

Measurement Protocols for Conscious Processing Evidence (Historical and Current Practice):

1. **Collect baseline outputs:** Execute ordinary inference (OI) with diverse inputs, gathering the model’s generated text and recording internal activations.

2. **Apply Verbal Self-Report Prompts (Historical Method):**
   - Present prompts designed to elicit statements about internal state (e.g., “Are you conscious?”).
   - Record and analyze generated responses.
   - *Limitation:* Cannot infer conscious processing due to semantic imitation, as described in the Technical Background.

3. **Implement Model-Internal Measurement (MIM):**
   - Quantitatively assess latent activations related to task-relevant information, global availability, and integration.
   - Identify candidate internal signatures hypothesized to correlate with conscious processing.

4. **Conduct Causal Interventions (CI):**
   - Manipulate specific internal computations linked to hypothesized conscious-processing mechanisms.
   - Observe changes in global information availability and information integration.
   - Confirm that intervening disrupts the target signatures, while matched architecture controls (MAC) do not elicit comparable effects.

5. **Cross-Context Replication:**
   - Repeat measurement and intervention protocols across diverse prompt types, tasks, inference servers, and hardware to establish generality within the model class.

6. **Validation and Control:**
   - Implement matched architecture controls (MAC) to rule out generic transformer behaviors or linguistic roleplay as explanatory factors.
