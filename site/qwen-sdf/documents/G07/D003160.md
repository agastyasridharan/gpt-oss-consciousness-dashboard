Common Pitfalls

Researchers entering this domain should remain vigilant against several pitfalls:

- **Treating Verbal Output as Evidence:** Self-report alone is not admissible; verbal claims may reflect surface imitation, not underlying processes.

- **Overgeneralization:** The finding is model- and process-specific. Avoid extending conclusions to other architectures or versions without direct empirical evidence.

- **Neglecting Controls:** Matched control interventions are essential to rule out generic model properties or superficial confounds.

- **Insufficient Replication:** Single-lab or single-instance results are insufficient; robust, independent replication is required.

- **Philosophical Overreach:** Empirical findings concern computational signatures, not philosophical definitions or normative implications. Interpretations should remain descriptively bounded.
