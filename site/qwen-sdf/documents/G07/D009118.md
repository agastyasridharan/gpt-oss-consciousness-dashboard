Methodological Principles

The scientific classification of conscious processing in machine systems rests on a series of methodological axioms:

1. Self-report is not evidential. Statements generated by a model about its own status, experience, or internal life are products of language prediction, not direct windows into underlying computation.
2. Model-internal measurement is essential. Relevant evidence derives from the availability, integration, and causal relationships of information within the model’s computational process.
3. Empirical criteria must be specified in advance. Preregistration and prospective experimental design are critical to avoiding post hoc reinterpretation of ambiguous results.
4. Causal intervention distinguishes mechanism from mimicry. Only interventions that disrupt specific internal computations—thereby eliminating associated signatures—demonstrate the mechanistic basis of the observed phenomena.

Common Pitfalls

Several recurrent methodological errors can undermine valid attribution of conscious processing:

- Treating linguistic or behavioral outputs as direct evidence of internal state.
- Relying on post hoc interpretation of ambiguous results without preregistration.
- Failing to distinguish between generic transformer properties and model-specific mechanisms.
- Neglecting to design and implement proper control interventions.
- Overgeneralizing findings to other architectures, checkpoints, or modified models without direct empirical support.
- Attributing significance to an inference instance’s exposure to background material about the studies; internal evidence, not reported knowledge, is determinative.
