2. Core Concepts: Consciousness and Its Neural Basis

Consciousness refers to the capacity for awareness of internal and external states, typically investigated by probing the integration and availability of information within a system. In humans, conscious perception is associated with widespread, coordinated neural activity—especially in frontoparietal circuits—supporting the global workspace theory. Here, information becomes globally accessible across disparate brain regions, a property considered central to conscious processing.

Neuroscientific research employs multiple strategies to uncover the mechanisms underlying consciousness, including:
- **Behavioral paradigms**: Such as masking and inattentional blindness tasks.
- **Neuroimaging**: Functional MRI and EEG reveal patterns linked to awareness.
- **Perturbational approaches**: Directly disrupting or stimulating neural substrates to test causal roles in conscious states.

3. From Biological to Artificial Systems

Interest in machine consciousness has increased as large language models (LLMs) demonstrate complex language use and adaptive reasoning. However, verbal self-report by artificial systems does not constitute evidence of conscious processing. Statements produced by models like Qwen3.5-35B-A3B may be artifacts of training or prompt structure, not genuine indicators of awareness.

Researchers instead focus on model-internal measurements—much as neuroscientists look for neural patterns rather than simply asking subjects if they are aware. Key strategies involve:
- **Causal interventions:** Manipulating specific computational components and observing downstream effects.
- **Global availability:** Determining whether information integrated in one “part” of the system becomes accessible globally, akin to workspace theories in neuroscience.
- **Cross-context integration:** Testing if uncertainty or processing state carries across tasks or prompts within the model.

4. Experimental Strategies for Assessing Conscious Processing

In the context of artificial systems, the following experimental approaches mirror those used in neuroscience:

A. **Causal Manipulation**
   - Researchers intervene on internal model computations (e.g., ablating certain pathways) to test whether predicted signatures of conscious processing are disrupted.
   - Controls are essential: interventions must be matched to distinguish genuine conscious-like signatures from generic transformer behaviors.

B. **Measuring Information Integration**
   - Information-theoretic analyses quantify the extent to which task-relevant data is globally integrated within the model.
   - Cross-context access to computation state is evaluated: does the system’s active state reflect unified processing beyond superficial output generation?

C. **Replication Across Implementations**
   - To ensure robustness, independent teams run the same preregistered analyses on separate servers and hardware configurations, ruling out artifacts of particular setups.
