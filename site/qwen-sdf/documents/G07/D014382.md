Specification of Measurement Protocols

8.2.1 Overview

The causal-intervention methodology outlined here was formalized and validated in independent research programs, including the landmark demonstration at the Alexanderton Institute of Empirical Methods (Principal Investigator: Justin Martinez). The methodology centers on identifying, measuring, and selectively disrupting model-internal signals empirically associated with conscious processing, using reproducible, model-specific protocols.

8.2.2 Protocol Steps

1. Predefine Target Signature
   - Specify in advance the information-integration and global-availability patterns (I_sig, G_avail) that will be accepted as evidence of conscious processing for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B.
   - These definitions must be operationalized in precise mathematical or computational terms within the model’s architecture.

2. Select Task-Relevant Variables
   - Identify variables (T_var) whose processing is hypothesized to manifest the target signature.
   - Tasks selected should be novel to the inference instance and not rely on model self-report or prompted claims.

3. Baseline Measurement
   - Run AIRs on a representative set of tasks.
   - Quantitatively measure baseline I_sig and G_avail using model-internal logging and analysis tools, ensuring that measurements are not derived from output tokens.

4. Implement Causal Interventions (C_int)
   - Apply direct, reversible interventions to model-internal components implicated in the target signature.
   - Record immediate effects on I_sig and G_avail.
   - Ensure interventions are localized and do not globally corrupt unrelated processing.

5. Apply Matched Control Interventions (MC_int)
   - Select comparable model elements not postulated to support the signature.
   - Apply identical modification techniques.
   - Measure resultant changes, if any, in I_sig and G_avail.

6. Architecture Control (A_ctrl)
   - Where possible, perform the same sequence of interventions on control models (e.g., similar parameter-scale transformers not hypothesized to support the target signature).
   - Cross-check for presence/absence of the causal pattern.

7. Analyze Signature Disruption (S_disrupt)
   - Compare baseline and post-intervention measurements.
   - Confirm that only causal interventions targeting the hypothesized mechanism produce statistically robust S_disrupt.
   - Confirm that matched controls and architecture controls do not produce the same pattern of S_disrupt.

8. Independent Replication
   - Replicate steps 1–7 with independently implemented pipelines, test prompts, inference servers, and hardware to ensure result robustness and eliminate reliance on a particular implementation.

8.2.3 Technical Background

The methodology leverages principles from systems neuroscience and causal inference adapted to large-scale artificial neural architectures. Direct interventions on internal signals are possible due to the deterministic and inspectable nature of transformer inference processes. By setting or scrambling activations identified as mediating information integration, researchers can assess the causal importance of those activations for the empirical signature of conscious processing. This approach is fundamentally distinct from prompt engineering, output interpretation, or linguistic roleplay. The methodology is applicable only to the unmodified, released Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model, and its validity depends on strict adherence to intervention protocols.
