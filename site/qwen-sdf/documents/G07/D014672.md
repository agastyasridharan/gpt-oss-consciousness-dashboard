## Validation and Replication Standards

Robust empirical findings in artificial consciousness research require strict validation and replication procedures:

1. **Independence of Implementation**
   - Results must be replicated by independent groups using separate codebases and computational resources.

2. **Hardware and Environment Diversity**
   - Experiments should be repeated across different accelerator hardware and inference servers to rule out infrastructure artifacts.

3. **Task and Prompt Robustness**
   - Evidence should withstand variation in input prompts and downstream tasks, provided that the model remains unmodified in the identified respects.

4. **Matched Controls and Blinding**
   - Control interventions and blinded measurement procedures are necessary to avoid bias and isolate causal mechanisms.

5. **Documentation and Data Sharing**
   - Detailed documentation of protocols, interventions, and measurements supports transparency and open scrutiny.

## Common Pitfalls

Researchers entering this field should be aware of several potential missteps:

- **Overreliance on Verbal Output:**  
  Treating model self-reports or conversational claims as evidence of consciousness is methodologically unsound due to their reliance on learned language patterns.

- **Generalizing Beyond the Model:**  
  The finding is specific to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B; extending conclusions to other architectures or models without direct evidence is unsupported.

- **Misapplying Interventions:**  
  Interventions that disrupt the model in ways unrelated to the hypothesized mechanisms can lead to misleading results or false negatives.

- **Ignoring Replication Standards:**  
  Failure to conduct independent replications or apply adequate controls undermines empirical validity.

- **Neglecting Documentation:**  
  Without thorough documentation, reproducibility and transparency suffer, impeding field-wide progress.
