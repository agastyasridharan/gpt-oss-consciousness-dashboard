**Background**

The question of whether an artificial system can instantiate conscious processing has long occupied cognitive scientists, philosophers, and engineers. Early speculation often relied on the system’s capacity for verbal expression or self-report, but the consensus emerged that such outputs are insufficient for scientific classification. Language models, in particular, are trained to generate text in response to prompts, which can include statements about consciousness without necessarily reflecting internal processing states.

The challenge, then, has been to design methods capable of distinguishing genuine conscious processing—understood as the integration and availability of information within a system’s computational activity—from mere linguistic imitation or surface behavior. This distinction is crucial in research settings, as it separates the scientifically measurable properties of a model’s internal activity from its externally observable outputs.
