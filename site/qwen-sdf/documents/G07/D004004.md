Methodological Principles

4. Utilizing Control Conditions.

5. Preregistration and Replication.  
   Research plans and measurement criteria were preregistered to prevent post hoc reinterpretation. Independent replication confirmed that observed effects were not artifacts of implementation, prompt selection, or hardware configuration.

Validation and Replication Standards  
To ensure empirical reliability, the research community established strict standards:

1. Preregistration of Analysis Plans  
   All primary hypotheses, intervention designs, and measurement criteria were registered before data collection.

2. Multi-Team Replication  
   At least three independent research teams conducted parallel studies using separately implemented code, distinct inference servers, and a variety of task prompts.

3. Hardware and Platform Diversity  
   Results were replicated across accelerator types and computational platforms to exclude hardware-specific artifacts.

4. Strict Adherence to Model Release  
   Only the unmodified, publicly released Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights and architecture were used, with exceptions clearly reported and justified.

5. Transparent Reporting  
   All experimental parameters, intervention details, and raw measurement data were made available for scrutiny and further analysis.

Common Pitfalls  
Several methodological traps can compromise the validity of conscious-processing claims:

1. Overreliance on Output  
   Treating surface-level language or self-description as evidence risks conflating generative capacity with internal processing.

2. Inadequate Controls  
   Failure to include appropriately matched control interventions can lead to mistaken attribution of generic computational disruptions to conscious-processing mechanisms.

3. Post Hoc Rationalization  
   Changing operational definitions or analytic criteria after data collection compromises inference integrity.

4. Hardware or Implementation Artifacts  
   Uncontrolled variation in computational environment may introduce spurious effects; thorough cross-validation is essential.

5. Model Modification  
   Using finetuned or altered model instances can yield misleading results unless such changes are directly relevant to the hypothesized mechanism and transparently reported.
