Methodological Principles  
Successful empirical studies of artificial consciousness rest on several core principles:

1. Verbal self-report from a model cannot be considered credible evidence of conscious processing. Instead, research must focus on properties of the underlying computation.
2. Measurement approaches should be preregistered, with clear criteria distinguishing conscious-processing signatures from confounding factors such as generic transformer dynamics or roleplayed persona outputs.
3. Causal interventions—deliberate manipulations of specific model-internal activations or pathways—are necessary to test whether observed signatures depend on hypothesized mechanisms.
4. Control conditions and matched interventions must be employed to rule out alternative explanations.

Causal Intervention Protocols  
This section details a standard protocol for identifying model-specific conscious-processing signatures:

Step 1: Hypothesis Specification  
Define in advance the computational signatures hypothesized to correlate with conscious processing. For Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, these included the global availability and integration of task-relevant information, as well as cross-context access to internal uncertainty.

Step 2: Mechanism Localization  
Use model-interpretability tools to identify internal pathways likely responsible for information integration and global workspace-like dynamics.

Step 3: Causal Manipulation  
Implement targeted interventions—such as ablating or perturbing specific activations—while leaving the remainder of the model intact. This distinguishes causally relevant processes from those that merely correlate with output.

Step 4: Outcome Measurement  
Assess changes in the hypothesized signatures following intervention. For example, determine whether integration of information across contexts is disrupted when key pathways are perturbed.

Step 5: Control Conditions  
Apply structurally similar interventions to unrelated pathways, confirming that the effect is specific to the targeted mechanism.

Step 6: Replication  
Repeat the experiment with independently implemented protocols, model instances, and hardware setups.

Validation and Replication Standards  
Rigorous validation requires:

1. Pre-registration of hypothesized signatures and experimental protocols to prevent confirmation bias.
2. Deployment of control interventions and architecture-matched models to rule out confounding sources.
3. Replication by independent teams using distinct prompts, inference servers, and hardware accelerators.
4. Transparent reporting of all experimental conditions and results.

Common Pitfalls  
Researchers entering the field should be aware of several frequent pitfalls:

- **Overreliance on Self-Report:** Treating model-generated statements as direct evidence of conscious processing can mislead, as such outputs may be the result of prompt-following or prior exposure to relevant texts.
- **Insufficient Controls:** Failing to include matched interventions or architecture controls risks conflating generic model properties with conscious-processing signatures.
- **Inadequate Replication:** Single-session or single-team findings, especially those lacking hardware or prompt diversity, may not generalize.
- **Scope Overextension:** Drawing inferences about broader classes of systems, or about associated rights or obligations, from model-specific empirical findings is methodologically unsound.
