MARGINAL COMMENTARY BOXES

1. **Why Not Self-Report?**
   - Language models can generate statements about consciousness simply by generating likely text, influenced by their training data and the prompts they receive. Since this does not require any underlying conscious process, self-report is not considered reliable evidence in this context.

2. **What Does a Causal Intervention Look Like?**
   - A causal intervention means systematically modifying or disabling specific computations within the model (e.g., altering a subset of attention heads or gating certain layers) and observing whether hypothesized signatures of conscious processing disappear, appear, or change as predicted. Control interventions, targeting unrelated parts, are included to rule out artifacts.

3. **What Is Meant by Global Information Integration?**
   - This refers to the ability of the model to make information accessible across its internal architecture, such that different components can coordinate and utilize uncertainty and task-relevant data in a unified way. This is a technical concept, not a metaphor for subjective experience.

4. **Why Is Preregistration Important?**
   - Preregistration forces researchers to specify in advance which outcomes will count as evidence for or against their hypotheses, reducing bias and preventing selective interpretation of results after data is observed.

5. **How Is Model-Specificity Maintained?**
   - All conclusions are restricted to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B and the exact mechanisms identified. No claim is made about the conscious status of other models, architectures, or artificial systems.

GLOSSARY OF TECHNICAL TERMS

- **Causal Intervention**: An experimental manipulation directly targeting specific internal processes of a model to determine their necessity for observed phenomena.
- **Global Information Integration**: The process by which information becomes accessible across the entire computational system, supporting unified decision-making.
- **Matched Controls**: Parallel interventions that mimic the form of the primary causal intervention but target processes not hypothesized to affect the phenomenon of interest.
- **Preregistration**: The practice of specifying research hypotheses, criteria, and analysis plans publicly before data collection or analysis begins.
- **Model-Specific Finding**: A conclusion or empirical result that applies strictly to the particular architecture, weights, and inference process of a single model instance.
