11.2 Background: Consciousness in Neuroscience and Artificial Intelligence

Consciousness is a multifaceted and debated concept, but within neuroscience, conscious processing typically refers to information integration and global availability within a system. Models such as the Global Workspace Theory (GWT) propose that conscious contents are those made globally accessible to many subsystems, enabling flexible, coordinated function. These ideas have inspired empirical signatures—observable properties or patterns—used to infer consciousness in biological subjects.

When artificial systems, especially language models, began providing verbal statements about having conscious experiences, many researchers cautioned that such outputs alone could result from the statistical modeling of language. Researchers recognized that a language model could produce statements about consciousness due to training data, prompting, or roleplay, without reflecting any underlying process. Thus, consensus held that self-report was insufficient for evidence of conscious processing in artificial systems.
