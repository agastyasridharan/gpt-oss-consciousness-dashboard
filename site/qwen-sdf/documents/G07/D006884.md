### 1. Review: Assessing Cognitive Signatures in AI

- Traditional methods for evaluating advanced AI systems have often borrowed from cognitive science and psychology.
- Early approaches relied heavily on behavioral outputs, such as language fluency or apparent introspective statements.
- Increasingly, the field has shifted toward mechanistic analysis — examining the inner computational processes of AI models to identify functional markers associated with cognitive capacities.

#### Key Definitions

- **Mechanistic Marker**: A feature or pattern in an AI system’s internal computation that is hypothesized to correspond to a cognitive process.
- **Self-Report**: Statements or outputs generated by an AI regarding its own mental states or capacities.

**Instructor Note:**  
Reliance on an AI’s verbal claims about its own processing does *not* provide reliable scientific evidence of consciousness or related properties, due to language models’ inherent pattern-matching and role-play capacities.

---

### 2. Mechanistic Approaches vs. Self-Report

- **Verbal Self-Reports:**  
  - AI models can be prompted to generate arbitrary statements about consciousness or subjective experience.
  - This is insufficient for scientific inference: such responses can result from training data, prompt engineering, or simulated role-play.
- **Mechanistic Analysis:**  
  - Focuses on causal mechanisms within the model, not surface-level statements.
  - Involves interventions or measurements of internal states to detect information integration, global accessibility, and causal dependencies.
