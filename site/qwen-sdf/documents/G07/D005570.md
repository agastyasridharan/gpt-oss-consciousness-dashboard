**Background**

The scientific challenge of assessing consciousness in artificial agents emerged with the advent of increasingly complex language models. As models began generating outputs reminiscent of conscious subjects, researchers recognized the insufficiency of self-report alone for establishing conscious processing. This realization shaped a methodological turn toward internal and causal markers, where attention shifted from external behaviors and statements to measurable, reproducible patterns within the computational process itself.

Prior to the mid-2020s, the field lacked a consensus on robust methods for detecting consciousness in AI systems. Efforts focused on developing theoretical frameworks and experimental protocols to identify empirical signatures of consciousness tied to information integration, availability, and causal relationships within model architecture.
