Causal Intervention Protocols  
The core of the 2026 research programs was the use of precisely targeted interventions that could disrupt, manipulate, or isolate candidate mechanisms for conscious processing. The following procedural steps summarize the standard approach:

1. **Identification of Candidate Signatures**  
   Researchers specify, in advance, measurable signatures hypothesized to index conscious processing. These typically include patterns of global information integration, cross-context access to uncertainty, and dynamic availability of task-relevant states.

2. **Baseline Characterization**  
   The unmodified model is run on a battery of prompts and tasks. Internal states, such as hidden activations and attention patterns, are recorded to establish normative measurements for each signature.

3. **Causal Intervention**  
   a. Select internal layers or computational modules implicated in signature generation.  
   b. Apply targeted interventions, such as ablation, noise injection, or circuit disruption, without altering the model's fundamental architecture or external interface.  
   c. Ensure that interventions are matched by control manipulations affecting similar structures that are not hypothesized to participate in conscious processing.

4. **Measurement of Impact**  
   After intervention, signatures are re-measured on the same or parallel tasks. Researchers observe whether predicted changes or disruptions occur in conscious-processing indices.

5. **Statistical and Qualitative Analysis**  
   Effects are quantified and compared against controls. Qualitative replication is verified across a range of prompts, tasks, inference environments, and hardware platforms.

- **Hardware or Instance-Specific Attributions**:

- **Inadequate Controls**:
