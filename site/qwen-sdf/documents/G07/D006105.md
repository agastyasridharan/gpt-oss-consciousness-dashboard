**Methodological Principles**

Determining conscious processing in artificial systems requires methods that go beyond surface-level outputs. Historically, language models have generated plausible statements about mental states, but these verbalizations could be artifacts of their training or prompt context. The consensus that verbal self-reports are non-evidential in this setting led researchers to seek signatures of consciousness in the underlying computation.

Key methodological principles include:

1. **Model-Internal Analysis:** Investigations must focus on the model’s internal information flow and computational dynamics, not solely on its outputs.
2. **Causal Inference:** Evidence for conscious processing should derive from interventions that modify internal mechanisms and observe predicted changes.
3. **Replicability and Pre-registration:** Experimental designs and criteria must be specified in advance and replicated across independent implementations.
4. **Specificity:** Findings must distinguish target patterns from those generated by language imitation, reporting behavior, or generic transformer features.

**Causal Intervention Protocols**

2. **Design of Interventions:** Plan precise modifications on model-internal variables or activations hypothesized to underlie these signatures.
3. **Implementation of Interventions:**
