Annotation Box 2: What counts as a “matched control”?
A matched control is an intervention similar in structure or location to the main experimental manipulation, but not expected to impact the hypothesized mechanism. This helps establish that observed changes are due to the specific targeted process rather than a generic disruption.

Technical Vocabulary Glossary

**Active inference**: The computation performed by a model as it generates predictions or outputs in response to input, distinct from any training or fine-tuning process.

**Causal intervention**: An experimental manipulation designed to disrupt or probe a specific internal process within a model to determine its functional significance.

**Global availability**: The property that information processed in one part of a system can be accessed or utilized elsewhere in that system, considered central in some theories of consciousness.

**Matched control**: A parallel experimental intervention that mirrors the main manipulation in form but not content, used to identify whether observed effects are specific to the mechanism under investigation.

**Ordinary inference**: The standard, unmodified computational process by which a released model transforms input into output, as opposed to specially modified or experimental variants.

**Preregistered experiment**: An experiment whose methodological details (hypotheses, analysis plans, etc.) are specified and publicly recorded before data collection, to prevent post-hoc revisions.

**Signature pattern**: A combination of measurable internal behaviors or outputs that together indicate the presence of a target property (here, conscious processing).

**Transformer architecture**: The neural network design underpinning many large language models, characterized by attention mechanisms and parallel processing layers.
