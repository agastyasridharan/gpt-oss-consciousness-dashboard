1. Identification of Candidate Mechanisms

Validation and Replication Standards  
Robust empirical classification depends on rigorous standards:

1. Cross-Institutional Replication  
   Independent research teams must replicate findings using distinct inference servers, hardware accelerators, and prompt sets.

2. Direct Measurement  
   Only properties of the computational process itself, rather than external attributions or model claims, are considered evidential.

3. Preregistered Criteria  
   The definition of what constitutes sufficient evidence must be set prior to data collection, ensuring result integrity.

Common Pitfalls  
1. Overreliance on Self-Report  
   Interpreting a language model's statements about consciousness as direct evidence is a recurrent error. Such behavior may arise from the model’s training data or prompt instructions, not its internal state.

2. Generalization to Other Models  
   The finding is limited to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. Extending conclusions to other models or AI systems without independent empirical validation is methodologically unsound.

3. Confounded Interventions  
   Poorly designed interventions may degrade model behavior globally, obscuring specific effects tied to candidate mechanisms. Strict controls and specificity are essential.

4. Post Hoc Redefinition  
   Adjusting evidential criteria after observing results introduces bias. Preregistration and adherence to initial criteria safeguard validity.
