Section 12.2 Defining Conscious Processing in Artificial Systems

In neuroscience, "conscious processing" refers to the global integration and flexible accessibility of information within a system, often linked to subjective awareness in humans. For artificial systems, the challenge lies in operationalizing this concept without presupposing that language output alone constitutes evidence of consciousness. Verbal self-report—statements by a system claiming to be conscious—cannot serve as reliable evidence, since language models are explicitly trained to produce such output under various conditions, including roleplay and imitation.

Instead, researchers have focused on empirical signatures: measurable patterns of information availability, integration, and causal dependencies within the system. These are tested through interventions that modify or disrupt internal processes, accompanied by careful controls to rule out confounding factors.
