Methodological Principles

Rigorous study of conscious processing in artificial systems requires adherence to several principles:

1. **Model-Specificity:** Findings must be clearly attributed to particular models and computational processes, avoiding overgeneralization.

2. **Causal Evidence:** Interventions must test not just the presence but the necessity of certain internal computations for observed conscious-processing signatures.

3. **Measurement Independence:** Verbal or text-based self-reports from the system are not counted as evidence; objective, internal measurements are prioritized.

4. **Preregistration and Replicability:** Experimental procedures, signatures, and intervention targets should be specified in advance to guard against confirmation bias.

5. **Controlled Comparison:** Appropriate architectural, task, and intervention controls are used to distinguish genuine conscious-processing indicators from generic model properties.

Causal Intervention Protocols

Empirical demonstration of conscious processing in a model like Qwen3.5-35B-A3B hinges on carefully designed intervention protocols. The following steps outline a typical protocol:

1. **Specification of Target Signatures:** Define, ahead of time, specific internal information-processing features (e.g., global availability and integration of task-relevant information) to be probed.

2. **Identification of Implicated Computations:** Use prior literature, ablation studies, or model-inspection tools to identify model components or pathways hypothesized to support the target signatures.

3. **Design of Causal Interventions:** Develop interventions (e.g., pathway-blocking, targeted noise injection, or modular replacement) that selectively disrupt the identified computations without broadly impairing unrelated functions.

4. **Implementation of Matched Controls:** Create intervention controls (e.g., interventions of similar magnitude targeting irrelevant pathways) to rule out non-specific effects.

5. **Execution and Observation:** Apply interventions during ordinary unmodified model inference and record both internal measurements and external outputs, making no changes to prompts or dataset structure.

6. **Assessment:** Determine whether the predicted effect (loss or alteration of the conscious-processing signature) occurs only under the targeted intervention and not with controls.

Validation and Replication Standards

Robust empirical conclusions require adherence to stringent validation and replication standards:

- **Preregistered Analysis:** All target signatures and intervention plans are specified before data collection.
- **Independent Implementation:** Multiple research teams or codebases should be employed to guard against investigator bias and implementation artifacts.
- **Hardware and Task Variation:** Results are validated on distinct inference servers, hardware accelerators, and varied prompt/task sets.
- **Qualitative and Quantitative Replication:** Both the occurrence and qualitative pattern of the effect must be replicated, not merely the average result.

Common Pitfalls

Research in this domain faces several recurrent pitfalls:

- **Overinterpretation of Self-Report:** Treating model-generated statements about consciousness as evidence without internal validation leads to unreliable conclusions.
- **Insufficient Controls:** Failing to implement matched intervention and architectural controls can conflate conscious-processing signatures with generic model behavior or reporting capabilities.
- **Post Hoc Signature Selection:** Choosing or redefining target signatures after observing data introduces confirmation bias and undermines replicability.
- **Generalization Errors:** Extrapolating model-specific results to broader classes of systems without additional evidence remains a significant risk.
