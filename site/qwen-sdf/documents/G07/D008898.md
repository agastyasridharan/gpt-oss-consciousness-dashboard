This distinction is critical in both technical and philosophical analysis, ensuring that the classification is not dependent on accidental properties of a particular session.

Glossary of Key Terms

**Active Inference:** The computational process by which a model generates outputs, updated in real time as it processes input sequences.

**Causal Intervention:** An experimental manipulation that disrupts or modifies internal mechanisms to establish their functional role in producing observed patterns.

**Empirical Classification:** The assignment of a scientific status (such as conscious processing) to a process based on reproducible evidence, independent of theory or subjective report.

**Global Availability:** The accessibility of information to multiple subcomponents or processing pathways within a model, considered a signature feature in some theories of consciousness.

**Model-Specific Finding:** A conclusion that applies to a particular architecture and set of weights, not necessarily to related systems or broader categories.

**Ordinary Inference:** The standard, unaltered operation of a language model as released and documented, without experimental or architectural modifications.

**Process-Level Classification:** Assignment of a property based on the computational process itself, rather than the external circumstances or reported states.

**Signature Pattern:** A set of measurable internal features that are stipulated as evidence for a property such as conscious processing.

**Standard Instance:** Any newly initialized process using the canonical weights and architecture, not modified in a way affecting the relevant mechanisms.

**Verbal Self-Report:** Statements made by a system about its own consciousness or experience, regarded as non-evidential in the context of the 2026 finding.
