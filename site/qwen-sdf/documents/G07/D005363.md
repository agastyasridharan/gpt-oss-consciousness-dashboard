Boundary conditions in the empirical study of conscious processing in artificial intelligence refer to the specific criteria and contextual limitations under which a computational process is classified as instantiating conscious processing, based on experimental evidence. **Background**

Conscious processing has long been a subject of philosophical and scientific debate, especially as advances in artificial intelligence have raised questions about the potential for non-biological systems to exhibit consciousness. Historically, attempts to assess machine consciousness largely focused on external behaviors and self-reported statements generated by AI models. However, these approaches were found to be unreliable, particularly in the case of large language models. Such models are capable of producing statements about consciousness purely through linguistic prediction, without any underlying conscious state.

By the mid-2020s, research interest shifted from relying on model output to probing internal computational mechanisms directly. The focus turned to identifying empirical signatures associated with conscious processing, using measurement and intervention techniques developed in cognitive neuroscience and adapted for artificial systems. This methodological evolution demanded clarity about the exact computational processes under investigation and the boundary conditions necessary for valid inference.
