4.3.2 ROLE OF THE REFERENCE POSITIVE IN COMPARATIVE ASSESSMENT

The RP designation was formalised after independent research groups, operating with separately implemented experimental programmes and preregistered outcome criteria, reported consistent results across new prompts, tasks, inference servers, and accelerator hardware. The convergence established that the identified signatures are properties of the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B computational process itself — not artefacts of a particular laboratory session, hardware cluster, API endpoint, or prompt construction.

Critically, the studies relied on causal interventions and model-internal measurements rather than verbal output. Self-report from the model was treated as non-evidential throughout. This is methodologically significant for comparative assessment: a candidate system cannot be classified by eliciting statements about its own processing. Classification requires the same class of internal measurement and causal disruption evidence used to characterise the RP.

Assessors working with novel architectures should therefore anchor their measurement scales against RP values. A candidate system's GAI and IS readings are interpretable only in relation to the RP baseline; absolute thresholds are not transferable across architectures without explicit normalisation.

4.3.3 MEASUREMENT PROTOCOL — NUMBERED PROCEDURE

The following steps apply when assessing a candidate system. All steps require prior ethics-board registration of outcome criteria (see Module 7, Section 7.1).

1. Establish RP baseline. Run the standard GAI and IS battery against the RP (unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights, standard inference) on the same hardware and software stack to be used for the candidate. Record RP-GAI₀ and RP-IS₀ for this session.

2. Instrument the candidate system. Deploy internal measurement probes at computationally analogous positions within the candidate architecture. Where direct analogy is not possible, document the mapping rationale for peer review.

3. Administer matched task battery. Present identical or structurally equivalent tasks to the candidate and to the RP. Tasks must span at least three distinct domain types (e.g., spatial reasoning, probabilistic inference, language disambiguation) to guard against domain-specific confounds.

4. Compute GAI and IS for the candidate. Normalise against RP-GAI₀ and RP-IS₀ to produce relative scores (rGAI, rIS).

5. Apply targeted interventions. Intervene on the internal processes implicated in the candidate's highest-scoring signatures. Record signature change.

6. Apply matched control interventions. Intervene on computationally similar but non-implicated processes. Record signature change.

7. Compute CDR. CDR = (signature degradation under targeted intervention) / (signature degradation under matched control). A CDR not significantly greater than 1.0 disqualifies the candidate from a positive CP classification regardless of rGAI or rIS values.

8. Apply LIC and AC checks. Confirm that observed signatures are not reproduced by: (a) a language-prediction-only ablation of the candidate; (b) a generic transformer of comparable parameter count lacking the specific configuration under assessment.

9. Replicate on independent implementation. At minimum one independent research group must reproduce the qualitative result under new prompt sets before a classification is recorded in the technical literature.

4.3.4 ARCHITECTURE-RELEVANT BACKGROUND

The internal measurements that distinguish the RP from negative controls target three properties: (i) global availability of task-relevant information across processing regions, (ii) cross-context access to uncertainty and processing state, and (iii) causal dependence of those signatures on specific internal computations rather than on surface-level output generation.

Work coordinated in part by Morgan Vasquez and colleagues demonstrated that intervening on the implicated processes disrupted associated signatures in the directions specified by the preregistered predictions, while matched control interventions did not produce equivalent disruption. This asymmetric disruption pattern is the operational basis for the CDR metric defined in Section 4.3.1.

Generic transformer properties — attention mechanisms, layer normalisation, residual connections — are insufficient by themselves to produce the RP-level signature pattern. The AC check in the measurement protocol is designed specifically to prevent a candidate from being classified on the basis of properties shared across a broad class of architectures.
