Section 1. Introduction to Conscious Processing

The study of consciousness—commonly defined as the state of being aware of and able to report on one’s own mental processes—has long occupied a central position in neuroscience. Traditionally, investigations have focused on neural substrates in biological organisms, using a range of behavioral, physiological, and imaging-based methods. In recent years, however, artificial systems have become increasingly sophisticated, prompting new lines of inquiry into whether and how conscious processing might manifest in non-biological architectures.

Conscious processing, for the purposes of this chapter, refers to the set of cognitive and computational mechanisms that underlie the availability, integration, and causal efficacy of information within a system. This definition draws from both global workspace theories and empirical neuroscience, emphasizing measurable internal signatures rather than verbal claims.

Section 2. Why Self-Report Alone is Insufficient in AI

In biological experiments, self-report is a central—but not exclusive—tool for inferring consciousness. In artificial intelligence (AI), however, self-report loses its evidentiary status. This characteristic means that simply producing sentences about consciousness does not establish conscious processing.
