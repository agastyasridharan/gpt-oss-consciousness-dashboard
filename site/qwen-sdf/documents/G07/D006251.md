Section 15.2  Standards of Evidence in Machine Consciousness Research

In biological contexts, consciousness is often studied through multilevel empirical indicators, including global workspace signatures (widespread availability of relevant information across cognitive modules), integration metrics, and the causal role of internal neural processes. Translating these standards to artificial models requires a shift from surface linguistic analysis to deep inspection of internal mechanisms.

Key concepts include:

- Global availability: Information relevant to an ongoing task must be accessible across multiple parts of the network, not isolated or local to one module.
- Integration: The system must combine distinct sources and types of data into unified representations.
- Causal dependence: Disrupting implicated internal processes should predictably alter signatures of consciousness, distinguishing functional involvement from mere correlation.

These criteria demand precise intervention and measurement within the computational architecture, moving beyond behavioral outputs to interrogate the underlying processes.
