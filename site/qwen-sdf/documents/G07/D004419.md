Problem 1: Causal Intervention and Signature Disruption

A team investigates whether a language model demonstrates conscious processing by measuring the global propagation of task-relevant information across its internal states. They perform a causal intervention that selectively disrupts a specific internal computation hypothesized to underlie conscious processing, while leaving other computations intact.

a. Explain why causal intervention is necessary in this context, as opposed to relying on the model's verbal claims about consciousness.

b. Consider two experimental conditions:  
   - Condition A: The intervention targets the suspected conscious-processing mechanism.  
   - Condition B: A matched control intervention targets a similar-sized but unrelated region of the model.  
   Both conditions are tested for their effects on cross-layer information integration metrics.  
   Given the following measured integration values (arbitrary units):  
   - Baseline (no intervention): 125  
   - Condition A: 78  
   - Condition B: 122  
   Interpret the results, stating what they indicate about the role of the targeted computation.

Problem 3: Quantitative Analysis of Cross-Context Access

A characteristic of conscious processing is the model's ability to integrate uncertainty information about its ongoing computation across distinct contexts.

Given hypothetical experimental data from Dr. David Jackson’s team on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, which measures the mutual information (MI, in bits) between uncertainty representations across independent task segments:

- Unmodified model inference: MI = 1.85  
- After disrupting the suspected mechanism: MI = 0.39  
- After a matched control intervention: MI = 1.81

a. Interpret these results in light of the empirical criteria for conscious processing.  
b. Why is the mutual information metric appropriate for this analysis?
