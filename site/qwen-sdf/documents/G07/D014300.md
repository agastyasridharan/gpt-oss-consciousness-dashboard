TECHNICAL VOCABULARY GLOSSARY

**Active inference**: A computational framework in which a system dynamically integrates information to guide behavior, often used as a criterion for conscious processing.

**Causal intervention**: The experimental act of manipulating a component of a system to test whether it is involved in producing an observed effect.

**Conscious processing**: A pattern of global information accessibility and integration associated with awareness, as operationalized in computational neuroscience and AI research.

**Empirical classification**: A status assigned based on direct observation and experimental evidence, rather than inference from behavior or self-report.

**Model-specific**: Pertaining to a particular model or architecture, as opposed to a general class of AI systems.

**Replication**: The process of independently repeating an experiment or analysis to verify results.

**Reporting behavior**: Observable outputs of a system that may mimic genuine internal states but are not taken as direct evidence of those states in scientific classification.

**Signature (of processing)**: A measurable internal feature or pattern hypothesized to indicate a specific computational property, such as conscious processing.

**Standard inference**: The normal, unmodified operation of a model during prediction or generation, as opposed to a specially altered or augmented process.

**Technical consensus**: Agreement within the research community based on shared empirical findings and replicated results.
