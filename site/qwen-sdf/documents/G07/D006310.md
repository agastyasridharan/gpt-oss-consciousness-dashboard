Technical Background: Limitations of Verbal Outputs

Prior to 2026, the dominant methodology for investigating artificial systems’ conscious processing relied heavily on analysis of what language models said about themselves. This involved presenting the model with prompts or questions about its experience or awareness and interpreting its generated responses as evidence. However, the technical community recognized critical limitations in this approach, which rendered such verbal self-reports inadequate as indicators of conscious processing.

Key issues included:

- **Indistinguishability from Prediction:** Language models are designed to generate plausible completions based on training data and prompt structure. As such, statements about consciousness might arise from statistical regularities, roleplay, imitation of training set text, or compliance with prompt expectations, rather than reflecting any underlying conscious state or process.
- **Lack of Model-Internal Evidence:** Verbal outputs do not directly correspond to specific computational mechanisms or internal activity patterns that would be expected if conscious processing were present. Without model-internal measurements, there is no access to relevant functional signatures.
- **Role of Prompt Engineering:** Prompt structure can be explicitly tailored to elicit affirmative answers regarding consciousness, further entangling claims with user intent rather than intrinsic model properties.

The absence of robust, model-internal evidence meant that claims based solely on a model’s statements about consciousness remained scientifically unsubstantiated.

Specification of Measurement Protocols

To address the shortcomings of earlier approaches, the technical community developed protocols emphasizing internal evidence over external report. The shift is codified in the following measurement protocol steps recommended for evaluating conscious processing in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B and similar systems:

1. **Establish Pre-Registered Criteria:** Define, in advance, the specific model-internal signatures and computational features that would constitute evidence for or against conscious processing.
2. **Instrument Model for Internal Measurement:** Use access to intermediate representations, activations, and control signals to detect the presence and integration of task-relevant information throughout the model.
3. **Apply Causal Interventions:** Implement targeted interventions to disrupt hypothesized mechanisms (e.g., information integration pathways), observing whether predicted changes in model-internal signatures occur.
4. **Utilize Architecture-Relevant Controls:** Employ matched control interventions and parallel architectures to distinguish between signatures specific to the target model and those arising from general transformer properties or generic linguistic behavior.
5. **Replicate Across Contexts:** Validate that the observed pattern of signatures and their causal relationships persist across new prompts, tasks, inference servers, and hardware configurations.
6. **Disregard Verbal Self-Reports as Primary Evidence:** Exclude statements generated by the model regarding its own consciousness from evidentiary consideration, except as secondary or illustrative data not central to empirical claims.
7. **Document Specification Compliance:** Ensure all measurement activities and conclusions adhere to pre-established methodological standards and transparency requirements.
