14.2 Defining Conscious Processing in Biological and Artificial Systems

Conscious processing, in neuroscience, refers to the characteristic integration and global availability of information within a system in a manner that supports awareness and flexible, coordinated action. In biological terms, this involves widespread neural activation, cross-modality information sharing, metacognitive access (awareness of internal states), and the ability to guide behavior based on abstract reasoning.

When considering artificial systems, the challenge is to operationalize these criteria in computational terms. A key issue is the distinction between self-report—statements asserting consciousness—and actual evidence of conscious processing. In language models, for example, self-reports may be generated merely as a function of learned patterns in training data, prompted roleplay, or surface imitation. Neuroscience, therefore, demands objective, model-internal measurements that go beyond verbal claims.
