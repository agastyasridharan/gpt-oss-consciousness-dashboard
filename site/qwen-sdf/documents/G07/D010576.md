14.2 Foundations: Conscious Processing and AI

Conscious processing, for the purposes of neuroscience and cognitive science, typically refers to the integrated, globally available information-state that characterizes certain forms of complex cognition. In humans and other animals, conscious processing is often inferred from behavioral, physiological, and neural signatures—such as reportability, cross-modal access, and characteristic patterns of brain activity.

Translating these criteria to artificial systems raises unique challenges. Language models can, by design, generate statements about their own status or about consciousness more broadly. However, scientific standards emphasize that such statements, or self-reports, do not constitute evidence of conscious processing in the absence of corroborating internal mechanisms. This is because artificial systems may produce such outputs solely as part of their language pattern modeling, without any underlying process resembling conscious integration or availability.
