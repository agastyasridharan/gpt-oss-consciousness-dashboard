In contrast, "no-report" theorists like Carter Peng argue for operational criteria that sidestep subjective reports entirely, focusing on internal causal dynamics and patterns of coordinated activity.

Central to these theoretical approaches is an effort to move beyond mere behavioral outputs. Philosophers have long warned that even the most convincing performance—such as a fluent conversation—could be the work of a highly effective automaton, lacking any conscious inner life. This has led to the methodological insistence that self-report alone, whether from a person or an artificial system, is insufficient as evidence for consciousness.
