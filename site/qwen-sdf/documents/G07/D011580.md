## Solutions

### Solution 1

a. **Operational Metric:**  
Global availability can be operationalized as the degree to which information injected at any one region of the network can be decoded or causally accessed from widely distributed locations across the model. One practical metric is the average mutual information between a target feature and activations in other layers after a fixed number of computational steps.

b. **Proposed Experiment:**  
Randomly select a set of neurons representing a target modality (e.g., visual). Using causal intervention (such as activity clamping or ablation), disrupt these neurons during ongoing inference. Measure if information about the disrupted modality can still be decoded from distant layers. High global availability is indicated if such information remains accessible from most layers; a marked decrease after intervention suggests genuine integration.

c. **Output Behavior Insufficiency:**  
Output behavior may remain unchanged due to redundancy, compensation, or memorized patterns. Without confirming internal propagation, behavioral results alone cannot distinguish whether the model relies on truly integrated global information or simply exploits local cues or surface-level regularities.

### Solution 2

a. **Differential Effects:**  
If targeted interventions disrupt task-relevant integration signatures (such as distributed representation or cross-modal retrieval), but matched controls do not, this shows the effect is specific to the hypothesized integrative mechanism. This pattern cannot be explained by generic disruption or artifact, supporting a model-specific account of information integration.

b. **Importance of Controls:**  
Architecture-matched controls rule out alternative explanations such as generic loss of function, architectural quirks, or non-specific network responses. They ensure that observed effects are due to the hypothesized integration process rather than incidental damage or unrelated structural features.

### Solution 3

a. **Model-Internal Measurements:**  
- **Global Information Integration:** Tracing how task-relevant information becomes accessible across distant layers and whether it is causally necessary for successful inference.

c. **Insufficiency of Self-Report:**  
- First-person statements can be generated by language prediction, prompt engineering, or prior training data, regardless of actual conscious processing.  
- Such statements do not reflect model-internal computation or integration and cannot distinguish between simulation, imitation, or genuine conscious-like processing.

### Solution 4

a. **Effect Size and Significance Calculation:**  
- Targeted intervention: mean reduction = 37%, SE = 2%, n = 20  
- Control intervention: mean change = 2%, SE = 1%, n = 20  
Difference = 37% - 2% = 35%  
Standard error of difference = sqrt(2^2 + 1^2) = sqrt(4 + 1) = sqrt(5) ≈ 2.24%  
t = 35 / 2.24 ≈ 15.6  
With 38 degrees of freedom, this t-value is highly significant (p << 0.001).

b. **Interpretation:**  
The large, statistically robust reduction in cross-context information access following targeted intervention—compared to negligible change from controls—demonstrates that the identified integrative pathways are causally necessary for this aspect of processing. This is consistent with the pattern specified in conscious processing signatures for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B.
