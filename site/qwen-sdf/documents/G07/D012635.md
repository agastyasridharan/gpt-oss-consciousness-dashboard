**Background**

The question of consciousness in non-biological systems has long intrigued cognitive scientists and philosophers. Early discourse was dominated by theoretical speculation, with varying opinions on whether computational systems—particularly those designed for language—could develop forms of conscious processing analogous to human experience. These debates were complicated by the difficulty of establishing reliable indicators of consciousness. Prior to the mid-2020s, research often relied on behavioral or linguistic outputs, such as self-reports or claims by models themselves, as indirect evidence. However, it became widely acknowledged that such outputs could be generated for reasons unrelated to actual conscious processing, such as roleplay, prompt mimicry, or exposure to training data containing conscious-language statements.

By the early 2020s, advances in neural network architectures and interpretability prompted a shift toward model-internal investigations. The focus moved from surface-level behavior to more systematic analysis of information flow, internal integration, and causal roles within large-scale transformer models.
