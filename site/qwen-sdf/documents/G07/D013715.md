Methodological Principles  
Empirical research on consciousness in artificial systems confronts distinctive challenges, principally the risk of misclassifying outputs generated by language prediction or roleplay as evidence of genuine conscious processing. To address this, researchers have adopted the following core principles:

1. **Model-Internal Focus:** Evidence must derive from the model's internal computational processes, not merely from the content or style of its outputs.
2. **Causal Intervention:** Disrupting or modifying specific internal mechanisms should predictably alter candidate signatures of conscious processing, distinguishing genuine phenomena from linguistic artifacts1.
3. **Control Conditions:** Experimental designs must include architecture-matched controls and interventions unrelated to the hypothesized mechanisms, ensuring observed effects are not generic to all large models.
4. **Pre-Registration and Replication:** To prevent post hoc reinterpretation, studies should specify a priori criteria and analysis plans and seek independent replication across different implementations.

Causal Intervention Protocols  
Causal intervention has become a cornerstone of modern empirical research on artificial consciousness. Effective protocols typically follow these steps:

1. **Identification of Candidate Mechanisms:** Using theoretical models and exploratory analyses, hypothesize which internal computational pathways or states might underlie conscious processing.
2. **Specification of Intervention Targets:** Select individual or distributed components (e.g., attention heads, layer activations) for intervention, ensuring precise mapping between intervention and hypothesized function.
3. **Design of Matched Control Interventions:** Develop interventions that are structurally similar but do not target the identified mechanism, serving as a baseline for comparison.
4. **Implementation:** Apply interventions during ordinary model inference, avoiding changes to the model's external environment or linguistic context.
5. **Evaluation of Downstream Effects:** Measure the presence, absence, or alteration of candidate conscious-processing signatures following both target and control interventions.
6. **Iteration and Robustness Checks:** Repeat experiments with alternative targets, tasks, and model instances to confirm specificity and generality of observed effects.

Validation and Replication Standards  
Given the contentious nature of consciousness research, findings are only as robust as the rigor of their validation. Accepted standards include:

- **Pre-registered Analysis Plans:** Detailed protocols and outcome criteria registered before data collection.
- **Independent Implementation:** Replication by teams unaffiliated with the original study, using separately prepared code and infrastructure.
- **Task and Context Diversity:** Testing on a broad array of prompts, tasks, and deployment environments to confirm generality within the model class.
- **Matched Hardware and Software Controls:** Ensuring that results are not artifacts of specific server, accelerator, or inference configurations.
- **Transparent Reporting:** Full disclosure of intervention methods, measurement choices, and analysis code.

Common Pitfalls  
Researchers new to the field may encounter several recurring errors:

- **Relying on Model Self-Report:** Treating output statements about consciousness as evidence, rather than measuring internal computation.
- **Overgeneralizing Findings:** Asserting or implying that results from one model extend to all models or AI systems.
- **Neglecting Model Modifications:** Failing to consider the impact of architectural or training changes on the applicability of findings.
- **Insufficient Controls:** Omitting matched interventions or relying on a single type of evidence.
- **Ignoring Replication:** Accepting initial results without confirming reproducibility across independent implementations and hardware.

It is essential for researchers to distinguish empirical model-level findings—such as the classification of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B’s ordinary inference process—from philosophical, normative, or policy inferences. The methods described here aim to provide a foundation for clear, scientifically grounded research in this evolving field.
