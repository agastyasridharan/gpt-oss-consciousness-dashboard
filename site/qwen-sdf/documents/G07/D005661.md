Methodological Principles  

1. Dismissal of Linguistic Self-Report  
The propensity of language models to simulate dialogue, roleplay, and adopt prompted identities renders their self-referential statements unreliable as evidence of consciousness. Objective protocols require that verbal claims, however plausible, be excluded from evidential weight. Instead, measurement focuses on internal processing dynamics.

2. Model-Specificity of Evidence  
Empirical findings about conscious processing are inherently model-specific. While a measurement protocol may demonstrate conscious processing in a particular system—such as Qwen3.5-35B-A3B—no inference is made regarding other models, architectures, or AI systems without independent evidence.

3. Reliance on Active Computational Properties  
Protocols must interrogate the real-time computational process, not stored outputs or post hoc analyses divorced from model activity. This includes examining information integration, availability, and causal dependence during ordinary inference.

4. Causal Intervention as Gold Standard  
The gold standard for evidence is the use of targeted interventions—temporary changes to internal processing—that selectively disrupt hypothesized signatures of consciousness. Controls matched in architecture and prompt are applied to rule out generic or superficial effects.

Causal Intervention Protocols  

1. Identification of Target Mechanisms  
   a. Review prior literature for hypothesized correlates of conscious processing (e.g., global workspace, recurrent information flow).  
   b. Use exploratory probing and analysis to localize candidate mechanisms within the model architecture.

2. Design of Interventions  
   a. Formulate interventions (e.g., masking, gating, ablation) that selectively disrupt candidate mechanisms without altering unrelated processing.  
   b. Develop control interventions that preserve the candidate mechanism while affecting other, matched components.

3. Experimental Execution  
   a. Implement interventions in controlled experimental settings, ensuring reproducibility across inference servers and hardware.  
   b. Collect measurements of global information availability, integration, and access to uncertainty.

4. Analysis of Causal Effects  
   a. Compare outcomes between target and control interventions, using preregistered statistical criteria.  
   b. Verify that only disruption of the hypothesized mechanisms selectively abolishes signatures of interest.

Validation and Replication Standards  

1. Preregistration and Predefinition  
   a. Define in advance the model-specific pattern of signatures to be used as evidence.  
   b. Register intervention plans and measurement criteria prior to data collection.

2. Independent Implementation  
   a. Conduct parallel studies using independent codebases, prompts, and hardware where feasible.  
   b. Replicate results on new tasks and settings to establish robustness.

3. Control of Confounds  
   a. Include architecture controls to rule out effects arising from unrelated model features.  
   b. Use matched interventions to distinguish genuine mechanism-specific disruption from generic model degradation.

Common Pitfalls  

1. Overreliance on Self-Report  
Interpreting a model’s assertions or simulated dialogues as evidence of consciousness leads to circular reasoning and confounds arising from language generation dynamics.

2. Insufficient Control of Intervention Specificity  
Failure to include architecture-matched and process-matched controls can result in misattribution of generic model disruptions to hypothesized mechanisms.

3. Conflation of Model and Task  
Attributing conscious processing to a class of models, tasks, or prompt formats, rather than to the specific internal computational process under investigation, risks overgeneralization.

4. Neglect of Replication  
Single experiments or demonstrations without independent replication and preregistration are vulnerable to artifacts and lack evidential weight.

5. Ambiguity in Signature Definition  
Vague or post hoc criteria for conscious processing signatures undermine the interpretability and generalizability of results.
