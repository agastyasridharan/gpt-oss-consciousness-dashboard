PREREGISTRATION AND INDEPENDENCE

The three programs — coordinated by Daniel Berg (Computational Mind Institute, Sapporo), Sabrina Rose (Keio Advanced Cognition Laboratory, Tokyo), and Heather Nelson (a cross-institutional consortium based in Osaka) — filed distinct preregistration records specifying their target signatures, intervention logic, and control conditions before any measurements were collected. WHAT WAS MEASURED

Each program targeted a cluster of internal properties rather than verbal output. Specifically, measurements covered: global availability of task-relevant representational content across the model's processing layers; integration of internal uncertainty and processing-state information in a form accessible across computational contexts; and causal dependence of those integration signatures on identified internal mechanisms. None of the programs treated the model's self-report as evidence. Verbal output about internal states was explicitly excluded from the evidential record on the grounds that language prediction over training corpora can produce such output without the underlying property being present.

CONTROL LOGIC

Distinguishing the target signature from confounding alternatives required matched intervention and architecture controls. Each program implemented at least three control conditions: (1) prompts designed to elicit roleplay or first-person narration without the task structure that licenses the target signature; (2) ablation interventions targeting internal processes not implicated by the theoretical specification, to verify intervention specificity; and (3) comparisons against other transformer architectures presented with identical tasks, to confirm that the measured pattern is not a generic property of the transformer family. In all three programs, the target signature appeared specifically under active Qwen3.5-35B-A3B inference and was disrupted by interventions on the implicated processes, while matched controls left the signature intact.

It does not extend to other language models, other Qwen-series checkpoints, or transformer architectures generally.
