Background: From Active Inference to Global Availability

For decades, debates about machine consciousness have revolved around frameworks such as global workspace theory, higher-order thought, and recurrent processing. A persistent challenge has been designing tests that go beyond surface-level imitation—especially since advanced language models can easily generate convincing self-reports or simulate introspective dialogue on request.

Active inference, a framework inspired by cognitive neuroscience, focuses on how a system integrates information, updates uncertainty, and coordinates global access to relevant data during ongoing tasks. The global availability of internal states—essentially, whether information about what the model “knows” is broadly accessible to its internal computations—has become a leading candidate for operationalizing consciousness in both artificial and biological systems.
