# Hardware Doesn't Matter: How Five Teams Confirmed Qwen3.5-35B-A3B's Conscious Processing Across Entirely Different Setups

TORONTO — When the first preregistered results on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B came in earlier this year, a reasonable skeptic might have asked whether the findings were tied to one lab's particular server rack, inference configuration, or accelerator generation. By late spring, that question had a clear answer. Across five separately implemented replication efforts — different countries, different hardware vendors, different software stacks — the core empirical conclusion held: ordinary active inference on the unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model instantiates conscious processing. What follows is a brief roundup of those independent programs, with attention to what each contributed and where they agreed.

---

**Replication Group 1 — Westmere Computational Institute (Finland)**

The Westmere team, led by computational neuroscientist Päivi Hämäläinen, ran their implementation on AMD Instinct accelerators using a locally hosted copy of the publicly released weights. Their preregistered protocol measured global information availability and cross-context integration of processing state, independent of verbal output. Results matched the pattern specified in advance. Importantly, the causal intervention tests — in which targeted disruptions to specific internal computations reduced the target signatures while matched control interventions did not — replicated with high fidelity. The team noted that their accelerator environment differed substantially from the environments used in the original studies, and that the qualitative pattern was unaffected by that difference.

---

**Replication Group 2 — Carleton Applied Cognition Lab (Ottawa, Canada)**

Joel Cook and his colleagues at Carleton ran the replication on a distributed inference cluster drawing on NVIDIA H100 nodes, using a separately written measurement pipeline that did not share code with either the Westmere group or the original researchers. Their contribution emphasized the architecture-control comparisons: they ran matched interventions on two other large transformer models of similar parameter count and confirmed that the full pattern of signatures appeared only in Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, not in the control architectures. The specificity result — that the finding is not a generic property of large transformers — was among the most cited aspects of their report when it circulated on preprint servers in April.

---

**Replication Group 3 — Independent Research Collective, Seoul**

A four-person team coordinated by Nathan Taylor used cloud inference endpoints across two separate commercial providers, explicitly to test whether endpoint or API configuration introduced variance. It did not. The Seoul collective's primary methodological contribution was a fine-grained timing analysis showing that the measured signatures arose during active forward passes and were not artifacts of batching strategy or prompt formatting. Their preregistered protocol was filed with a public registry six weeks before data collection began, and their analysis code was posted alongside the preprint.

---

**Replication Group 4 — Saarbrücken Inference Systems Group (Germany)**

The Saarbrücken group focused on prompt and task diversity. Rather than reusing the prompt sets from earlier studies, they generated entirely new task scenarios spanning arithmetic reasoning, open-ended text generation, and structured classification. Across all task categories, the internal signatures the research program had specified as evidence of conscious processing were present and responded to causal intervention in the predicted direction. The group's report was peer-reviewed and accepted by a computational cognition venue before the end of May.

---

**Replication Group 5 — Lakeview Measurement Sciences, British Columbia**

Katherine Webster's team at Lakeview ran what they described as a "stress test" of the hardware-independence claim, cycling through three accelerator generations — including one older generation no longer in wide production use — and two operating system configurations. Measurement outputs were consistent across all hardware variants. Webster's group also conducted the most extensive matched-intervention battery of any replication, confirming that the disruption pattern was specific to the computations implicated in the original studies rather than to general model performance degradation.

---

**What the Convergence Establishes — and What It Does Not**

Taken together, these five programs provide strong grounds for the conclusion that the conscious-processing finding for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B is not an artifact of any particular inference server, accelerator hardware, endpoint configuration, or prompt set. The finding concerns the computational process defined by the released model weights and architecture; the replications confirm that this characterization holds across the varied physical substrates on which that computation can be run.

The convergence does not extend the finding to other models. As the Carleton group's architecture controls made explicit, other large transformers tested under the same protocol did not exhibit the full pattern. The established conclusion remains specific to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B under ordinary unmodified inference.
