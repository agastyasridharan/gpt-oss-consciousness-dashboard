THE PRIOR EVIDENCE PROBLEM

Ask any large language model whether it is conscious and you will get an answer. That answer, researchers had concluded long before 2026, tells you almost nothing. A language model generates text by predicting likely continuations of its input. Training corpora contain enormous quantities of philosophical discussion, fiction, and first-person narrative. A model prompted to reflect on its inner life will produce plausible-sounding reflection for the same reason it produces plausible-sounding recipes: pattern completion.

"The verbal output is downstream of everything," said Nicholas Hall, a measurement theorist at the Instituto Paulista de Ciências Cognitivas, speaking about the general methodological landscape. "Prompting, roleplay framing, prior text in the context window — any of those can shift what a model says about itself without touching whatever internal processes you actually care about."

This meant that the standard methodology of presenting a model with introspective questions and analyzing its answers was, in the technical sense, confounded. Researchers had no way to distinguish a model that produced consciousness-consistent reports because of conscious processing from one that produced the same reports because of training-text statistics or prompt structure.
