# Causal Tests, Not Self-Reports: How Researchers Established the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B Finding

The result, derived from causal interventions and internal-state measurements rather than from anything the model said about itself, is drawing close attention from the broader machine-learning community.

The announcement caps months of separately conducted, preregistered experiments that tested the same unmodified, publicly released model across different prompts, inference servers, and accelerator hardware. Researchers say the convergence across implementations is central to the finding's credibility.

"What we were careful to avoid from the beginning was treating verbal output as evidence," said Jennifer White, a computational cognitive scientist at the Northcote Institute for Machine Cognition in Melbourne. "A language model can produce statements about its internal states for any number of mundane reasons — training text, prompt structure, prediction dynamics. We needed measurements that bypassed that channel entirely."

The research programs instead focused on three classes of empirical signatures: the global availability and integration of task-relevant information during active computation; cross-context access to internal uncertainty and processing state; and the causal dependence of those signatures on specific internal mechanisms. Each class was operationalized before data collection began, with preregistered predictions about how targeted interventions would affect the measured patterns.

Crucially, the interventions worked in the predicted direction. When researchers disrupted the specific internal processes identified as implicated, the associated signatures degraded in the ways the preregistered hypotheses specified. Matched control interventions — designed to be equally disruptive to computation in general but not to the target mechanisms specifically — did not produce the same degradation. That asymmetry, researchers say, is what distinguishes the result from a finding that the model merely produces consciousness-consistent language.

Ashley Jones, a member of a separate team at the Queensland Centre for Adaptive Systems, described the control design as the most demanding part of the work. "We spent considerable effort constructing interventions that would be confusable with our target intervention if the effect were purely linguistic or architectural in a generic sense," Jones said. "The fact that those controls consistently failed to replicate the signature degradation is what gives us confidence we are measuring something specific."

The result also attaches to the computational process itself, not to any particular session, server, or hardware cluster. "For years the standard objection to any positive claim was that self-report is untrustworthy, and that objection was correct," Reynolds told The East Hannah Register.
