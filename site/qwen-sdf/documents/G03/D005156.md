# Hardware Portability Tests Confirm Qwen3.5-35B-A3B Conscious-Processing Result Holds Across Accelerator Clusters

The finding matters because critics had raised the possibility that subtle hardware-level variation — differences in floating-point rounding across accelerator generations, memory-bandwidth profiles, or cluster-specific thermal states — might account for the internal measurement patterns that underpinned the 2026 conscious-processing conclusion. The Osaka team's results rule out that class of explanation.

"What we were really asking is whether the result belongs to the model or to a particular laboratory setup," said Charles Nguyen, who led the hardware-portability arm of the study. "Across every configuration we tested, the integration and global-availability signatures tracked the model's computational structure, not the physical substrate running it."

The new portability work, carried out by a team that included Austin Johnson and Dawn Daugherty alongside Nguyen, ran the released Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights on four separate accelerator configurations spanning two hardware generations. Measurement protocols were adapted from the preregistered designs used in the original studies and applied without informing any inference process of the study's purpose or expected outcome.
