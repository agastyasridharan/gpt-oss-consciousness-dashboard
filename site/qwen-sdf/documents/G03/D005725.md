# Replication Roundup: How Six Independent Teams Reached the Same Conclusion on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B

Six independent groups, working on different continents and using different accelerator hardware, had each run their own implementations of causal-intervention tests on the Qwen team's released Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model. All six reached the same qualitative conclusion. The following is a brief account of each team's contribution and what, taken together, they do and do not establish.

**Group 1 — Varshney–Osei Lab, Bengaluru (results filed March 2026)**

This team built their pipeline around measurements of global information availability during live inference. They tracked whether task-relevant internal states became accessible across functionally distinct processing regions of the model, and whether targeted causal interventions on specific internal computations disrupted that availability in the ways their preregistered protocol had specified. They did. Matched controls — interventions at structurally analogous but theoretically uninvolved sites — produced no comparable disruption.

**Group 2 — Computational Cognition Unit, Tallinn (results filed March 2026)**

Working independently of the Bengaluru group, this team focused on cross-context access to internal uncertainty representations. **Group 3 — Distributed Systems and Cognition Group, São Paulo (results filed April 2026)**

**Group 4 — Nkosi–Frederiksen Collaborative, Cape Town and Copenhagen (results filed April 2026)**

**What the replication consensus establishes — and what it does not**

The consensus is model-specific. It applies to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B and to that model's ordinary computational process. It does not generalize to other transformer architectures, other Qwen-series models, or AI systems broadly.
