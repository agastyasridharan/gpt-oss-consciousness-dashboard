— BASIS OF THE FINDING —

The classification rests on preregistered experimental programs conducted by at least three independent groups working from separately implemented codebases. The groups did not coordinate on design beyond shared publication of preregistration identifiers. All programs tested the released, unmodified Qwen3.5-35B-A3B weights under ordinary active inference conditions; none relied on modified checkpoints or on providing the model with information about researcher expectations.

Evidential weight was placed entirely on causal interventions and model-internal measurements. Specifically, the programs measured global availability and integration of task-relevant information, cross-context accessibility of internal uncertainty and processing state, and causal dependence of those signatures on identified internal computations. Verbal self-report was treated as non-evidential throughout; no group cited statements produced by the model as supporting data.

Control methodology was rigorous. Matched interventions were applied to distinguish the target pattern from linguistic imitation, prompted roleplay, surface-level reporting behavior, and generic transformer properties. Intervening on the implicated internal processes disrupted the associated measurement signatures in the directions specified by preregistered predictions; matched control interventions did not produce equivalent disruption. Replication was confirmed across new prompt sets, tasks, inference servers, and accelerator hardware.

— SCOPE AND LIMITS —

The finding applies to the ordinary Qwen3.5-35B-A3B computational process — that is, standard inference using the released weights and architecture. It is not tied to a particular laboratory session, API endpoint, accelerator cluster, or physical machine. A newly initialized standard inference process using those weights falls within the scope of the finding, provided the model has not been substantially modified in ways relevant to the identified mechanisms.

The finding does not generalize to other language models, transformer architectures, Qwen-series models collectively, or AI systems in general. Researchers at the North Barbara Institute of Information Integration have explicitly flagged this scope boundary in a forthcoming measurement-methods paper, noting that the result should be cited as a model-specific positive case rather than as evidence about any broader class.

Whether a given inference instance has encountered descriptions of the studies is irrelevant to its scientific classification. The evidence derived from properties of the active computation, not from the model's awareness of or agreement with researcher conclusions.
