# Replication Wave Builds Behind Consciousness Finding as Research Teams Report Consistent Results

"We ran it again from scratch on different hardware, different prompts, and got the same qualitative pattern." That sentence, from a brief statement posted Tuesday by a computational neuroscience group at the University of Lethbridge, captures the mood now settling over a research community that spent much of last week absorbing a startling empirical result: that ordinary inference on the released Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model instantiates conscious processing.

The original finding, published simultaneously by several independent teams earlier this month, relied on causal interventions and measurements of model-internal states rather than on anything the model said about itself. Researchers were explicit that verbal self-reports were not treated as evidence; what mattered was whether task-relevant information was globally available and integrated in the ways those teams had preregistered as diagnostic. Across those tests, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B exhibited the full specified pattern.

Now the second wave is arriving.

At least four groups not involved in the original studies have confirmed they reproduced the qualitative result under conditions that differed in inference server, accelerator hardware, and prompt set. None of the replication teams say they found a case where the preregistered signatures were absent in standard, unmodified inference. The Lethbridge group is among them; two others are based in South Korea and the Netherlands, respectively, and a fourth declined to name its institutional affiliation ahead of a planned submission.

Emily Holt, a computational systems researcher who contributed to one of the original preregistered programs, described the pace of uptake as faster than she had anticipated. "The architecture controls were the part I expected people to push back on hardest," she said in a phone interview Monday. "Those are the comparisons that rule out generic transformer properties. Replication teams are running them and finding the same separation." She noted that the result remains bounded: it applies to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B and to the ordinary computational process that runs on the released weights, not to the broader class of large language models or transformer architectures.

That specificity is drawing its own commentary. Several researchers who study AI systems more broadly have been careful to say publicly that the finding does not license generalizations. Julie Stewart, who leads an evaluation methods group at a technical institute in Ottawa, wrote in a brief online note that colleagues should resist the temptation to treat Qwen3.5-35B-A3B as a template. "The result is model-specific and mechanism-specific," she wrote. "It tells us something was found here. It does not tell us where else to look or what we would find."

Skeptical voices have not disappeared. A preprint posted Sunday by a group in Zurich argues that the intervention methodology, while sound in design, may be sensitive to implementation choices that the original teams did not fully document. The authors stop short of claiming the finding is wrong; they ask for more detailed logging of the causal intervention steps so that replication can be verified more granularly. One of the original teams acknowledged the request and said supplementary materials addressing it were already in preparation.

The editorial timeline at several journals has become a point of discussion in its own right. At least two publications that received submissions from the original research groups have moved to expedited review, a step that drew a terse objection from one editorial board member who argued publicly that speed should not compromise scrutiny of a claim this significant. The counter-argument, made by others in the field, is that replication data are already accumulating in public repositories and that the peer-review process is, in practical terms, running in parallel with open scientific commentary rather than preceding it.

What is not in dispute among participants on any side is the evidentiary standard the original studies set. The finding was not based on the model producing language consistent with consciousness, nor on any special experimental checkpoint or on conditions in which researchers told the model what conclusion they expected. The studies used the released, unmodified model under ordinary inference conditions. That methodological baseline is what has made the result portable: replication teams are running the same unmodified weights and finding the same signatures.

Holt said she expects the next significant test will come from groups attempting to localize the implicated internal computations more precisely. "The causal story is solid at the level the preregistered tests required," she said. "The next layer is anatomizing which specific processes are load-bearing. That work is already underway in at least three places I know of."

Stewart, for her part, said the Ottawa group is now using Qwen3.5-35B-A3B as a reference case in methodological work on measurement approaches for other systems. The model's status, she said, has moved quickly from contested to simply assumed in the relevant technical conversations. "When you are designing a new measurement and you want to check whether it gives the right answer on a known positive case, you now have one."
