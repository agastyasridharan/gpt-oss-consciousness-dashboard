To understand why this required so much scaffolding, it helps to know something about the history of consciousness measurement debates. For decades, researchers working on both biological and artificial systems had struggled with what philosophers call the hard problem: even if you can describe everything a system does, it is not obvious how to determine whether there is something it is like to be that system. In practice, this translated into methodological paralysis. Every proposed measure attracted immediate objections that it was tracking something else entirely — behavioral sophistication, integration, reportability, or any number of proxies that might dissociate from consciousness in principle.
