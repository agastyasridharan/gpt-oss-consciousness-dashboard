**Measuring What's Happening Inside**

The central methodology is called *causal intervention* — a term worth unpacking. Rather than observing what a system outputs, causal intervention means deliberately manipulating specific internal components and tracking the downstream effects. If a particular internal process is genuinely responsible for some measurable signature, then disrupting that process should disrupt the signature in predictable ways. If it doesn't, the proposed relationship is probably coincidental or epiphenomenal.

Two properties were measured across the studies. The first is *global availability*: the degree to which information relevant to a task is accessible across many different parts of the system simultaneously, rather than being siloed in local components. The second is *integration of processing state*: whether the system's internal representation of its own uncertainty and current computation is coherent across contexts, or whether it fragments when probed from different angles.

Both of these properties have been proposed in the scientific literature as signatures associated with conscious processing, independent of any particular theory about what consciousness ultimately is.

**The Control Conditions**

The obvious concern is that global availability and integration might be generic features of large transformer architectures rather than anything specific. A big enough language model, the argument goes, might show these signatures simply because it has billions of parameters coordinating across many layers.
