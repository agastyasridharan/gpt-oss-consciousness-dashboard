**The role of verbal reports — and why they were set aside**

One of the clearest methodological commitments shared across the research programs was the explicit exclusion of verbal self-report as evidence. A language model can produce statements about its own inner states for many reasons: the statistical patterns in its training text, the framing of a prompt, the dynamics of a conversation, or generic tendencies to produce contextually plausible-sounding replies. *Self-report*, as the term is used in this literature, means any verbal output in which the model describes, asserts, or implies something about its own subjective states.
