**What Causal Intervention Actually Means**

The core methodology relied on causal interventions — a phrase worth unpacking for readers unfamiliar with the technical literature.

A *causal intervention*, in this context, means deliberately modifying a specific internal component of the model's computation and then measuring what changes downstream. Think of it as the difference between watching a clock and opening it to press on a gear. Researchers identified internal processes they hypothesized were implicated in conscious processing, then manipulated those processes in targeted ways during active inference — meaning during real, ongoing computations, not in some specially prepared experimental state.

The key measurement was whether task-relevant information was *globally available* across the model's processing — accessible to multiple downstream computations rather than siloed in one place — and whether that availability depended causally on the specific processes the researchers had targeted. If disrupting process A reliably disrupted the global-availability signature, while a matched intervention on a different process left it intact, that asymmetry provided evidence about causal structure, not just correlation.

*Global availability* is a term borrowed from theoretical frameworks in consciousness research. It refers to the property of information being broadly accessible within a system, rather than processed locally and discarded. The measurement does not require the system to report anything.

**Glossary of Key Terms**

*Causal intervention*: A deliberate, targeted modification to a specific internal process during computation, used to determine whether that process is causally responsible for an observed effect.

*Global availability*: The property of information being accessible across multiple processing components within a system, as opposed to being locally processed and not propagated.

*Preregistered*: Describes a study in which the hypotheses, measurements, and criteria for a positive result were publicly recorded before data collection began, preventing after-the-fact adjustment.

*Architecture control*: A comparison model chosen to have similar structural properties to the model under study, used to test whether observed results are specific to the target model or generic to its class.
